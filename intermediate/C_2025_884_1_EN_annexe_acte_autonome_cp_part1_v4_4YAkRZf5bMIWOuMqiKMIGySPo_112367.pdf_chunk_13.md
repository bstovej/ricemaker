This document segment provides a detailed legal interpretation of specific prohibitions within the **EU AI Act**, specifically focusing on **Article 5(1)(f)** (Emotion Recognition) and **Article 5(1)(g)** (Biometric Categorization).

Below are the key insights and facts organized by regulatory theme.

### 1. Prohibition of Emotion Recognition (Article 5(1)(f))
The regulation prohibits the use of AI to infer emotions in specific environments due to the "imbalance of power" between the subject and the authority.

**A. The "Workplace" Scope**
*   **Broad Definition:** A "workplace" includes any physical or virtual space where tasks are performed (offices, factories, warehouses, cars, mobile sites, etc.).
*   **Universal Application:** It applies regardless of status (employee, contractor, trainee, or volunteer) and includes the **recruitment/hiring stage**.
*   **Prohibited Uses:**
    *   Tracking employee emotions (e.g., anger, happiness) via webcams or cameras.
    *   AI monitoring the emotional tone of hybrid/video calls to manage social dynamics.
    *   Using emotion recognition during a probationary period.
*   **Permitted Uses:**
    *   **Personal Training:** If the results are not shared with HR and cannot impact assessments/promotions.
    *   **Customer Tracking:** Tracking the emotions of *customers* (e.g., to help staff handle angry clients) is **not** prohibited.
    *   **Security/Safety:** Detecting suspicious behavior (e.g., potential robbery) is permitted, provided no employees are being tracked and safeguards are in place.

**B. The "Education" Scope**
*   **Broad Definition:** Covers all levels (vocational to higher ed), all ages, and all modes (online, in-person, or blended). It also applies to the **admissions process**.
*   **Prohibited Uses:**
    *   Using AI to infer student interest, attention, or engagement.
    *   Using emotion recognition during admissions/admissibility tests.
*   **Permitted Uses:**
    *   **Eye-Tracking:** Allowed if used strictly for gaze/fixation (to detect unauthorized materials) but **not** if used to detect emotional arousal or anxiety.
    *   **Role-play/Training:** Allowed for actors or teachers if the results do not impact certification or evaluation.

### 2. The "Medical and Safety" Exception (Narrow Interpretation)
There is a strict, narrow exception for emotion recognition used for medical or safety reasons.
*   **Medical Use:** Must be limited to **CE-marked medical devices** (e.g., assisting individuals with autism or blindness).
*   **Prohibited "Well-being" Monitoring:** General monitoring of stress, burnout, or depression is **prohibited** and does not qualify as a medical exception.
*   **Safety Use:** Limited strictly to the **protection of life and health**. It does **not** cover the protection of property (e.g., preventing theft).
*   **Constraint:** Use must be the "least intrusive" possible, limited in time/scale, and must not be used for any secondary purpose (like performance reviews).

### 3. Out-of-Scope Applications
Certain AI systems do not fall under the Article 5(1)(f) prohibition:
*   **Non-Biometric Systems:** AI that infers emotions without using biometric data.
*   **Physical States:** AI used to detect **pain or fatigue** (e.g., for monitoring pilots/drivers) is explicitly excluded from the definition of emotion recognition.
*   **Commercial Contexts:** Using emotion recognition for advertising or customer service (e.g., "intelligent billboards") is generally allowed, provided it does not cross into "harmful manipulation."
*   **Crowd Control:** Monitoring a group's general noise or mood level is permitted if it does not target the emotions of specific individuals.

### 4. Biometric Categorization (Article 5(1)(g))
This section prohibits using biometric data to categorize individuals based on "sensitive" characteristics.

*   **The Prohibition:** It is illegal to use AI to categorize people based on **race, political opinions, trade union membership, religious/philosophical beliefs, sex life, or sexual orientation.**
*   **The "Ancillary" Exception:** Categorization is permitted if it is an **ancillary feature** to a primary service and **strictly necessary** for technical reasons.
    *   *Example of Permitted:* AR filters on social media that change facial features for aesthetic purposes.
    *   *Example of Prohibited:* An AI system that categorizes social media users based on their assumed political orientation.
*   **Law Enforcement Exception:** The prohibition does **not** cover the labeling or filtering of lawfully acquired biometric datasets for law enforcement purposes.
