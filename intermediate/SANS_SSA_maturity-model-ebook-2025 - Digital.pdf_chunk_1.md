This document outlines the **SANS Security Awareness Maturity Model®**, a framework designed to help organizations move from basic compliance-based training to a deeply embedded security culture.

The following are the key insights and facts categorized by theme:

### 1. The Core Problem: The "Human Element"
*   **The Human Risk Statistic:** Employees are involved in **68% of all global breaches** (per the 2024 Verizon Data Breach Investigations Report).
*   **The Shift in Strategy:** As technical defenses become more effective, threat actors are increasingly targeting people. Cybersecurity is no longer a purely technical challenge; it is a human one.
*   **The Ultimate Goal:** A successful program moves beyond mere "behavior modification" (changing what people do) to "culture change" (changing what people believe and value).

### 2. Key Elements of a Successful Program
To build a high-maturity program, the document highlights several critical requirements:
*   **Dedicated Resources:** For organizations with over 1,000 employees, the most mature programs often utilize **3–5 Full-Time Employees (FTEs)** dedicated solely to managing human risk.
*   **Integration:** Security awareness should not be a standalone "compliance" silo; it must be part of the core security team and report to security leadership.
*   **Effective Communication:** Training must be communicated in **"people terms"** (explaining why it matters and how it benefits the individual) rather than technical jargon.
*   **Continuous Engagement:** Moving away from annual "check-the-box" training toward continuous, ongoing reinforcement and engagement.

### 3. The 5 Stages of the Maturity Model
The model provides a roadmap for organizational evolution:

*   **Stage 1: Non-existent**
    *   No program, no metrics, and no leadership discussion regarding security awareness. Employees are at extremely high risk.
*   **Stage 2: Compliance-Focused**
    *   **Goal:** Meeting legal or audit requirements.
    *   **Characteristics:** Training is sporadic/annual; "check-the-box" mentality; minimal coordination with other departments.
    *   **Metrics:** Focused on *process* (e.g., % of employees who completed training).
*   **Stage 3: Promoting Awareness & Behavior Change**
    *   **Goal:** Reducing specific high-impact risks (like phishing).
    *   **Characteristics:** Ongoing engagement; creation of an advisory board; use of phishing simulations.
    *   **Metrics:** Focused on *effectiveness* (e.g., phishing click rates, number of reported incidents).
*   **Stage 4: Long-term Sustainment & Culture Change**
    *   **Goal:** Embedding security into the organizational fabric.
    *   **Characteristics:** Security is part of daily operations; employees act as "security ambassadors"; can take **3–10 years** to achieve.
    *   **Metrics:** Focused on *attitudes* (e.g., employee surveys regarding security beliefs).
*   **Stage 5: Strategic Metrics Framework**
    *   **Goal:** Aligning security awareness with business mission and ROI.
    *   **Characteristics:** Automated data collection; integration with frameworks like NIST or CIS; awareness metrics are used by leadership to drive business strategy.
    *   **Metrics:** Focused on *risk reduction* (e.g., time to detect/recover from incidents; reduction in total incidents).

### 4. Summary of Progression
| Feature | Low Maturity (Stages 1-2) | High Maturity (Stages 4-5) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Driver** | Compliance and Audits | Business Strategy and Risk Reduction |
| **Training Style** | Annual / Ad hoc | Continuous / Integrated |
| **Employee Role** | Passive recipients (targets) | Active participants (defenders/ambassadors) |
| **Metric Focus** | "Did they watch the video?" | "Did we reduce the number of breaches?" |
