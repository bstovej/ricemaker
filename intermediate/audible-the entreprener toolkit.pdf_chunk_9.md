Based on the text provided, here is a summary of the key financial concepts and advice for entrepreneurs:

### **1. Understanding Core Financial Statements**
*   **Income Statement (Profit and Loss):** Essential for tracking revenue and expenses.
*   **Balance Sheet:** Used to understand the company's financial position.
*   **Cash Flow Statement:** Crucial for tracking the actual movement of cash in and out of the business.

### **2. Managing Expenses and Costs**
*   **Cost of Goods Sold (COGS):** Entrepreneurs must account for all direct costs, including materials and labor, to determine true profitability.
*   **Operating Expenses (OpEx):** It is vital to include all overhead, such as rent, utilities, and administrative costs, in your projections.
*   **The Danger of Underestimating:** A common mistake is failing to account for all expenses. If operating costs rise and aren't accounted for, profit margins will shrink.

### **3. Key Accounting Concepts**
*   **Depreciation:** This is the process of allocating the cost of a tangible asset (like machinery or vehicles) over its useful life. For example, if you buy a truck, you don't deduct the full cost at once; instead, you spread the cost over the years the truck is expected to be in service.
*   **Margin Compression:** If your expenses (like labor or materials) increase but you cannot raise your prices, your profit margins will be "squeezed" or compressed.

### **4. Strategic Financial Advice**
*   **Use Data-Driven Estimates:** When creating financial projections, do not guess. Use market research, quotes from suppliers, and historical data to create realistic estimates for costs like rent, utilities, and wages.
*   **Plan for "Hidden" Costs:** Always include expenses that might not be immediately obvious, such as interest on loans, taxes, insurance, and maintenance.
*   **Monitor Margins:** Regularly review your profit margins to ensure that rising costs are not eroding your business's sustainability.
