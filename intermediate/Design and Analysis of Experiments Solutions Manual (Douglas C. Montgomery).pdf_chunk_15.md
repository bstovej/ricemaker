### **2. Summary of Key Experimental Results**

#### **A. Copper Brightness Study (Study 1)**
*   **Objective:** To determine the effect of furnace temperature and copper concentration on brightness.
*   **Key Findings:**
    *   **Primary Drivers:** Both furnace temperature and copper concentration significantly impact brightness.
    *   **Optimal Settings:** High temperature and moderate copper concentration were found to produce the highest brightness.
*   **Conclusion:** A controlled interplay between temperature and concentration is vital for maintaining consistent brightness levels.

#### **B. Glass Strength Analysis (Study 2)**
*   **Objective:** To assess how cooling rate and glass thickness affect the mechanical strength of glass.
*   **Key Findings:**
    *   **Cooling Rate:** A slower cooling rate significantly increases the strength of the glass by reducing internal stresses.
    *   **Thickness:** Increased thickness leads to higher strength but also increases the risk of structural flaws.
*   **Conclusion:** To maximize strength, a slow cooling process is recommended, though thickness must be balanced against weight requirements.

#### **C. Chemical Yield Optimization (Study 3)**
*   **Objective:** To optimize the yield of a chemical reaction by adjusting catalyst concentration and reaction time.
*   **Key Findings:**
    *   **Catalyst:** Increasing catalyst concentration improves yield up to a specific threshold, after which diminishing returns occur.
    *   **Time:** Longer reaction times generally increase yield, but excessive time leads to byproduct formation.
*   **Conclusion:** An optimal "sweet spot" exists where both time and catalyst concentration are balanced to maximize yield without excessive byproduct formation.

#### **D. Semiconductor Defect Rate (Study 4)**
*   **Objective:** To identify the causes of high defect rates in semiconductor manufacturing.
*   **Key Findings:**
    *   **Process Temperature:** Fluctuations in temperature are a primary cause of defect spikes.
    *   **Cleanroom Airflow:** Inconsistent airflow patterns correlate with higher particle contamination levels.
*   **Conclusion:** Tightening the controls on temperature stability and airflow regulation is essential for reducing defect rates.
