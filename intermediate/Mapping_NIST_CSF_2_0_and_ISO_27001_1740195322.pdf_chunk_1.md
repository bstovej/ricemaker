This document provides a detailed structural mapping between the **NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0** and the **ISO/IEC 27001:2022** standard. It serves as a cross-walk for organizations looking to align their cybersecurity strategy (NIST) with a formal Information Security Management System (ISO).

Below are the key insights and facts extracted from the document.

### 1. Core Purpose and Scope
*   **Objective:** The document maps specific **NIST CSF 2.0 Subcategories** (the "what" and "how" of cybersecurity outcomes) to **ISO 27001:2022 Clauses** (management system requirements) and **Annex A Controls** (specific security controls).
*   **Framework Hierarchy:** 
    *   **NIST CSF 2.0** is structured by **Functions** (highest level), **Categories**, and **Subcategories**.
    *   **ISO 27001:2022** is structured by **Clauses** (organizational requirements) and **Annex A** (operational controls).
*   **Version/Date:** The mapping is version 1.0, dated March 1, 2024.

### 2. Key Functional Insights

#### **A. The "Govern" (GV) Function (New/Expanded in 2.0)**
The document places heavy emphasis on Governance, which is a central pillar of NIST CSF 2.0. 
*   **Organizational Context (GV.OC):** Maps heavily to ISO clauses regarding understanding the organization (4.1–4.4) and legal/regulatory requirements (A.5.31–A.5.34).
*   **Risk Management Strategy (GV.RM):** Connects NIST risk objectives to ISO’s risk assessment and treatment processes (Clauses 6.1, 8.2, 8.3).
*   **Supply Chain Risk Management (GV.SC):** This is one of the most detailed sections. It maps NIST’s supply chain requirements to ISO controls for supplier relationships (A.5.19–A.5.23), outsourced development (A.8.30), and cloud services (A.5.23).
*   **Roles and Policy (GV.RR & GV.PO):** Aligns NIST's need for clear roles and policies with ISO’s leadership (5.1) and policy (5.2) requirements.

#### **B. The "Identify" (ID) Function**
Focuses on visibility and understanding the organization's risk posture.
*   **Asset Management (ID.AM):** Maps the inventory of hardware, software, and data to ISO’s asset management controls (A.5.9) and classification/labeling (A.5.12, A.5.13).
*   **Risk Assessment (ID.RA):** Links NIST’s identification of vulnerabilities and threats to ISO’s management of technical vulnerabilities (A.8.8) and threat intelligence (A.5.7).
*   **Improvement (ID.IM):** Connects the identification of improvements to ISO’s internal audit (9.2) and continual improvement (10.1) clauses.

#### **C. The "Protect" (PR) Function**
Focuses on the implementation of safeguards.
*   **Identity and Access Management (PR.AA):** Aligns NIST’s requirements for authenticated identities and least privilege to ISO’s access control, identity management, and segregation of duties (A.5.3, A.5.15–A.5.18, A.8.2–A.8.5).
*   **Awareness and Training (PR.AT):** Maps training requirements directly to ISO’s competence and awareness clauses (7.2, 7.3).
*   **Data Protection:** Includes protections for hardware, software, and information integrity (e.g., protecting data in transit/rest).

### 3. Key Technical Themes Identified
*   **Lifecycle Management:** The document emphasizes a full lifecycle approach, from identifying assets (Identify) to managing them (Protect) and improving processes (Improve).
*   **Integration of Compliance and Security:** The mapping shows that NIST CSF (a framework for managing risk) can be directly operationalized using ISO/IEC 27001-style controls (the Annex A controls in the ISO standard).
*   **Emphasis on Supply Chain Risk:** A significant portion of the mapping focuses on third-party/supplier management (GV.SC), reflecting the modern importance of supply chain security.
*   **Standardization of Evidence:** By mapping these two, an organization can use the implementation of ISO controls as evidence of meeting NIST CSF outcomes, reducing "audit fatigue."
