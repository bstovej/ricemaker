Based on the provided document segment from the **Singapore Cyber Landscape 2022** report, here are the key insights and facts categorized by theme:

### 1. Evolution of Ransomware Tactics
The report highlights a significant shift in how ransomware groups operate, moving toward more sophisticated, "professionalized" models.
*   **Rise of RaaS and Cloud Targeting:** The Ransomware-as-a-Service (RaaS) model has lowered the barrier to entry, allowing anyone with resources to launch attacks. There has been a massive surge in **cloud exploitation**, which grew by **95% in 2022** and nearly tripled since 2021.
*   **Malware-Free Attacks ("Data Kidnapping"):** There is a growing trend toward "extortion-only" tactics. Instead of encrypting files, attackers focus on **exfiltrating sensitive data** to coerce victims via the threat of public leaks. CrowdStrike reported that these malware-free attacks accounted for **71%** of their detections in 2022.
*   **Exploiting Interconnectivity:** Attackers are increasingly targeting **Managed Service Providers (MSPs)** and cloud environments because a single breach in these interconnected systems can allow ransomware to spread rapidly to numerous downstream customers.

### 2. The "Paying Up Does Not Pay" Phenomenon
The document provides stark statistics suggesting that paying ransoms is ineffective and often leads to further victimization.
*   **Low Recovery Rates:** According to Proofpoint, while **64% of organizations paid ransoms**, only **52%** of those victims actually regained access to their data. Furthermore, **54%** still encountered system errors or corrupted data after decryption.
*   **The Vicious Cycle:** Paying a ransom marks an organization as a target. **80% of organizations that paid were hit by a second ransomware attack**, and in **48%** of those cases, the attack was perpetrated by the **same attackers**, often demanding a higher ransom.

### 3. Increasing Aggression and Coercion
Ransomware groups are adopting "commercial and professional-like" behavior, using extreme psychological pressure.
*   **Aggressive Negotiation:** Groups are diversifying their portfolios to include more aggressive tactics to force payment.
*   **Extreme Coercion:** The report notes tactics akin to physical kidnapping, where perpetrators threaten the families of victims with bodily harm to force a payment. 
*   **High-Profile Examples:** The group **Lapsus$** targeted major tech companies like Nvidia and Samsung, while the **Medusa** group released sensitive videos of cancer patients and student records to pressure healthcare and educational institutions.

### 4. Geopolitical Cyber Warfare (Russia-Ukraine)
The conflict in Ukraine has fundamentally altered the landscape of state-linked and non-state cyber activity.
*   **Rise of Hacktivism:** The conflict saw an unprecedented involvement of non-state actors, including the formation of the **"IT Army of Ukraine,"** a volunteer-based group dedicated to defending Ukrainian infrastructure and conducting offensive operations.
*   **Infrastructure Disruption:** The use of "wiper" malware and attacks on satellite communications (e.g., the Viasat attack) demonstrated how cyberattacks can have significant real-world consequences on critical infrastructure.

### 5. Global and Local Countermeasures
Efforts to combat these threats are becoming increasingly coordinated across borders.
*   **International Collaboration (CRI):** In October 2022, 36 countries and the EU met for the **Counter-Ransomware Initiative (CRI) Summit**. Key pillars include Policy (led by Singapore/UK), Diplomacy (Germany/Nigeria), and a Task Force (Australia).
*   **Success Story:** The report cites the FBI’s January 2023 operation to disrupt the **Hive ransomware group** as a notable success in the international crackdown.
*   **Singapore’s Stance:** Singapore has established the **Cyber Ransomware Threat Force (CRTF)** to bring together various government agencies to strengthen national counter-ransomware efforts.
