Based on the provided document segment from the **Singapore Cyber Landscape 2022** report, here are the key insights and facts organized by theme:

### 1. The Dual Role of Artificial Intelligence (AI)
The document describes AI as a "double-edged sword" that is fundamentally changing the cybersecurity landscape.

*   **Offensive Use (The Threat):** 
    *   Advanced LLMs (like ChatGPT-3.5 and 4) can be used to automate and scale malicious activities, including crafting phishing emails, writing ransomware code (specifically for macOS), generating malware detection rules (YARA and Sigma), and identifying software vulnerabilities (buffer overflows).
    *   **Deepfakes:** AI-enabled art generators are making photorealistic deepfakes more accessible, which threat actors can use for highly targeted spear-phishing and account takeovers.
*   **Defensive Use (The Benefit):**
    *   AI and Machine Learning (ML) can establish baselines for system behavior, provide real-time insights, and enhance network monitoring to detect anomalous patterns.
    *   **Efficiency Gains:** According to an IBM study, organizations using AI and automation had a **74-day shorter breach lifecycle** and saved an average of **US$3 million**.
*   **Market Growth:** The cybersecurity market is projected to grow from **US$22.4 billion in 2023 to US$60.6 billion by 2028**, driven largely by the need to combat NLP and ML-based threats.

### 2. Evolution of Ransomware: "Ransom for Reputation" (R4R)
Ransomware tactics are shifting from simple data encryption to more psychological and reputational forms of extortion.

*   **Extortionware:** Threat actors are increasingly focusing on "data exfiltration and public shaming" (leaking data on "leak sites") rather than just locking files.
*   **R4R Trend:** Organizations are beginning to pay ransoms not to regain access to data, but to protect their brand reputation.
*   **Economic Impact:** Cybersecurity company Acronis predicts that global ransomware damages will exceed **US$30 billion by 2023**.
*   **Regulatory Pressure:** Gartner predicts that **30% of countries will pass legislation against ransomware payments by 2025**. However, the report warns that legislating against payments might drive the practice further underground.

### 3. Economic Adversity and "Permacrisis"
Global economic instability (driven by the Russia-Ukraine conflict, inflation, and energy shortages) is creating new systemic cyber risks.

*   **Increased Phishing Vulnerability:** Economic crises create "manufactured urgency." For example, the report notes threat actors used the UK government's energy bill discount announcement in May 2022 to launch phishing/SMS scams.
*   **Insider Threats:** Economic hardship makes individuals more susceptible to bribery. The report cites the ransomware group **Lapsus$**, which actively sought to buy remote VPN access from insiders at major tech firms like Microsoft and AT&T.
*   **Workforce Distraction:** A 1Password study found that **79% of North American workers** felt distracted during the "permacrisis," increasing the likelihood of human error in security.
*   **Budgetary Risks:** There is a risk that during economic downturns, C-suites may view cybersecurity as an "overhead" or "non-essential expenditure," leading to hiring freezes and weakened security postures.
