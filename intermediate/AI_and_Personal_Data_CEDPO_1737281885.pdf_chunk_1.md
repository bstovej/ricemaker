This document is a guidance manual prepared by the **CEDPO AI Working Group** (dated June 16, 2023) intended for **Data Protection Officers (DPOs)**. It explores the intersection of Artificial Intelligence (AI)/Machine Learning (ML) and the EU’s General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), while also addressing the emergence of the EU AI Act.

Below are the key insights and facts extracted from the text:

### 1. The GDPR is "Technology-Neutral"
*   **Scope:** The GDPR does not explicitly mention "AI" or "machine learning." However, it is designed to be technology-neutral. This means the regulation applies to **any** technology that processes personal data, regardless of whether the technology is known or unknown at the time the law was written.
*   **Purpose of Neutrality:** This prevents companies from circumventing privacy laws by using new, emerging technologies (like blockchain or AI) to bypass existing regulations.

### 2. Key Distinctions in Technology
*   **Automated Processing $\neq$ AI/ML:** Not all automated processing involves AI. For example, a simple CRM tool or an algorithm that denies credit based on a fixed set of pre-set criteria is "automated" but does not necessarily utilize AI or ML.
*   **AI does not always process Personal Data:** AI can be used for purely non-personal purposes (e.g., weather prediction or precision farming). However, the document warns of three specific risks:
    *   **Re-identification:** "Anonymized" datasets can sometimes be linked back to individuals.
    *   **Mixed Datasets:** AI often processes datasets containing both personal and non-personal data.
    *   **Inference:** AI can "deduce" or infer personal information from seemingly non-personal data.
*   **Training vs. Production:** An AI system has two phases. Personal data may be used in the **training phase** (learning from data), the **production phase** (applying the model to new data), or both.

### 3. The Relationship Between GDPR and the AI Act
The document clarifies that these two regulations are complementary, not redundant:
*   **GDPR Focus:** Focuses specifically on the protection of **personal data** and individual privacy rights. It is "horizontal" and applies whenever personal data is involved.
*   **AI Act Focus:** Focuses on **broader societal and ethical risks** (e.g., algorithmic bias, safety, and security) that may occur even when no personal data is being processed. It is a "hands-on," bespoke framework for regulating the technology itself.

### 4. Critical GDPR Articles for DPOs to Monitor
The document identifies specific legal "touchpoints" that DPOs must manage when AI is implemented:
*   **Article 5 (Principles):** AI must adhere to principles like data minimization and accuracy (noting the risk of AI "hallucinations" creating inaccurate data).
*   **Articles 4, 6, & 7 (Lawfulness/Consent):** There must be a valid legal basis (such as "legitimate interest") for using datasets to train AI.
*   **Article 22 (Automated Decision-Making):** Subjects have the right to object to automated decisions and the right to "meaningful information about the logic involved."
*   **Article 25 (Privacy by Design):** Controllers must incorporate "state of the art" protections into the development of AI.
*   **Articles 35 & 36 (DPIA):** The high-risk nature of AI makes **Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs)** highly likely to be necessary.

### 5. Emerging Transparency Obligations
*   **The AI Act (Article 52):** Adds specific requirements for transparency, such as the obligation to disclose when a person is interacting with an AI system (e.g., to prevent deception via deepfakes).
