This document is a book review by cybersecurity expert Dan Lohrmann regarding Carter Schoenberg’s book, *Why Cybersecurity Fails in America*. It provides a deep dive into the systemic failures within the cybersecurity industry and offers professional perspectives on risk management and recent major cyber incidents.

### **Key Insights**

**1. The "Interdependency" Problem**
The core thesis of the book is that cybersecurity failure is not solely a technical issue but a systemic one. Success is hindered by the complex interdependencies between the U.S. Government, Human Resources, Higher Education, Corporate Boards, and Cybersecurity Professionals.

**2. Shift in the CISO Role: From "No" to "Risk Identifier"**
A significant leadership insight is the evolution of the Chief Information Security Officer (CISO). Historically, CISOs were viewed as "party poopers" who simply said "no" to new technologies. Modern effective leadership requires shifting the role to identifying risks, defining exposure, and providing alternative solutions, leaving the final decision-making to the business leaders.

**3. Strategic Risk Communication**
To gain executive buy-in, cybersecurity professionals must move away from purely technical jargon. Effective communication involves:
*   Translating technical facts into a **business narrative**.
*   Focusing on **lost opportunity costs** and alignment with corporate risk tolerance.
*   Using **dollar-level impacts** to make the conversation relevant to the Board of Directors.

**4. Critique of Modern Cybersecurity Trends**
*   **The Skills Shortage & AI:** Carter Schoenberg expresses skepticism regarding the idea that Artificial Intelligence (AI) can effectively bridge the current cybersecurity skills shortage, suggesting that reliance on AI for this purpose may fail.
*   **Risk Modeling:** The book critiques "legacy risk modeling" as flawed and suggests a shift toward "resilience" rather than just "safeguarding."

**5. Perspective on the CrowdStrike/Microsoft Incident**
Schoenberg provides a nuanced view of the 2024 CrowdStrike outage:
*   **Scale vs. Impact:** While the technical disruption was significant, he argues the media's "worst ever" label is debatable when compared to historical events like WannaCry or Code Red.
*   **The "Bellwether" Effect:** The real significance lies in how the event impacts **shareholder value** (noting a 30% drop for CrowdStrike) and how Boards of Directors will use such incidents to evaluate the performance of CEOs.
