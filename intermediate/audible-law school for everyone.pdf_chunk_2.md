### 1. Legal Foundations: "Thinking Like a Lawyer"
The text outlines the fundamental mechanics of how law is interpreted and applied in a common law system (such as the U.S.).

*   **The Doctrine of Precedent:** 
    *   **Definition:** Precedent involves following decisions handed down in the past to ensure consistency and predictability.
    *   **Binding vs. Persuasive Authority:** A decision is **binding** if it comes from the same court or a higher court in the same jurisdiction. Decisions from different jurisdictions are **persuasive** (informative but not mandatory).
    *   **Functions of Precedent:** It prevents judicial whim/bias, provides societal stability (so people know how to behave), and ensures judicial efficiency by preventing the need to re-litigate every issue from scratch.
    *   **Holding vs. Dicta:** The **holding** is the essential part of a decision that resolves the case and is binding. **Dicta** refers to non-essential commentary by the judge, which is informative but not binding.
*   **Textual Interpretation:** 
    *   Lawyers must focus on the exact wording of statutes and precedents. 
    *   **Bright-line rules** are clear, inflexible requirements (e.g., filing deadlines) that do not account for intent or excuses.
    *   Legal arguments often oscillate between the **"letter of the law"** (plain meaning) and the **"intent of the law"** (purpose/context).
*   **Logical Reasoning Methods:**
    *   **Analogy:** Comparing the facts of the current case to a past case to argue for a similar outcome.
    *   **Deductive Logic (Syllogism):** Applying a general rule (major premise) to specific facts (minor premise) to reach a conclusion.
    *   **Inductive Logic:** Drawing broad generalizations from a large number of specific observations.

### 2. Professional Ethics: The Lawyer’s Dual Responsibility
Lawyers operate under a tension between their duty to their client and their duty to the legal system.

*   **Duty to the Client:** Lawyers must act with zeal, protect client confidentiality, and follow the client's instructions regarding objectives.
*   **Duty to the Court/System:** Lawyers are officers of the court and must not facilitate fraud or perjury. They have a duty to the administration of justice that can override client interests.
*   **Confidentiality & Privilege:** 
    *   Lawyers must protect client information but may be ethically required to disclose information to prevent death, bodily harm, or certain crimes.
*   **The Ethics of Confidentiality (Rule 1.6 context):** While lawyers protect secrets, they must disclose information if it is necessary to prevent reasonably certain death or substantial bodily harm.
*   **Client Authority:** The client decides the *objectives* (e.g., whether to settle), but the lawyer decides the *means* (e.s., the technical legal tactics used).

### 3. The Litigation Process: Trial and Discovery
The text outlines the mechanics of how a case moves through the legal system.

*   **The Discovery Phase:** This is the process of exchanging information before trial.
    *   **Interrogatories:** Written questions that must be answered under oath.
    *   **Document Production:** The right to inspect relevant documents and physical evidence.
    *   **Depositions:** Oral testimony taken under oath outside of court.
*   **Pre-Trial Motions:** Lawyers use motions to request specific actions from the court (e.e., motions to dismiss or motions to compel evidence).
*   **Trial:** The formal process of presenting evidence and arguments to a judge or jury to reach a verdict.
