### 1. Hedge Funds and Regulation
*   **Regulatory Distinction:** Unlike mutual and pension funds, the SEC does **not** impose diversification or leverage limits on hedge funds, provided these strategies are disclosed in the fund's operating documents.
*   **The A.W. Jones Model:** As the pioneer of the first hedge fund, Jones utilized a strategy of **long/short positions** to mitigate market volatility. He also employed **leverage** to increase conviction in his trades, a tactic that allowed his partnership to achieve positive returns over 90% of the time across a 30-year period.

### 2. Activist Investing
Activist investors acquire large stakes in companies to pressure management into changes that increase share prices (e.g., strategy shifts, dividends, or buybacks).

**Key Tactics & Defensive Maneuvers:**
*   **Tactics:** Direct negotiation, publishing "white papers" to pressure management, and **proxy contests** (campaigning to install a new board of directors).
*   **Greenmail:** An (now largely outlawed) practice where a company pays an activist a premium to abandon a takeover attempt.
*   **Poison Pill:** A defense where a firm issues new, cheap shares to existing shareholders to make a takeover prohibitively expensive.
*   **White Knight:** A friendly investor (e.g., Warren Buffett) who rescues a company from a hostile takeover.
*   **Shark Repellent:** Structural changes to a corporate charter, such as "staggered boards," to make it harder for raiders to seize control.

**Prominent Activists:**
*   **Carl Icahn:** Known for hostile takeovers and arbitrage. A notable success was his pressure on **Apple** to use its cash reserves for share buybacks and increased dividends.
*   **Dan Loeb (Third Point):** Focuses on **"value compounders"**—companies with talented management, strong free cash flow, and a proven track record of wise capital allocation.
*   **Bill Ackman (Pershing Square):** Focuses on "moat" companies with high barriers to entry, utilizing strategies similar to finding companies with strong cash flows and defensible business models.

### 4. Short Selling and Market Mechanics
*   **Short Selling:** The practice of selling borrowed securities in hopes of buying them back at a lower price. While it can profit from market declines, it carries significant risk if the price rises.
*   **The Role of Short Sellers:** Short sellers provide market liquidity and can act as a check against overvalued companies, though they are often viewed controversially.
