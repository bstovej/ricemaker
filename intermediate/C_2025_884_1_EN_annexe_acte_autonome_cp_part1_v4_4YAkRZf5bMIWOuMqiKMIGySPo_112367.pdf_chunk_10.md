This document segment provides a detailed legal analysis of specific prohibitions within the **EU AI Act**, focusing on the regulation of "social scoring" (Article 5(1)(c)) and "individual risk assessment/crime prediction" (Article 5(1)(d)).

Below are the key insights and facts categorized by regulatory focus.

### 1. Scope of Article 5(1)(c): Permitted AI Uses
The document clarifies that certain types of data processing and "scoring" are **out of scope** (not prohibited) provided they are proportionate, relevant, and do not result in unjustified detrimental treatment.

*   **Medical/Health Data:** Collecting data (e.g., for diagnosing schizophrenia) is permitted if it is necessary for a legitimate purpose and does not lead to unjustified discrimination.
*   **Safety & Security:** Online platforms may profile users for safety reasons, provided the evaluation is not disproportionately detrimental to the user.
*   **Commercial Advertising:** AI-enabled targeted advertising is permitted if it aligns with Union consumer/data protection laws and does not involve exploitative practices like unfair differential pricing.
*   **Retail/Loyalty Programs:** AI-enabled "scoring" by retailers (e.g., offering faster returns to frequent customers) is permitted, as long as it rewards positive behavior without denying standard access to other users.
*   **Law Enforcement:** Evaluating individuals for crime prevention is permitted if the data is relevant to the specific purpose of prosecution and the treatment is justified by national/Union criminal law.

### 2. Article 5(1)(d): Prohibition of Crime Prediction
This section details the strict prohibition against AI systems used to predict the likelihood of a person committing a crime based on **profiling or personality traits.**

*   **The Core Prohibition:** It is prohibited to use AI to assess or predict the risk of an individual committing a crime if that assessment is based **solely** on profiling or the assessment of personality traits and characteristics.
*   **The "Human-in-the-loop" Exception:** The prohibition **does not apply** if the AI system is used to *support* a human assessment that is already based on **objective and verifiable facts** linked to criminal activity.
*   **The Three Cumulative Conditions:** For a practice to be prohibited under this article, three things must all be true:
    1.  The system is being placed on the market, put into service, or used.
    2.  The system assesses or predicts the risk of a person committing a crime.
    3.  The prediction is based *solely* on profiling or personality/characteristic assessments.
*   **Defining "Personality Traits":** This is a broad category and includes non-obvious indicators such as nationality, place of birth, number of children, level of debt, or even the type of car a person drives.
*   **Defining "Profiling":** The document adopts the GDPR definition—automated processing to evaluate personal aspects (performance, economic situation, behavior, etc.). It also notes that "group profiling" (using data from a group to predict the behavior of an individual) can trigger this prohibition.

### 3. Legal Interplay and Overlap
The document emphasizes that the AI Act does not exist in a vacuum; providers must comply with several other Union legal frameworks:

*   **Consumer Protection (UCPD):** Private-sector social scoring may violate consumer laws if it is misleading or distorts the economic behavior of consumers.
*   **Data Protection (GDPR):** All scoring practices must adhere to principles of data minimization, fairness, transparency, and legal grounds for processing.
*   **Non-Discrimination Law:** Any evaluation based on protected grounds (race, religion, sex, etc.) is subject to strict Union non-discrimination laws.
*   **Consumer Credit Directive (CCD):** When assessing creditworthiness, the use of social network data and "special categories" of personal data is explicitly prohibited.
*   **Anti-Money Laundering (AML):** AI used for anti-money laundering and terrorism financing must comply with specific sectoral regulations.

### 4. Illustrative "Prohibited" Scenarios
The text provides "red flag" examples of what the AI Act intends to stop:
*   **Predictive Policing based on traits:** Using AI to predict terrorism based only on age, nationality, and marital status.
*   **Opaque Tax Audits:** Tax authorities using AI to identify potential tax crimes based on "inferred" or "opaque" variables (like double nationality) without reasonable suspicion.
*   **Guilt by Association:** AI systems that label children as "high risk" simply because they are related to or associated with individuals with high-risk profiles.
