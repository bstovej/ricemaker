### **Overview of Narrative Endings**
The text explores the different ways stories can conclude, focusing on the relationship between reader expectation and the execution of the plot. It categorizes endings into three primary types: **Binary**, **Logical**, and **Hybrid**.

### **1. The Binary Ending (The "Binary" Choice/Resolution)**
This type of ending relies on a clear-cut resolution, often involving a choice or a definitive outcome.
*   **Mechanism:** It presents a situation where a specific action or discovery resolves the central tension.
*   **Key Example:** *Jane Eyre*. The author notes that the resolution (the revelation of Rochester's secret) provides a satisfying conclusion because it answers a specific question raised during the narrative.

### **2. The Logical Ending (The "Logical" Resolution)**
This type of ending focuses on internal consistency and character development, where the conclusion feels inevitable based on the groundwork laid by the author.
*   **Mechanism:** It relies on "plausibility" and "internal consistency." The ending is not necessarily a surprise, but it feels "right" because it follows the established rules of the characters and the world.

### **3. The Hybrid Ending (The "Hybrid" Approach)**
The hybrid ending combines elements of both a dramatic "shock" (the binary/unexpected) and a deep "logical" satisfaction.
*   **Mechanism:** It utilizes a sudden, dramatic event (the "shock") but grounds it in the character's history or the story's established themes (the "logical").
*   **Key Example:** *A Good Man is Hard to Find* by Flannery O'Connor. 
    *   **The "Shock":** The sudden, violent arrival of the Misfit and the subsequent death of the characters.
    *   **The "Logic":** The spiritual or thematic transformation of the protagonist (the "grace" found in a moment of violence) feels consistent with the dark, Southern Gothic themes of the story.

### **Core Themes in Narrative Craft**
*   **Believability vs. Surprise:** The text emphasizes that while an ending can be surprising, it must remain believable within the established universe of the story.
*   **Reader Satisfaction:** The ultimate goal of navigating these ending types is to provide a sense of "satisfaction" or "closure," whether through the resolution of a mystery or the completion of a character arc.
