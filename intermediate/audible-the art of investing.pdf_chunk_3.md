### 1. Growth Investing Philosophies
The text contrasts two legendary growth investors who focused on long-term potential but utilized different analytical lenses.

**Philip Fisher (The Qualitative Approach)**
*   **Focus:** Identifying companies with significant future sales potential.
*   **Key Metrics:** 
    *   **P/E Ratio:** Stocks with a P/E higher than the market average are considered growth stocks.
    *   **Price-to-Book (P/B) Ratio:** High P/B suggests growth (valued on future potential); low P/B suggests value.
*   **Qualitative Criteria:** Fisher utilized "rigorous fundamental analysis," which included talking to competitors, suppliers, and employees. He looked for management integrity, robust R&D, and the ability to pivot when products become obsolete.
*   **Portfolio Strategy:** He advocated for a **concentrated portfolio** (roughly 30 stocks) to ensure deep understanding of each business, arguing that over-diversification can increase risk if you don't understand your holdings.

**T. Rowe Price (The Life-Cycle Approach)**
*   **Focus:** Investing based on the maturity stage of an industry.
*   **Industry Life Cycle Stages:** 
    1. Rapid growth (e.g., early Internet).
    2. Stable growth (e.g., smartphones).
    3. Maturity/Slowing growth (e.g., Coca-Cola).
    4. Decline (e.g., railroads post-automobile).
*   **Preferred Strategy:** Price preferred the **stable growth phase** because it is more predictable and less volatile than the startup phase.
*   **Key Criteria:** High R&D, avoidance of cutthroat competition, immunity from regulation, and a Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) of at least 10%.

### 2. Modern Portfolio Theory (Harry Markowitz)
Markowitz revolutionized investing by introducing **risk** as a measurable component equal to **return**.

*   **The Core Insight:** The risk of a portfolio is not merely the sum of the risks of individual stocks, but rather the **interaction** between them.
*   **Correlation:** This is the mathematical measure of how assets move in relation to each as a number between -1 and +1. 
    *   To reduce risk, investors should seek assets with **low correlation** (e.g., a retail stock in Singapore and a healthcare stock in Brazil), as their "zigging" and "zagging" offset each other.
*   **The Efficient Frontier:** A graphical curve representing the set of optimal portfolios that offer the highest expected return for a specific level of risk.

### 3. Capital Asset Pricing Model (William Sharpe)
Sharpe expanded on Markowitz’s work to simplify the calculation of efficient portfolios.

*   **Beta ($\beta$):** The measure of market risk. 
    *   $\beta = 1$: The stock moves in tandem with the market.
    *   $\beta < 1$: The stock is less risky than the market.
    *   $\beta > 1$: The stock is more volatile than the market.
*   **The Concept:** The model posits that investors should be compensated for systematic risk (market risk) that cannot be diversified away.

### 4. Factor Investing and Market Efficiency (Fama-French Context)
The text references the findings of Fama and French regarding market premiums:
*   **Size Premium:** Smaller companies (small-cap) tend to outperform larger companies over time.
*   **Value Premium:** "Value" stocks (low price relative to fundamentals) tend to outperform "growth" stocks.
*   **Market Efficiency:** The text touches on the idea that in an efficient market, it is difficult to consistently "beat" the market because all known information is already priced in.
