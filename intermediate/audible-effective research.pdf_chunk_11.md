This document segment is a comprehensive overview of research data management, from collection techniques and data preparation to the basics of descriptive statistical analysis.

Here are the key insights and facts, organized by topic:

---

### 🔍 I. Data Collection and Types

#### A. Methods of Data Gathering
*   **Questionnaires:**
    *   **Close-ended responses:** Easy to input directly into a spreadsheet (quantitative).
    *   **Open-ended responses:** Require manual *coding* or qualitative analysis.
    *   **Surveys:** Generally aim for a **large number of responses ($N$)** from a random selection.
*   **Interviews:** Tend to focus on a **smaller number of subjects** and do not always require random selection.
*   **Observational Data:** Derived from direct or indirect observation (e.g., field notes; observing facial expressions in focus groups).
*   **Written Records:** Data found in pre-existing documents (e.g., government reports, historical archives). These are not collected by the researcher for the study's primary purpose.
    *   **Content Analysis:** A method used to systematically analyze written materials (like news articles) by identifying and counting themes, tones, or keywords, thereby transforming text into quantifiable data.

#### B. Quantitative vs. Qualitative Data
| Feature | Quantitative Data | Qualitative Data |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nature** | Numbers; quantifiable information. | Descriptive; process-oriented; narratives. |
| **Scale** | Large $N$ (number of cases). | Small $N$ (depth of study per case). |
| **Analysis** | Suitable for statistical analysis. | Requires coding or deep thematic analysis. |
| **Insight** | Researchers often assign numbers (e.g., 0s and 1s) to make *any* data type processable statistically. | |

### 📊 II. Data Preparation and Coding

*   **Coding:** The essential process of transforming raw, non-numerical data into usable categories or numbers for analysis.
*   **Spreadsheets:** Are the primary tool for organization (cases in rows, variables in columns). They allow for basic statistics and spotting missing data.
*   **Dummy Variables:** Binary variables (0/1 or yes/no) used to code categorical data (e.g., 1 if the subject has blue eyes, 0 otherwise).
*   **Codebook:** A critical document that must accompany the data. It explains the definition, meaning, and numerical value for every single variable and code, ensuring **validity** and **reliability**.
*   **Reliability:** Ensuring that the coding is consistent, especially when multiple people (raters) are involved.
*   **Missing Data:** Must be accounted for by marking the cell with a specific value (rather than leaving it blank).

---

### **Key Concepts Summary:**

*   **Distribution:** Frequency charts or graphs showing how often values occur.
*   **Measures of Central Tendency:** Indicate the center of the data (Mean, Median, Mode).
*   **Measures of Variability:** Indicate how spread out the data is (Standard Deviation, Range).

---

### **What follows:**

*   **Descriptive Statistics:** Used to summarize and present data (means, standard deviations, frequencies).
*   **Inferential Statistics:** Used to draw conclusions or make predictions about a larger population based on a smaller sample.
