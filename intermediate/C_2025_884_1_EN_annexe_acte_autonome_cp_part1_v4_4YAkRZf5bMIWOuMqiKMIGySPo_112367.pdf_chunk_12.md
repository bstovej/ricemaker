This document provides a detailed legal interpretation of two specific prohibitions established under the **EU AI Act**: the untargeted scraping of facial images for databases, and the use of emotion recognition systems in specific environments.

Below are the key insights and facts categorized by the two main regulatory focuses.

---

### 1. Prohibition of Untargeted Facial Scraping (Article 5(1)(e))

This section addresses the practice of using AI to mass-collect facial images to build or expand recognition databases.

**The Core Prohibition:**
The Act prohibits the "placing on the market," "putting into service," or "use" of AI systems that create or expand facial recognition databases through the **untargeted scraping** of facial images from the internet or CCTV footage.

**The Four Cumulative Conditions:**
For a practice to be prohibited, **all four** of these conditions must be met simultaneously:
1.  **Action:** The practice involves placing a system on the market, putting it into service, or using it.
2.  **Purpose:** The goal is to create or expand facial recognition databases.
3.  **Method:** The data is gathered via AI-driven "untargeted scraping."
4.  **Source:** The images are sourced from the Internet or CCTV footage.

**Key Insights & Definitions:**
*   **"Untargeted" vs. "Targeted":** 
    *   **Untargeted (Prohibited):** Operates like a "vacuum cleaner," indiscriminately harvesting as much data as possible without focusing on specific individuals or groups.
s
    *   **Targeted (Permitted):** If the tool is instructed to find specific individuals or pre-defined groups (e.g., searching for a specific criminal or a group of victims), it is **not** prohibited.
*   **The "Internet" Clause:** Posting a photo on social media does **not** constitute consent for that image to be used in a facial recognition database.
*   **Definition of Database:** The prohibition applies to any organized collection of data used for rapid retrieval, whether the database is temporary, centralized, or decentralized.
*   **Out of Scope:** 
    *   Scraping biometric data *other* than facial images (e.g., voice) is not covered by this specific article.
    *   AI systems that generate images of *fictitious* people are permitted (as they do not recognize real persons).
    *   Using facial databases for training/testing AI models is permitted, provided the individuals are not identified.

### 2. Prohibition of Emotion Recognition (Article 5(1)(f))

This section addresses the use of AI to infer human emotions, particularly in sensitive environments.

**The Core Prohibition:**
The use of AI systems to **infer emotions** of a natural person is prohibited specifically within **workplace** and **educational institutions**.

**Key Insights & Definitions:**
*   **Rationale for Ban:** The ban is driven by concerns regarding the scientific unreliability of emotion recognition, the risk of discrimination, and the potential for "mass surveillance" or intrusion into privacy and human dignity—especially in "asymmetric" relationships (e.g., employer/employee or teacher/student).
*   **The "Biometric" Requirement:** The prohibition specifically applies to inferences made on the basis of **biometric data** (e.g., facial expressions, voice characteristics, gait, or even keystroke patterns).
*   **Inference vs. Observation:**
    *   **Prohibited (Inference):** Using AI to conclude that an employee is "unhappy" or a student is "furious" based on their facial movements or body language.
    *   **Permitted (Simple Observation/Physical State):** Noticing a person is smiling, or using AI to detect **physical states** like fatigue, pain, or sickness (e.g., for a pilot's safety) is **not** considered prohibited emotion recognition.
*   **Text Analysis Exception:** Using AI to analyze the *sentiment* or *tone* of written text (e.g., analyzing an article) is **not** prohibited because it does not rely on biometric data.

**Key Exemptions:**
*   **Medical & Safety Purposes:** The prohibition does **not** apply if the emotion recognition system is intended for medical reasons (e.g., diagnosing health conditions) or safety reasons (e.g., preventing accidents).
*   **Specific Contexts:** The ban is strictly limited to the **workplace** and **educational/training institutions**. Use in other sectors (like entertainment or advertising) is governed by different, non-prohibitive rules (such as transparency requirements).
