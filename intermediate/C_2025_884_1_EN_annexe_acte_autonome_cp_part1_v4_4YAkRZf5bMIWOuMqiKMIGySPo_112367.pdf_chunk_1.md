This document is an annex to a European Commission Communication (dated February 4, 2025) providing official guidelines on the **prohibited AI practices** under the EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689).

Below are the key insights and facts extracted from the provided text.

### 1. Core Purpose and Legal Status
*   **Objective:** The guidelines aim to provide legal clarity and a consistent interpretation of "unacceptable risk" AI practices to assist regulators, providers, and deployers in ensuring compliance.
*   **Legal Weight:** These guidelines are **non-binding**. The ultimate authoritative interpretation of the AI Act rests with the Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU).
*   **Effective Date:** The prohibitions listed in Article 5 of the AI Act began to apply on **February 2, 2025**.
*   **Regulatory Framework:** The AI Act follows a four-tier risk-based approach:
    1.  **Unacceptable Risk:** Prohibited (the focus of this document).
    2.  **High Risk:** Subject to strict requirements and obligations.
    3.  **Transparency Risk:** Subject to specific transparency obligations.
    4.  **Minimal/No Risk:** Unregulated (though voluntary codes of conduct may apply).

### 2. The Prohibited AI Practices (Article 5)
The document details eight specific categories of AI practices that are banned in the EU because they violate fundamental rights and Union values:

#### **A. Manipulation and Exploitation**
*   **Harmful Manipulation (5.1.a):** AI using subliminal, manipulative, or deceptive techniques to distort a person's behavior in a way that causes significant harm.
*   **Exploitation of Vulnerabilities (5.1.b):** AI that targets vulnerabilities related to age, disability, or specific socio-economic situations to distort behavior and cause harm.

#### **B. Social Control and Scoring**
*   **Social Scoring (5.1.c):** Evaluating or classifying individuals based on social behavior or personality traits, leading to detrimental or unjustified treatment in unrelated social contexts.
*   **Biometric Categorization (5.1.g):** Using biometric data to categorize people based on "sensitive" traits, including race, political opinions, religion, trade union membership, sex life, or sexual orientation.

#### **C. Surveillance and Biometrics**
*   **Untargeted Facial Scraping (5.1.e):** Creating or expanding facial recognition databases by "scraping" images from the internet or CCTV footage.
*   **Emotion Recognition (5.1.f):** Using AI to infer emotions in **workplace or educational settings** (with exceptions for medical or safety reasons).
*   **Real-time Remote Biometric Identification (RBI) (5.1.h):** The use of real-time biometric identification in publicly accessible spaces for law enforcement is prohibited, **except** in strictly defined cases (e.g., searching for victims, preventing terror threats, or locating suspects of specific serious crimes).

#### **D. Predictive Policing**
*   **Individual Crime Prediction (5.1.d):** AI systems that predict the likelihood of a person committing a crime based *solely* on profiling or personality traits. 
    *   *Exception:* Use is permitted if it supports human assessment based on objective, verifiable facts linked to actual criminal activity.

### 3. Fundamental Rights Context
The prohibitions are rooted in protecting the **EU Charter of Fundamental Rights**, specifically:
*   **Human Dignity and Equality:** Protecting against discrimination and social scoring.
*   **Privacy and Data Protection:** Limiting invasive biometric surveillance and scraping.
*   **Freedom of Thought and Expression:** Preventing manipulative/subliminal AI.
*   **Right to a Fair Trial:** Preventing biased predictive policing and profiling.
