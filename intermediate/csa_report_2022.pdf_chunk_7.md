Based on the document segment provided, which appears to be an excerpt from the **Singapore Cyber Landscape 2022** report, here is a review of the key insights and facts.

### **Overview**
The document outlines the multi-layered strategy employed by the Cyber Security Agency of Singapore (CSA) to build a trusted, resilient, and safer cyberspace. The strategy spans three main pillars: **Public Awareness**, **Organisational Empowerment**, and **Product/Infrastructure Security**.

---

### **1. Public Awareness & Community Engagement**
The CSA is implementing targeted outreach programs for different demographic groups to improve cyber hygiene:
*   **Seniors:** The *SG Cyber Safe Seniors Programme* has reached over **80,000 seniors** since 2021. Notable initiatives include the "Youths Help Seniors Go Digital" workshops, pairing students with seniors.
*   **Students:** The *Experience Cybersecurity Programme for Peer Support Leaders* (launched Nov 2022) trains student leaders via workshops and "Capture-the-Flag" exercises at Singtel’s Cyber Security Institute.
*   **Professionals (PMEs):** Through "Cyber Safe in the City" pop-up events in the Central Business District (CBD), the CSA engaged approximately **1,960 respondents**. Surveys revealed that PMEs are specifically interested in learning how to defend against ransomware and Business Email Compromise (BEC), and how to secure IoT devices.
*   **General Public:** Over **50,000 copies** of the "Better Cyber Safe Than Sorry" guide have been distributed to provide actionable cyber tips.

### **2. Strengthening Organisations & Enterprises**
The CSA focuses on "levelling up" businesses through tiered certification and resource provision:
*   **SG Cyber Safe Programme:** This programme uses a two-tier certification system:
    *   **Cyber Essentials Mark:** Targeted at SMEs and less digitalized organisations. Over **75 organisations** are certified or in the process.

    *   **Cyber Trust Mark:** Targeted at larger, more digitalized organisations. Over **25 organisations** are certified or in the process.
*   **Resource Distribution:** The CSA developed cybersecurity toolkits for business leaders, SME owners, and IT personnel, which have been downloaded more than **9,000 times**.
*   **Financial Support:** In collaboration with IMDA, the CSA supports SMEs in developing cybersecurity health plans through the **Productivity Solutions Grant**, helping over **860 organisations**.

### **3. Product Security & International Standards**
Singapore is actively working to secure the "Internet of Things" (IoT) and medical technology:
*   **Cybersecurity Labelling Scheme (CLS):** Aimed at consumer IoT devices (e.g., routers, smart lighting). Over **250 products** from global brands (including Google, Asus, TP-Link, and Nokia) have already been labelled.
*   **International Collaboration:** An agreement (**MRA**) was signed in October 2022 with Germany’s Federal Office for Information Security to recognize cybersecurity labels between the two nations. Singapore is also contributing to the development of the international standard **ISO/IEC 27404**.
*   **Medical Devices [CLS(MD)]:** Developed in collaboration with the Ministry of Health (MOH) and HSA, this scheme focuses on securing medical devices that handle health data.

### **4. Infrastructure & Digital Hygiene**
*   **Internet Hygiene Portal (IHP):** A one-stop platform for enterprises to perform self-assessments and adopt best practices.
*   **Internet Hygiene Rating (IHR) Table:** A tool used to evaluate the security posture of domains. As of late 2022, the system had scanned **2,300 domains** (12,000 unique websites).
*   **E-commerce Focus:** The CSA is collaborating with the **10 most popular e-commerce platforms** to improve the security of digital transactions.

### **5. Emerging Threats**
*   **Counter-Ransomware Task Force (CRTF):** The document notes the establishment of a multi-disciplinary task force involving various government agencies to implement a four-pillar strategy to effectively combat ransomware threats.
