### 📚 Course Overview and Scope

*   **Audience:** Research is presented as a skill for *everyone*, not just academics or scientists. It is applicable to professional projects, solving life puzzles, or evaluating programs.
*   **Overall Goal:** To provide systematic tools and guidelines that allow the reader to conduct research and, crucially, to evaluate the claims and findings of others.
*   **Structure (3 Parts):**
    1.  **Fundamentals:** Establishes the scientific method as the core framework, focusing on ethics and sound practice.
    2.  **Design Tools:** Teaches various methodologies (e.g., experiments, surveys, case studies) and how to select the appropriate design for a given question.
    3.  **Data Analysis & Presentation:** Covers quantitative and qualitative analysis techniques, data preparation, and communicating findings effectively.

### 🧠 Foundational Principles (The "Why" of Research)

**1. The Importance of Sound Methods:**
*   Research methods are systematic ways to study anything to advance knowledge, uncover new information, or explain causality. The rigor of the method matters as much as the findings themselves.
*   **Ethical Practice:** Understanding the historical development of ethical principles (like the Belmont Report) is covered.

**2. Scientific Mindset:**
*   **Openness:** A crucial principle is to be open to both the *unintended* and the *undesired* results.
*   **Falsification Principle:** The goal of research should not be to prove that initial ideas are correct, but rather to **try your hardest to disprove your ideas**.
*   **Objectivity:** Research must be conducted with neutrality, avoiding confirmation bias (the tendency to only seek information that supports existing beliefs).

**3. Characteristics of Good Research (The Criteria):**
*   **Systematic:** The research must follow a clear, traceable set of procedures (e.g., a defined research design).
*   **Objective:** The researcher must remain neutral throughout the process.

**B. The Research Cycle (The Steps):**

1.  **Identify/Design:** Formulate questions and structure the study.
2.  **Collect:** Gather data using various methods.
3.  **Analyze:** Interpret the gathered data.
4.  **Conclude/Report:** Formulate findings and recommendations.

**C. The Scientific Method (Step-by-Step):**

*   **Hypothesis (Initial Idea):** A testable proposed explanation.
*   **Observation/Literature Review:** Gathering existing knowledge.
*   **Methodology (Execution):** Detailed plan for data collection.
*   **Results:** Raw data and findings.
*   **Conclusion:** Interpretation of the results relative to the hypothesis.

**D. Key Terms & Concepts:**

*   **Correlation vs. Causation:** A critical distinction—just because two things happen together (correlation) does not mean one caused the other (causation).
*   **Validity:** The extent to which a test or study actually measures what it claims to measure.
*   **Reliability:** The consistency of the measurement; the ability to repeat the study and get the same results.

### 🧠 Summary of Core Takeaways

*   **The process is cyclical:** Research is rarely linear; findings often lead to new questions.
*   **The approach must match the goal:** If you want to count how many people use a product (numbers), use quantitative methods. If you want to know *why* they use it (reasons/stories), use qualitative methods.
*   **Objectivity is paramount:** Good research aims to minimize bias, relying on systematic procedures.
