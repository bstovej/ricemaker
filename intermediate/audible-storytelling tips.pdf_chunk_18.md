### **Core Concept: Point of View and Narrative Voice**
The text explores how the choice of narrator (the "voice") and their perspective (the "point of view") fundamentally shape a story's impact, reliability, and emotional connection with the reader.

### **1. The Primary Types of Narrators**

#### **A. The "Recording Eye" (Objective/Neutral Narrator)**
This narrator acts as a camera, recording events without providing much internal thought or judgment.
*   **Function:** Provides a sense of detachment or observation.
*   **Example:** **Nick Carraway in *The Great Gatsby***. The text notes that while he is the narrator, he functions as a way to observe the broader social dynamics of the era.

#### **B. The "Recording Eye" with Subjectivity (The "Observer" Narrator)**
A narrator who observes the world but provides a lens through which the reader views the characters.
*   **Example: Nick Carraway (continued):** While he records events, his presence allows the reader to see the characters through his specific social and moral lens.

#### **C. The "Subjective" or "Self-Telling" Narrators**
These narrators are central to the story, providing their own thoughts, biases, and emotional reactions. This is subdivided into two categories:

*   **The "Recording Eye" as Observer (The Witness):**
    *   **Function:** A narrator who is present for the events but remains somewhat external, often serving as a "window" for the reader.
    *   **Example: Nick Carraway in *The Great Gatsby*:** He is an observer whose presence allows the reader to witness the tragedy.
    *   **Example: Nathan in *The Great Gatsby*:** (Referenced as a way to observe the characters).

*   **The "Heroic" or "Self-Telling" Narrator:**
    *   **Function:** The narrator is the protagonist. The story is their personal experience, and their biases, prejudices, and emotions are central to the narrative.
    *   **Example: Philip Marlowe (in detective fiction):** A narrator whose internal morality and reactions drive the plot.
    *   **Example: Jane Eyre in *Jane Eyre*:** A highly subjective narrator where the reader experiences the world directly through her emotional and moral development.

### **2. Key Literary Techniques & Elements**

#### **The "Double Consciousness" / "Double Consciousness" of the Narrator**
The text highlights a sophisticated level of narration where the narrator possesses two layers:
1.  **The Character's Voice:** The immediate, emotional, and often biased reaction of the person living the story.
2.  **The Author's/Reflective Voice:** The layer where the narrator (or author) looks back on the events, often with the benefit of hindsight or a different moral perspective.
*   **Example: Huckleberry Finn:** The text uses Huck as a prime example of "double consciousness." The reader experiences the raw, uneducated, and prejudiced perspective of the child (the character), while simultaneously recognizing the deeper moral struggle and the author's critique of slavery and societal norms (the reflective layer).

#### **Narrative Reliability and Bias**
*   **The Unreliable Narrator:** The text touches on the idea that a narrator's personal biases (such as the prejudices held by Huck Finn) can create a tension between what the character believes and what the reader understands to be true.
*   **The "Moral Compass" of the Narrator:** The text discusses how a narrator’s internal struggle (like Huck Finn’s struggle between the laws of man and the laws of conscience) becomes the central engine of the narrative.
