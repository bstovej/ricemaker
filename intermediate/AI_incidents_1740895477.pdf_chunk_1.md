## Key Insights and Facts: Common Reporting Framework for AI Incidents

This document outlines the development of a standardized, global mechanism for reporting harms and failures related to Artificial Intelligence (AI), designed to enhance international governance and regulatory alignment.

---

### 💡 Core Insights (The "Why" and "How")

1. **Urgency and Necessity:** The report stresses that due to the rapid global deployment of AI, the increase in incidents (harms, safety issues, discrimination) is inevitable. Establishing a common reporting framework *now* is critical to prevent an inefficient and costly retroactive system.
2. **Global Benchmark, Local Flexibility:** The framework serves as a **global standard (a common benchmark)**, allowing participating countries to harmonize their incident reporting while still retaining the flexibility to tailor their responses according to their specific domestic policies and legal frameworks.
3. **Complementary, Not Replacement:** The framework is explicitly designed to **complement**—not replace—existing national incident reporting systems or regulatory guidelines. Its goal is to enhance international interoperability.
4. **Living Framework:** The framework is intended to be tested and continually improved by incorporating open submissions into the **AI Incidents Monitor (AIM)** platform, providing a real-world, dynamic environment for evaluation.

### 📑 Key Facts and Specifications

#### I. Scope and Objectives
* **Primary Goal:** To provide policymakers with a clear understanding of AI incidents in diverse contexts, enabling the identification of high-risk systems and the assessment of current and emerging risks.
* **Interoperability:** It aims to facilitate the sharing of knowledge and incident information across jurisdictions without infringing upon privacy, intellectual property, or security laws.
* **Report Volume:** The final framework consolidates **29 criteria** for reporting.

#### II. Definitions of Harm
The document provides distinct definitions for two key concepts:

* **AI Incident:** An event or series of events where the development, use, or malfunction of an AI system **directly or indirectly leads to** specific types of harm.
    * *Examples of Harm:* Injury to health, disruption of critical infrastructure, violation of human rights, or harm to property/environment.
* **AI Hazard:** An event or series of events where the AI system's use or malfunction **could plausibly lead to** an AI incident.

#### III. Methodology and Development
* **Source Inputs:** The development of the framework was informed by a detailed stocktaking process of **four existing, key resources/frameworks:**
    1. OECD Framework for the Classification of AI systems.
    2. Responsible AI Collaborative AI Incidents Database (AIID).
    3. OECD Global Portal on Product Recalls.
    4. OECD AI Incidents Monitor (AIM).
* **Criteria Derivation:**
    * **Total Potential Criteria:** 88 criteria were initially identified across the four resources.
    * **Final Criteria:** 29 criteria were selected based on two criteria types:
        * **Recurrent Criteria (10):** Criteria that appear in at least three of the four analyzed frameworks (e.g., affected stakeholders, sector of deployment).
        * **Complementary Criteria:** Criteria providing essential details not covered by the recurrent criteria.

#### IV. Structural Dimensions
The 88 potential criteria are grouped into **8 comprehensive dimensions** to ensure thorough reporting:

1. Incident metadata
2. Harm details
3. People and planet (Impacted stakeholders/AI principles)
4. Economic context
5. Data and input (Training data description)
6. AI model (Type, evolvability, usage rights)
7. Task and output (Action autonomy level)
8. Other information about this incident
