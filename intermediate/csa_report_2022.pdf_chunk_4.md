### 2. Singapore’s Cyber Threat Statistics (2022)
The Cyber Security Agency (CSA) reported a mix of increasing and decreasing threats in Singapore for 2022:
*   **Phishing Attempts:** **8,500 cases** (a massive **174% increase** from 3,100 cases in 2021).
*   **Infected Infrastructure:** **81,500 systems** (a **13% decrease** from 94,000 in 2021).
*   **Ransomware Incidents:** **132 cases** (a slight **4% decrease** from 137 in 2021).
*   **Website Defacements:** **340 websites** (a **19% decrease** from 419 in 2021).

### 3. Deep Dive: Phishing Trends in Singapore
Phishing remains the most significant growing threat, characterized by several key factors:
*   **Top Targeted Sectors:**
    1.  **Banking & Financial Services:** Accounted for **more than 80%** of all phishing attempts on organizations.
    2.  **Government:** Targeted via agencies like LTA, Singpass, and IRAS.
    3.  **Logistics:** Specifically targeting services like SingPost.
*   **Primary Vector:** Over **50% of phishing attempts** use malicious links in emails or SMS alerts using the **".xyz" domain**, which is popular because it is extremely cheap to register.
*   **Drivers of Phishing:** Increased digitalization following the COVID-19 pandemic and heightened anxieties surrounding China-based banking (following the China rural bank scandal) have provided "compelling lures" for attackers.

### 4. Key Drivers of the Cyber Landscape
*   **Increased Attack Surface:** While "infected infrastructure" decreased, the number of connected devices per household in Singapore grew from **6.5 (in 2020) to 7.0 (in 2021)**, expanding the potential targets for attackers.
*   **Shift in Hacktivism:** Website defacements are decreasing because hacktivists are migrating to social media platforms to achieve wider reach.
*   **Ransomware Vulnerability:** Despite a slight drop in cases, ransomware remains a major threat, particularly targeting **SMEs in the Manufacturing and Retail** sectors.

### 5. Note on Methodology
The CSA revised its reporting method in 2022 to provide more accurate "actual threat levels":
*   **Phishing:** Now measures the number of **attempts** rather than just the number of malicious URLs.
*   **Infected Infrastructure:** Now combines "command and control (C&C) servers" and "botnet drones" into a single category.
