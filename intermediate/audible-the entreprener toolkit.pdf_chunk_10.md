    *   Under **accrual accounting**, revenue is recorded when earned and expenses when incurred, regardless of when cash moves.
    *   The text highlights the importance of tracking **Accounts Receivable** (money owed to the company) and **Accounts Payable** (money the company owes).

### **3. Key Financial Metrics & Terms**
*   **Assets:** Items of value owned by the company (e.g., cash, accounts receivable, inventory, and equipment).
*   **Liabilities:** Debts or obligations owed to others (e.g., accounts payable).
*   **Equity:** The residual interest in the assets of the company after deducting liabilities.
*   **Accounts Receivable:** Money customers owe the business for goods or services already delivered.
*   **Accounts Payable:** Money the business owes to suppliers or creditors.
*   **Net Income:** The "bottom line" or profit remaining after all expenses are subtracted from total revenue.

### **4. Strategic Financial Management**
*   **Cash Flow Management:** A critical skill for entrepreneurs is managing the timing of cash inflows and outflows to ensure the business remains liquid and can meet its obligations (like paying employees and suppliers).
*   **Analyzing Operations:** Analyzing the cash flow statement helps determine if a company's core operations are generating enough cash to sustain the business without relying on external financing.
*   **Investment Decisions:** Understanding how cash is used for investing activities (e.g., buying equipment/assets) versus financing activities (e.g., taking out loans or paying dividends) is vital for long-term stability.
