This document segment outlines the legal requirements, procedural safeguards, and regulatory constraints regarding the use of **real-time Remote Biometric Identification (RBI) systems** by law enforcement under the **EU AI Act**.

The following are the key insights and facts categorized by regulatory theme:

### 1. The Principle of Prior Authorization
*   **Requirement for Authorization:** The use of real-time RBI systems in publicly accessible spaces for law enforcement purposes is not automatic. It requires **prior authorization** from a judicial or an independent administrative authority.
*   **The "Double Assessment" Standard:** Before deployment, two assessments must occur:
    1.  The user must perform a **Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment (FRIA)**.
    2.  The competent authority must assess whether the use is **necessary and proportionate** to achieve specific objectives (e.g., searching for victims, preventing specific threats, or identifying offenders).
*   **Scope of Use:** Authorization is not granted for the mere installation of equipment (e.g., biometric-ready cameras); rather, it must be sought for **each individual, concrete use** of the system.
*   **Legal Basis:** For such use to be authorized, the relevant Member State must have existing national laws that permit the use of these systems.

### 2. Human Oversight and Decision-Making
*   **Ban on Solely Automated Decisions:** Article 5(3) explicitly prohibits any decision that would have an **adutverse legal effect** on an individual from being made solely based on the output of a real-time RBI system.
*   **Human Intervention Requirements:** 
    *   Human agents must be involved in verifying and interpreting the system's output.
    *   The person in charge of oversight must possess sufficient **"AI literacy, training, and authority"** to recognize when the system is malfunctioning or underperforming.
    *   The FRIA must clearly define the procedures for deployment, the role of the human agent, and instructions for interpreting results.

### 3. The "Urgency" Exception and its Consequences
*   **Emergency Deployment:** In "duly justified" situations of urgency (where obtaining prior authorization is objectively impossible, such as a live shooter scenario), law enforcement may begin using the system before receiving authorization.
*   **The 24-Hour Rule:** If the system is used under the urgency exception, the request for authorization must be submitted **without undue delay** and **no later than 24 hours** after the commencement of use.
*   **Mandatory Deletion upon Rejection:** If an emergency authorization request is **rejected**, the law enforcement authority must:
    1.  Cease use of the system **immediately**.
    2.  Immediately **discard and delete** all data, results, and outputs generated during that use.

### 4. Registration and Risk Mitigation
*   **EU Database Registration:** Deployers of real-time RBI systems used for law enforcement are obligated to register the system in a specific **EU database** (as per Article 49).
*   **Transparency of Emergency Use:** While registration can be delayed in extreme emergencies, the delay must not be deliberate and must be justified by the circumstances.
*   **Redress and Accountability:** Deployers must establish **redress measures**, governance procedures, and complaint mechanisms (e.g., for instances of misidentification) to mitigate risks and protect fundamental rights.
