This report, published in **May 2024** by **Boston Consulting Group (BCG)** and the **Semiconductor Industry Association (SIA)**, analyzes the shifting landscape of the global semiconductor supply chain. It focuses on the transition from a highly efficient but geographically concentrated model toward a more "resilient" and diversified global footprint.

The following are the key insights and facts extracted from the document:

### 1. The Core Paradox: Efficiency vs. Vulnerability
*   **Economic Benefit:** The globally integrated supply chain is highly efficient, realizing **$45 billion–$125 billion in annual cost savings**. This integration keeps chip prices **35%–65% lower** than they would be with fully localized supply chains.
*   **The Risk:** This efficiency created extreme geographic concentration. The report identifies at least **50 points** in the supply chain where a single region holds **over 65% of the global market share**, making the industry vulnerable to pandemics, natural disasters, and geopolitical conflicts.

### 2. Massive Surge in Capital Investment
The report highlights a dramatic increase in private-sector spending on wafer fabrication:
*   **Projected Investment (2024–2032):** **$2.3 trillion**.
*   **Comparison:** This is significantly higher than the **$720 billion** invested in the previous decade (2013–2022).
*   **The US "Reversal":** Before the CHIPS Act, the US was projected to capture only 9% of global capital expenditures. Following recent incentives, the US is now projected to capture **28%** of these global expenditures.

### 3. Geographic Shifts and "Resilience"
The industry is moving toward "geographic diversification," specifically in two key areas:
*   **Wafer Fabrication (Leading-Edge):** Capacity is diversifying beyond Taiwan and South Korea to include the **US, Europe, and Japan**.
    *   The US is expected to increase its fab capacity by **23%** between 2022 and 2032, raising its global share from **10% to 14%**.
    *   Major new investments include Intel/TSMC in Germany, and large-scale "mega clusters" in South Korea.
*   **Assembly, Test, and Packaging (ATP):** While China and Taiwan will remain leaders, new activity is expanding into **Southeast Asia, Latin America, and Eastern Europe**.
    *   Advanced packaging (specifically **chiplets**) is a key driver for building new ATP capacity in the US and Europe, closer to new wafer fabrication sites.

### 4. Segmented Industry Specialization
The supply chain remains highly specialized by region:
*   **Design, Core IP, and EDA:** Primarily led by the **United States**.
*   **Manufacturing Equipment ("Tools"):** Led by the **US, EU, and Japan**.
*   **Materials:** Primarily led by **Mainland China, Japan, Taiwan, and South Korea**.
*   **Wafer Fabrication & ATP:** Currently dominated by **Taiwan and South Korea**.

### 5. Government Incentives (The "Push" Factors)
Major economies are using massive subsidies to drive these shifts:
*   **United States:** The **CHIPS Act** provides **$39 billion** in grants and a 25% investment tax credit (ITC).
*   **European Union:** The **European Chips Act** is driving large-scale investments in Germany and France.
*   **Mainland China:** Utilizing large-scale equity funds (the "Big Fund") and domestic standards to increase self-sufficiency.
*   **Other Regions:** Taiwan, South Korea, Japan, and India are all expanding incentive programs to attract/retain capacity.

### 6. Critical Risks and Future Requirements
The report warns of several "bottlenecks" that could undermine these resilience efforts:
*   **Non-Market Risks:** Large-scale industrial policies could lead to **over-concentration or oversupply** if investments are driven by subsidies rather than market demand.
*   **The "Self-Sufficiency" Trap:** The authors warn that extreme policies aimed at full "country-level self-sufficiency" will **undermine resilience, increase costs, and stifle innovation**.
*   **The Talent Gap:** A robust pipeline of engineers and technicians is as vital as physical factories. The industry faces a tight labor market that requires improved workforce development and favorable immigration policies.
*   **Supply-Demand Imbalance:** While advanced nodes are expanding, there is a risk of supply-demand imbalances in **mature node capacity**.
