This document segment, an excerpt from a McKinsey & Company article, focuses on the concept of the **"Personal Operating Model"** and how leaders can use purpose, support networks, and self-reflection to navigate high-stakes organizational changes.

### Key Insights

**1. The Value of a "Kitchen Cabinet"**
*   **Quality over Quantity:** Effective leaders do not need massive networks; they need a small, trusted group of people (inside and outside the workplace) to serve as impartial sounding boards.
*   **Cognitive Diversity:** A strong support group (like the "kitchen cabinet" used by the featured CEO) provides differing perspectives and challenges a leader's assumptions, which is critical for handling sensitive business transformations.
*   **Work-Life Balance:** These networks serve a dual purpose: helping with professional decision-making and assisting in maintaining a balance between work and personal life.

**2. Purpose as a Driver of Resilience**
*   **Motivation through Meaning:** A sense of purpose is the "fuel" that allows leaders to endure the long hours and resistance that accompany large-scale change.
*   **Alignment of Values and Business:** Successful leaders link their personal values (e.g., diversity, innovation, or mentorship) to business outcomes, creating a more energized leadership style.
*   **The Legacy Mindset:** Taking a long-term perspective—specifically thinking about the legacy one leaves behind—helps leaders navigate "tectonic" organizational shifts.

**3. The Concept of the "Personal Operating Model" (POM)**
*   **Continuous Upgrades:** Leaders should treat their personal operating models with the same rigor an IT department uses to upgrade software. This involves regularly updating priorities, roles, time management, and energy usage.
*   **Scaling Productivity:** The authors suggest that if individual leaders master their POMs, the benefits can scale. When everyone in an organization knows how to manage their energy and priorities, overall organizational productivity increases.
*   **Humanizing Leadership:** Sharing personal operating models (as seen in the insurance company example) can humanize executives and provide a roadmap for the next generation of leaders.

**4. The Necessity of Accountability**
*   **The Self-Maintenance Gap:** The text notes that humans are often better at caring for others (like pets) than for themselves. Therefore, leaders cannot rely on willpower alone.
*   **Accountability Partners:** To successfully maintain a POM, leaders need "accountability partners"—such as executive assistants, colleagues, or family members—to help them stay on track, reflect, and make bold decisions.

### Key Facts
*   **Source:** The article is authored by Arne Gast (McKinsey Amsterdam) and Suchita Prasad (McKinsey Singapore), published by McKinsey & Company in 2024.
*   **Case Study (CEO):** A former financial-services CEO credited her "kitchen cabinet" (husband, former boss, consulting partner, and friends) for her success in business transformation.
*   **Case Study (Insurance Company):** A multinational insurance company successfully used "fireside chats" featuring board members' personal operating models to anchor its leadership development program.
*   **Management Principle:** The text references management expert Peter Drucker’s observation that, in a sense, every leader is a "volunteer" because they ultimately choose how to use their working years.
