This document segment provides guidance on the interpretation and implementation of the **EU AI Act**, specifically regarding prohibited practices involving Real-time Biometric Identification (RBI) and the timeline for the Act's enforcement.

### **1. Key Distinction: Prohibited vs. Permitted Use of RBI**
The document establishes a clear boundary for the use of Real-time Biometric Identification (RBI) based on whether the activity constitutes "law enforcement."

*   **Permitted (Not Prohibited):** 
    *   **Private/Commercial use:** Using RBI for non-law enforcement purposes, such as hotels recognizing VIP guests or shopping malls identifying shoplifters for private security, is generally permitted.

*   **Prohibited:** 
    *   **Delegated Law Enforcement:** When a private entity (e.g., a shopping mall) is **entrusted by the police** to use real-time RBI to find individuals (e.g., shoplifters) in publicly accessible spaces. This is prohibited because it functions as law enforcement without meeting the specific legal exceptions outlined in Article 5(1)(h).
*   **Legal Precedent on Retrospective Use:** The document references the *Glukhin v Russia* case, noting that the use of Facial Recognition Technology (FRT) must be "strictly necessary" and "proportionate." Even if crime detection is a legitimate aim, the "highly intrusive" nature of FRT means its use must address a "pressing social need."

### **2. Implementation Timeline & The "Interim Period"**
The document outlines a staggered rollout of the AI Act, creating a critical window for compliance.

*   **February 2, 2025 (Prohibitions Begin):** The prohibitions on prohibited AI practices (Article 5) become active. This applies to all AI systems, even those already on the market.
*   **August 2, 2025 (Governance & Penalties Begin):** The chapters regarding enforcement, market surveillance authorities, and financial penalties become applicable.
*   **The Interim Period (Feb 2025 – Aug 2025):** 
    *   **Legal Reality:** Although official market surveillance authorities and fines are not yet active, the prohibitions are **fully mandatory**. 
    *   **Direct Effect:** Providers and deployers cannot legally use prohibited systems during this window. Affected parties can still use national courts to seek **interim injunctions** to stop prohibited practices, as the prohibitions have "direct effect."

### **3. Nature of the Guidelines**
*   **Iterative Process:** The current document is a "first interpretation." The Commission intends to update these guidelines based on practical experience, technological advancements, and rulings from the Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU).
*   **Stakeholder Engagement:** The Commission explicitly calls for input from AI providers, deployers, the AI Board, and civil society organizations to shape future interpretations.
