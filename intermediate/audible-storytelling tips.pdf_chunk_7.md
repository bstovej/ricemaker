It discusses the literary techniques of character development, specifically focusing on the distinction between **flat characters** and **round characters** (referencing E.M. Forster's famous terminology).

Below is a summary of the key concepts presented in the text:

### **1. Flat Characters**
*   **Definition:** Characters built around a single idea or quality.
*   **Function:** They are often used to move the plot forward or provide background (e.g., a character who represents a specific social class or a "type").
*   **Purpose:** They can be efficient and easily recognizable, allowing the author to focus more on the plot or more complex characters.
*   **Risk:** If overused, they can become "cardboard" or one-dimensional.

### **2. Round Characters**
*   **Definition:** Complex characters capable of surprising the reader in a convincing way.
*   **Key Traits:**
    *   **Complexity:** They possess multiple facets, contradictions, and layers of personality.
    *   **Predictability vs. Surprise:** While they can behave in ways the reader doesn't expect, those surprises must be "convincing"—meaning they must feel consistent with the character's established nature.
    *   **Humanity:** They feel "real" because they embody the psychological depth and unpredictability found in actual human beings.
*   **The Goal of Surprise:** The text emphasizes that a good "round" character provides a surprise that makes the reader say, *"Of course they did that!"* rather than *"That makes no sense."*

### **3. The Relationship Between the Two**
*   **Efficiency:** The text suggests that a skilled writer uses flat characters to manage the "weight" of the story, ensuring that the reader's emotional energy is focused on the round characters.
*   **Balance:** A story needs a balance. Too many round characters can overwhelm the reader and slow the plot; too many flat characters can make the story feel shallow or unengaging.

### **Key Literary Terms Mentioned:**
*   **Characterization:** The process by which an author reveals the personality of a character.
*   **Consistency:** The requirement that a character's actions, even surprising ones, must align with their established traits to maintain reader trust.
*   **Complexity:** The depth of thought, emotion, and motivation attributed to a character.
