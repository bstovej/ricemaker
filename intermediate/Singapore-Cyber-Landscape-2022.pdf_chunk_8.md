Based on the provided document segment from the **Singapore Cyber Landscape 2022**, here are the key insights and facts organized by thematic area:

### 1. Counter-Ransomware Strategy (CRTF)
Singapore has established the **Counter-Ransomware Task Force (CRTF)** to address ransomware as a critical national security risk.
*   **Strategic Pillars:** The strategy is built on four pillars:
    1.  Strengthening defenses of high-risk targets (e.g., Critical Information Infrastructure - CII).
    2.  Disrupting the ransomware business model to reduce the financial payoff for attackers.
    3.  Supporting victims to prevent ransom payments, thereby starving the ransomware industry.
    4.  Working with international partners for a coordinated global response.
*   **Key Achievements:** 
    *   Developed a practical **blueprint** for government agencies to respond to ransomware.
    *   Achieved "Whole-of-Government" (WOG) alignment on the urgency of the threat.
    *   Played a leading role in the **Cyber Ransomware Initiative (CRI)**, a plurilateral effort involving 3/9+ countries (including the US, UK, and Germany) to arrest the global ransomware industry.
    *   Influenced international platforms such as the Financial Action Task Force (FATF) and the UN Office on Drugs and Crime (UNODC).

### 2. SME Protection: Asset Based Cyber Defence (ABCD)
To bridge the cybersecurity gap for smaller entities, Singapore utilizes the **ABCD** initiative.
*   **Model:** Operates as a **Security-as-a-Service (SecaaS)** model using Managed Security Service Providers (MSSPs) to provide 24/7 monitoring and response.
*   **Scope:** Aims to protect SMEs and individuals against malware, data theft, and insider threats.
*   **Key Stats:** 
    *   Over **500 SMEs** have onboarded the ABCD SecaaS.
    *   As of May 2022, the system detected and defended against nearly **4,000 cyber threats**.
    *   Helps organizations (like the Singapore National Co-operative Federation) attain the "Cyber Essentials" certification.

### 3. Cybersecurity Regulation and Monitoring
The Cyber Security Agency (CSA) is actively regulating the industry and monitoring threats.
*   **Cybersecurity Services Regulation Office (CSRO):** Launched on April 11, 2022, to license providers of penetration testing and managed SOC monitoring. 
    *   **Stats:** As of Dec 31, 2022, it received ~730 applications and approved nearly **400 licenses**.
    *   **Goal:** To address "information asymmetry" between consumers and providers and safeguard consumer interests.
*   **National Cyber Threat Monitoring Centre (NCTMC):** Focuses on proactive threat watching.
    *   **2022 Impact:** Worked with SingCERT and CII sectors to address over **5,000 leaked credentials** and over **50 phishing websites**.

### 4. International Cyber Diplomacy
Singapore continues to position itself as a global leader in cyber diplomacy and capacity building.
*   **Singapore International Cyber Week (SICW):** The 7th edition saw significant international participation, including ministerial officials from ASEAN, Australia, UK, US, and others. Notably, it saw first-time attendees from countries like Canada, Japan, and the Netherlands.
*   **ASEAN Engagement:** Active involvement in the 7th ASEAN Ministerial Conference on Cybersecurity (AMCC) to foster regional cooperation.
