This document, a Goldman Sachs Global Investment Research report, explores the transformative impact of Artificial Intelligence on the software industry. It presents a debate between the resilience of established "incumbents" and the disruptive potential of "AI-native" startups.

The following are the key insights and facts extracted from the text:

### 1. The Core Thesis: "Re-architecture" vs. "Add-on"
The document highlights a fundamental shift in how software is built and used.
*   **Beyond Incrementalism:** Rick Sherlund argues that the AI shift is not a simple "bolt-on" of new features (like the move to mobile), but a fundamental "re-architecture" similar to the move to relational databases in the 80s/90s. 
*   **From Menus to Agents:** Traditional SaaS relies on human-centric, menu-driven interfaces and hard-coded workflows. The future lies in **agentic AI**, where LLM-based agents use reasoning to execute multi-step tasks (e.g., moving from a customer support role to a sales role autonomously).
*   **The "Vibe Coding" Fallacy:** While AI can automate code generation, Sherlund notes that coding is only 15–20% of creating enterprise systems. Complexities like data structure, security, and workflow design remain critical, meaning AI will not make software development "trivial."

### 2. Competitive Landscape: Incumbents vs. AI-Native Firms
The document weighs the "moats" of legacy companies against the agility of new entrants.
*   **The Incumbent Advantage (The Moat):** Companies like Microsoft, Salesforce, and ServiceNow possess "systems of record," deep domain expertise, and massive proprietary datasets. These "moats" allow them to layer AI on top of existing infrastructure, making them "fast followers" who can potentially outpace competitors.
*   **The AI-Native Strategy:** New firms are expected to use a **"land and expand"** strategy—entering the market at the lower end (SMBs) where there is less resistance, and gradually moving upmarket to challenge giants, much like Salesforce did historically.
*   **The Risk of "Value Abstraction":** The danger for incumbents is failing to execute. If they fail to innovate, their value could erode from "immersive ecosystems" to mere "databases."

### 3. Sector-Specific Outlooks
The analysts provide a segmented view of which software layers will win or lose:
*   **Winners (High Opportunity):**
    *   **Infrastructure & Data:** Viewed as the "picks and shovels." **Snowflake, MongoDB, and Rubrik** are highlighted because AI increases the demand for clean, governed, and secure data.
    *   **Cybersecurity:** **Cloudflare, Palo Alto Networks, and CrowdStrike** are viewed as high-quality assets due to their data architecture and "leading-edge roadmaps."
    *   **Governance & Security:** As autonomous agents increase, the need for guardrails becomes a major growth driver.
*   **At-Risk (High Disruption):**
    *   **Applications:** The application layer is considered more vulnerable to disruption by agentic AI.
    *   **Vertical Software:** While firms in specific industries (e.g., insurance, life sciences) have strong data moats, they risk being "unbundled" if a general-purpose agent layer can perform their functions.

### 4. Market Facts & Financial Data
The document provides concrete evidence of a significant recent downturn in the software sector:
*   **ETF Performance:** The iShares Expanded Tech-Software Sector ETF (**IGV**) has declined approximately **17% Year-to-Date (YTD)** and **26%** from its late 2025 highs.
*   **Valuation Compression:** Software valuations have plummeted. The IGV NTM P/E ratio is currently around **22x**, a massive drop from its COVID-era peak of **51x**.
*   **Market Cap Loss:** The ten largest stocks within the IGV ETF have lost a combined total of nearly **$800 billion** in market capitalization since the beginning of the year.
*   **Investment Sentiment:** Despite the selloff, analysts remain "bullish" on certain assets, suggesting the recent price action may have "gone too far" due to a gap between market perception and the actual reality of incumbent resilience.

*   **Application Leaders:** **ServiceNow, Salesforce, and HubSpot**.
*   **Infrastructure/Data:** **Snowflake**.
