This document provides a legal interpretation of the **EU AI Act** regarding the use of **Real-Time Remote Biometric Identification (RBI)**—such as facial recognition—in publicly accessible spaces by law enforcement.

The following are the key insights and facts categorized by the document's primary themes:

### 1. Defining "Publicly Accessible Spaces"
The determination of whether a space is "publicly accessible" is based on a **case-by-case analysis** of accessibility rather than ownership.

*   **Criteria for Public Access:** A space is public if it is accessible to the general public, even if it requires a ticket, registration, or meets an age requirement (e.g., a concert venue or an age-restricted trade fair).
*   **The "Unlocked Door" Rule:** An unlocked door or gate does not automatically make a space public. If signs or circumstances indicate restricted access (e.g., a gated residential area), it is not considered a public space.
*   **Irrelevance of Ownership:** A space can be owned by a private entity (e.g., a shopping mall) or a public entity and still be considered "publicly accessible."
*   **Exclusions (Not Public Spaces):**
    *   **Online Spaces:** Chat rooms and social media are not "physical spaces" and are excluded from this specific prohibition.
    *   **Controlled Workplaces:** Offices or factories requiring badge access or entry control.
    *   **Specific Security Zones:** Prisons and border control areas (though the streets leading to a border crossing *are* public).
*   **Dual-Function Spaces:** Certain areas have split status. For example, an airport's common areas are "publicly accessible," but the specific area used for border control is "excluded."

### 2. Scope of Law Enforcement Activities
The prohibition on real-time RBI applies to all law enforcement activities, including those performed by private parties acting "on behalf of" the state.

*   **Definition of Law Enforcement:** Activities related to the prevention, investigation, detection, or prosecution of criminal offenses, and the execution of criminal penalties.
*   **The "On Behalf Of" Distinction:** 
    *   **Included:** If a private entity (e.g., a bank or a transport company) conducts security under the **instruction and supervision** of law enforcement, their actions fall under the AI Act's law enforcement restrictions.
    *   **Excluded:** If a private entity detects crime on its **own behalf** (e.g., a bank detecting its own fraud), they are not considered to be performing "law enforcement" under this specific article.

### 3. The Three Legal Exceptions to the Prohibition
While real-time RBI is generally prohibited in public spaces for law enforcement, the AI Act allows for **three specific, exhaustive exceptions**. Importantly, these exceptions only become active if a **Member State passes domestic law** to authorize them.

**I. Targeted Search for Victims and Missing Persons**
*   **Scope:** Limited to locating and identifying victims of:
    1.  Abduction.
    2.  Trafficking in human beings.
    3.  Sexual exploitation of human beings.
*   **Missing Persons:** Includes searches for missing persons, though the document notes that searches for adults are subject to different legal standards (e.g., whether the disappearance is a law enforcement matter or a purely administrative one).

**II. Prevention of Imminent Threats**
*   **Threat to Life/Safety:** Must be a **specific, substantial, and imminent** threat to the life or physical safety of natural persons. This includes threats to **critical infrastructure** (e.g., a power plant or water supply).
*   **Terrorist Attacks:** Must be a **genuine and present** or **genuine and foreseeable** threat of a terrorist attack. This allows for tracking "terrorists on the move" even if the specific target is not yet known.

**III. Identification of Suspects for Serious Crimes**
*   **Scope:** Limited to the identification of suspects involved in specific serious crimes listed in **Annex II** of the AI Act.
*   **Threshold:** The crime must be punishable by a maximum period of **at least four years** of imprisonment or a detention order.
