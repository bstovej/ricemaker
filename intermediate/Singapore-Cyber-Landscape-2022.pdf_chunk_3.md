Based on the document segment provided (which appears to be an excerpt from the **Singapore Cyber Landscape 2022** report), here are the key insights and facts organized by theme.

### 1. Evolution of Ransomware Tactics
The landscape of ransomware is shifting from simple data encryption to more sophisticated, "professionalized" methods.
*   **Shift to "Data Kidnapping":** There is a significant rise in **malware-free attacks** (extortion-only). Instead of encrypting files, attackers focus on exfiltrating sensitive data and threatening to leak it. 
    *   **Fact:** CrowdStrike reported that malware-free attacks accounted for **71%** of their detections in 2022, up from 62% the previous year.
*   **Professionalization:** Ransomware groups are increasingly operating like tech startups, with structured models (Ransomware-as-a-Service or RaaS) and diversified portfolios targeting Linux and Cloud environments.
*   **Extreme Coercion:** Attackers are using "coercion by any means," including threatening the families of victims or leaking highly sensitive/disturbing content (e.g., the Medusa group leaking photos of cancer patients).
*   **Notable Groups:** 
    *   **Lapsus$:** Targeted high-profile tech companies like Nvidia and Samsung.
    *   **Karakurt:** Targeted healthcare institutions.

### 2. The Failure of Ransom Payments ("Paying Up Does Not Pay")
The document highlights a "vicious cycle" where paying ransoms emboldens attackers rather than resolving the issue.
*   **High Payment Rates vs. Low Recovery:** While **64%** of organizations hit by ransomware ended up paying, the outcomes were poor:
    *   Only **52%** of victims regained access to their data after paying.
    *   **54%** still encountered system errors and corrupted data after decryption.
*   **The "Double Extortion" Risk:** Paying the ransom significantly increases the likelihood of being targeted again.
    *   **80%** of organizations that paid were hit by a second ransomware attack.
    *   **48%** of these second attacks were perpetrated by the same attackers.
    *   **68%** of these subsequent attacks involved the attackers demanding a higher ransom.

### 3. Expansion into Cloud Environments
As businesses move to the cloud, attackers are pivoting to exploit these interconnected systems.
*   **Rapid Growth:** Cloud exploitation cases grew by **95%** over the course of 2022.
*   **Increased Presence:** Cloud-conscious actors nearly tripled compared to 2021.
*   **High-Value Targets:** Managed Service Providers (MSPs) for the cloud are primary targets because a single breach in an MSP can lead to a "downstream" cascade of attacks across many connected customers.

### 4. Geopolitical Cyber Warfare (Russia-Ukraine Conflict)
The conflict in Ukraine has redefined the role of cyber operations in modern warfare.
*   **Non-State Actors:** The conflict saw unprecedented involvement from non-state actors, including hacktivists and "Big Tech" companies.
*   **The "IT Army of Ukraine":** A unique phenomenon where a volunteer army of hackers was formed to provide both defensive and offensive cyber capabilities for Ukraine.
*   **Wiper Malware & Infrastructure Attacks:** Russia has been accused of using disruptive "wiper" malware and attacking critical infrastructure, such as the Viasat satellite communications provider.

### 5. Global and Local Counter-Measures
There is an increasing movement toward international and coordinated cooperation to combat ransomware.
*   **The Counter-Ransomware Initiative (CRI):** A summit involving 36 countries and the EU to develop cooperative actions. It operates on three pillars:
    1.  **Policy:** Led by Singapore and the UK.
    2.  **Diplomacy and Capacity Building:** Led by Germany and Nigeria.
    3.  **Task Force:** Coordinated by Australia.
*   **Success Story:** The FBI's January 2023 campaign to disrupt the **Hive** ransomware group is cited as a notable success.
*   **Singapore’s Approach:** Singapore established the **Cyber Crime Response Task Force (CRTF)** in 2022 to strengthen national counter-ransomware efforts.
