This document segment from the **Singapore Cyber Landscape 2022** report outlines the strategic efforts of various Singaporean agencies (MINDEF, PDPC, and SPF) to defend against cyber threats, protect personal data, and combat scams.

Below are the key insights and facts categorized by the three main focus areas presented:

### 1. National Cyber Defence (MINDEF & DIS)
**Focus:** Protecting Critical Information Infrastructure (CII) and fostering a researcher community.

*   **CIDeX 2022:** The inaugural Critical Infrastructure Defence Exercise (CIDeX) was held in November 2022. It simulated a "nation under attack" scenario involving 16 organizations (including SingTel, SMRT, Sembcorp, and ST Engineering) to train cyber defenders to protect both IT and OT (Operational Technology) infrastructure, such as power and water plants.
*   **Vulnerability Discovery:** The Cyber Vulnerability Disclosure Programme (CVDP), launched in 2018, has seen over **2,000 members** of the public and researchers identify and remediate more than **1,000 valid vulnerabilities** in government systems.
*   **Community Engagement:** GovTech rewards the security researcher community through awards (e.g., Jaga Golden Trophy) and digital badges to encourage "white hat" hacking and proactive identification of flaws.

### 2. Data Protection and Privacy (PDPC)
**Focus:** Minimizing data breach risks and supporting SMEs.

*   **Rising Cyber Incidents:** In 2022, the Personal Data Protection Commission (PDPC) investigated over 60 data breaches, with **more than 60% categorized as cyber incidents** (primarily ransomware and phishing).
*   **Regulatory Mandate:** Under the PDPA, organizations are now legally obligated to notify the PDPC and affected customers of significant data breaches.
*   **Support for SMEs (DPE):** Recognizing that small businesses struggle with security, the PDPC and IMDA developed **Data Protection Essentials (DPE)**. This is a one-stop service providing training and security tools to help SMEs build a foundation for data protection.
*   **Case Study – SQL Injection:** The report cites an electronics retailer that suffered an SQL injection attack. The breach was mitigated because the retailer had regular backups and took swift action to disable affected systems.

### 3. The Fight Against Scams (SPF - Anti-Scam Command)
**Focus:** Reducing the prevalence of scams and increasing recovery rates.

*   **Surging Scam Numbers:** 2022 saw a significant spike in crime:
    *   **Scam cases:** 31,728 (a **33% increase** from 2021).
    *   **Cybercrime cases:** 33,669 (a **25% increase** from 2021).
*   **Financial Impact:** Scams accounted for more than **S$660.7 million** in losses in Singapore in 2022.
*   **Recovery Efforts:** The Anti-Scam Command (ASCom) successfully recovered approximately **S$146.6 million** (roughly 27%) of the amount lost by victims.
*   **Enforcement Success:** In 2022, ASCom coordinated 25 anti-scam operations, leading to the arrest or investigation of over **8,000 scammers and money mules**.
*   **International Cooperation:** The Singapore Police Force (SPF) works with agencies like the Royal Malaysia Police and Interpol. In 2022, this collaboration helped take down **13 scam syndicates**, including those specializing in job scams, impersonation, and phishing.
*   **Operational Strategy (S.C.A.M.S):** The ASCom utilizes a specific framework: **S**trengthened sense-making, **C**lose collaboration, **A**gile response, **M**aximize effective management, and **S**wift/strategic actions.
