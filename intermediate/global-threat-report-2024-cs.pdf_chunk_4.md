This document is a segment from the **CrowdStrike 2024 Global Threat Report**. It details the evolving landscape of cybercrime (eCrime), information operations (IO), and the increasing sophistication of "Big Game Hunting" (BGH) ransomware groups.

Below are the key insights and facts categorized by theme:

### 1. The Rise of Big Game Hunting (BGH) & Extortion
The report identifies BGH as the most significant eCrime threat globally, characterized by targeting high-revenue organizations.
*   **Explosive Growth in Victim Exposure:** There was a **76% increase** in the number of victims named on Dedicated Leak Sites (DLSs) in 2023, totaling 4,615 posts.
*   **Dominant Threat Actors:** Five "Spider" groups (**Bitwise, Alpha, Graceful, Recess, and Brain Spider**) accounted for **77% of all posts** across tracked adversary leak sites.
*   **Shift from Encryption to Data Theft:** Adversaries are moving away from solely using ransomware (encryption) toward "data theft and extortion." This involves stealing sensitive data to pressure victims through embarrassment or regulatory pressure (e.g., SEC disclosure rules).
*   **Targeted Data Harvesting:** Threat actors like **VICE SPIDER** are using automated scripts to search for specific keywords (e.g., *"violence," "abuse," "death"*) to find data that maximizes the potential for public embarrassment.

### 2. Economic Shifts in the eCrime Ecosystem
The "CrowdStrike eCrime Index" (ECX) shows a massive increase in the cost of criminal operations:
*   **Rising Costs:** There were massive year-over-year increases in the cost of buying criminal tools:
    *   **Stealer Cost:** +250%
    *   **Crypter Cost:** +169%
    *   **Loader Cost:** +76%
*   **Decreasing Spam:** There was a decrease in observed spam emails (-27%) and identified spam (-15%), suggesting adversaries are moving away from traditional email-based initial access in favor of more sophisticated methods.
*   **Ransom Demands:** While the *average* ransom demand appeared lower, the report notes this is likely an **outlier** caused by victims and attackers using stricter privacy measures to hide payment amounts.

### 3. Emerging Malware & Delivery Techniques
As security software improves, attackers are adopting new "enablers":
*   **macOS Vulnerability:** There is a growing trend of **macOS-specific malware** (e.g., MacOS Stealer, AMOS) designed to harvest passwords, cookies, and cryptocurrency wallets.
*   **Abuse of Legitimate Tools:** Attackers are increasingly using legitimate **Remote Monitoring and Management (RMM) tools** (like ConnectWise ScreenConnect and NetSupport Manager) to conduct attacks, making them harder to detect.
*   **New Delivery Methods:** Following patches to Microsoft’s "Mark-of-the-Web" (which hindered ISO files), attackers pivoted to **malicious OneNote files**, **malvertising**, and **SEO poisoning** (manipulating search engine results to lead users to malicious sites).
*   **Access Brokers:** The market for "initial access" is growing, with a **20% increase** in advertised access compared to 2022, particularly targeting the academic sector and U.S. entities.

### 4. Geopolitical & Information Operations (IO)
*   **AI-Driven Disinformation:** The report warns that **Generative AI** is being used in 2024 to create highly convincing disinformation for political influence.
*   **Observed Use Case:** Chinese actors were already observed using AI-generated content to target candidates in the Taiwan presidential election.
*   **Strategic Threats:** Russia and Iran are identified as likely to leverage IO against the U.S. and EU.

### 5. Law Enforcement Successes & Adversary Resilience
*   **Major Takedowns:** 2023 saw significant law enforcement wins, including the seizure of **HIVE SPIDER** infrastructure (preventing \$130 million in ransom payments) and the shutdown of **QakBot** (Mallard Spider).
*   **The "Hydra" Effect:** Despite these successes, the eCrime ecosystem remains resilient. When major infrastructures like QakBot are shut down, the void is quickly filled by competing ransomware-as-a-service (RaaS) and malware-as-a-service (MaaS) actors.
