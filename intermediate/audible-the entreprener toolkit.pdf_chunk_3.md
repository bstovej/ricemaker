### **Summary of Key Concepts**

**1. Prototyping (Building a Prototype)**
*   **Purpose:** To test the feasibility of an idea, reduce risk, and gather feedback.
*   **Functions:** A prototype acts as a tool to test economic viability, technical feasibility, and user experience. It allows you to move from a concept to a tangible object that can be physically manipulated and tested.
*   **Benefits:** 
    *   It helps identify flaws in the design or business model before significant capital is invested.
    *   It allows for "learning by doing"—seeing how a product or service works in a real-world context.
    *   It provides a basis for gathering qualitative feedback (what people feel/think) and quantitative feedback (how it performs).
*   **Key Examples from Text:** 
    *   The **Super Soaker** (using a physical prototype to test water pressure).
    *   The **Apple/iPhone** concept (using a physical prototype to test user interface and ergonomics).
    *   **The "Super Soaker" approach to prototyping:** Using simple materials (like a spray bottle) to test the mechanics of a complex idea.

**2. Market Research Methods**
*   **Quantitative Research:** Focusing on "how many" and "how much." It involves structured data, such as surveys, scales, and statistical analysis, to find measurable trends.
*   **Qualitative Research:** Focusing on "why" and "how." It involves gathering deep, descriptive insights through interviews, focus groups, and observation to understand human emotions, motivations, and behaviors.
