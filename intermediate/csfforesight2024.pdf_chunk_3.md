This document contains two distinct but related essays regarding the practice of "foresight" (the study of future trends and scenarios). The first focuses on the **institutional application of foresight in Singapore’s governance**, while the second, by Jevon Chandra, explores the **conceptual parallels between foresight and the theatrical discipline of dramaturgy.**

Here are the key insights and facts categorized by theme:

### 1. Singapore’s Institutionalized Foresight
The first section details how the Singapore government has integrated long-term thinking into its core governance model.

*   **Systemic Integration:** Foresight is not an isolated activity but is embedded across various domains, including defense, foreign policy, environment, and education. It is part of the curriculum for all milestone programs at the **Singapore Civil Service College**.
*   **Key Institutions:**
    *   **Scenario Planning Office (est. 1995):** Led the development of national scenarios.
    *   **Future Systems Directorate (MOD, 2003):** Created to develop new combat doctrines and technologies (forming the basis of the 3G Singapore Armed Forces).
    *   **Centre for Strategic Futures (CSF):** Established to uncover latent assumptions and biases.
    *   **Future Economy Council (est. 2017):** Facilitates conversations between public, private, and people sectors regarding economic transformation.
*   **The "Why" of Foresight:** For a small, vulnerable nation with no natural resources, foresight is a survival mechanism. It allows the state to "invent its own future" and navigate unpredictable global changes rather than simply reacting to them.
*   **Collaborative Governance:** The government utilizes international advisory boards (like the **Research, Innovation and Enterprise Council - RIEC**) to ensure that insights are not a monopoly of the state but include diverse, external expertise.

### 2. The Dramaturgical Analogy (Foresight as "Stagecraft")
The second essay, *Stagecraft for Tomorrow*, argues that the role of a "futurist" is remarkably similar to that of a "dramaturg" (an analyst/critic of theatrical composition).

*   **The Twin Roles (Pendamping & Penganggu):** Using Indonesian terminology, the author suggests futurists/dramaturgs occupy two simultaneous roles:
    *   **Pendamping (Companion):** An invested traveler who journeys with a process/production to support and provide resources.
    *   **Penganggu (Provocateur):** An agitator who asks "why" and "where," challenging deep-seated beliefs and pushing for change.
*   **Mediating Information:** Both disciplines act as bridges. They identify "invisible habits" and knowledge gaps (e.g., differences in cultural trauma or age-based digital literacy) and provide the necessary context to bring disparate groups to a common understanding.
*   **Creation of "Heterotopias":** Both foresight and theatre create "heterotopias"—spaces that exist in reality but feel "outside" or "different" (like a library, museum, or a stage). These spaces provide the "social technology" necessary to ask "what if" and imagine alternatives to the status quo.
*   **Beyond Utopia and Dystopia:** The author argues that effective foresight should move beyond the binary of "utopia" (escapist daydream) and "dystopia" (fear-based entertainment) to explore a "third option": a space that is simply *different*.

### 3. Key Cross-Cutting Themes
*   **Persuadability of the Future:** A central insight is that the future is not predetermined; it is "persuadable." Through strategic foresight, risks can be recast into opportunities.
*   **The Power of "Relational" Work:** Whether it is the Aceh peace treaty (using shared grief to pivot toward possibility) or the Welsh Future Generations Act (using creative media to engage youth), the success of foresight depends on its ability to connect with human emotions, social capital, and diverse stakeholders.
*   **Translation as a Tool:** For foresight to be effective, complex data/scenarios must be "translated" into actionable, legible formats—such as the CSF’s "17 Driving Forces" card deck or the use of songs and podcasts to explain legislative acts.
