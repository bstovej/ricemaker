### 1. The Evolving Global Threat Landscape
The digital landscape is becoming increasingly borderless and complex, with several emerging risks:
*   **Increasing Financial Impact:** According to the US Government Accountability Office (GAO), losses due to major cyber-attacks rose from **US$4/470 million in 2016 to US$2.6 billion in 2021**.
*   **Expanding Attack Surface:** The proliferation of IoT devices is expected to result in more than **55 billion interconnected devices by 2025**, providing more entry points for attackers.
*   **Emerging Technologies as Risks:**
    *   **Generative AI (e.g., ChatGPT):** Malicious actors are using AI to create data-stealing programs, develop dark web marketplaces, and generate convincing phishing/deepfake content.
    *   **Quantum Computing:** This poses a future threat to the encryption currently used to protect digital communications.
*   **Physical/Geopolitical Vulnerabilities:** Digital infrastructure is susceptible to non-cyber events, such as the **Tonga earthquake** (which severed a submarine cable, disconnecting the nation for a month) and **power surges** (which disrupted Microsoft Azure services in Southeast Asia).

The document highlights real-world examples of how digital disruptions impact society:

*   **MongoDB (2022):** Following US/Western sanctions against Russia and Belarus, MongoDB unilaterally decided to terminate all software and services for customers in those regions.
*   **Infrastructure Fragility:** The document notes that even "secure" environments are at risk from environmental disasters and geopolitical tensions that affect supply chains and tech availability.

### 3. Singapore’s "Ecosystem Approach" to Cybersecurity
Singapore’s strategy for a safer cyberspace relies on a tripartite collaboration between the **Government, Industry, and the Public**.

*   **The Government’s Role:**
    *   Sharpening regulations to ensure "baseline requirements" for security are built into new infrastructure.
    *   Establishing models for collaboration with the private sector to customize safety programs.
    *   Promoting international cooperation to harmonize standards and rules.
*   **The Industry’s Role:**
    *   Adopting a **"secure-by-design"** approach to product development.
    *   Balancing profit motives with the need for upstream investments in consumer safety and ecosystem resilience.
*   **The Public’s Role:**
    *   Making informed choices (e.g., using products that meet **Transaction Safety Rating (TSR)** standards).
    *   Acting as a "layer of defense" by reporting vulnerabilities.

### 4. Proactive Defense & Digital Services in Singapore
The document highlights specific tools and initiatives used by the Singapore government to manage digital risks:
*   **GovTech’s Vulnerability Discovery:**
    *   **VDP (Vulnerability Disclosure Programme):** A program allowing anyone to report security flaws in government systems.
    *   **CVDP (Crowdsourced Vulnerability Discovery Programmes):** Includes the **GBBP** (Government Bug Bounty Programme) and **VRP** (Vulnerability Rewards Programme), the latter of which involves engagement with researchers via **HackerOne**.
    *   **Success Story:** A researcher identified a vulnerability in an authentication module that could have redirected users to phishing sites; GovTech successfully patched the flaw.
*   **Digital Identity & Services:**
    *   **Singpass:** Used by over **5 million residents** to access more than 2,700 services.
    *   **LifeSG:** Integrates over 400 services to support citizens through different life stages.
*   **Training & Readiness:** **Exercise Cyber Knights 2021 (XCK21)** was conducted by the CSA to allow incident responders to hone their skills against advanced persistent threats (APTs) and ransomware using a cyber range.
