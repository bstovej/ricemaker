This document, the **2024 7th Edition of the Cybersecurity Guide for CFOs**, explores the escalating financial risks posed by the proliferation of Generative AI. The central theme is the transition into a **"post-trust future,"** where traditional methods of verifying identity and authenticity are becoming obsolete due to AI-driven deception.

Below are the key insights and facts categorized by theme:

### 1. The Escalating Financial Impact of Cybercrime
The document highlights a significant and growing trend in global and regional financial losses:
*   **Global Projections:** Global cybercrime costs are expected to grow by **15% annually**, reaching an estimated **$10.5 billion USD** by 2025.
*   **Australia/NZ Trends:** 
    *   Australian businesses lost **$224 million AUD** to payment redirection schemes in 2022.
    *   There was a **73% increase** in reported business scams in Australia last year.
    *   New Zealand saw a **66% increase** in financial losses related to scams in late 2023.
*   **Underreporting:** The document notes that actual losses are likely much higher than reported because there is no single primary reporting authority, leaving many finance leaders unsure if fraud is even being reported.

### 2. The Evolution of Business Email Compromise (BEC)
BEC remains the primary driver of large-scale corporate losses, but the tactics are evolving:
*   **Supply Chain Vulnerability:** Attackers are no longer just targeting the target company; they are infiltrating the email systems of **suppliers** to manipulate accounts payable (AP) officers into changing payment details.

*   **The "Multi-Persona" Tactic:** Scammers are using AI to orchestrate entire communication chains, where they masquerade as multiple different people (e.g., a supplier and an executive) within a single thread to build "social proof" and trust.
*   **Elimination of Language Barriers:** Historically, BEC attacks were limited by the attacker’s ability to speak the target's language or understand local business customs. LLMs (Large Language Models) have eliminated this barrier, leading to increased attack volumes in regions like Japan.

### 3. The Role of Generative AI in Fraud
The document distinguishes between the "defensive" use of AI and the "offensive" use by criminals:
*   **Malicious LLMs:** While mainstream tools like ChatGPT have safety guardrails, the dark web hosts "black-hat" alternatives like **WormGPT** and **FraudGPT**. These tools are specifically designed to create polished phishing emails, develop malware, and brainstorm attack strategies without restrictions.
*   **Synthetic Media (Deepfakes):**
    *   **Voice Cloning:** Scammers can create "pitch-perfect" voice clones using only a few seconds of audio from the internet. Real-world examples include CEOs receiving calls from "bosses" instructing fraudulent transfers.
    *   **Visual Deception:** The "next frontier" involves using AI to generate highly realistic video and faces to deceive employees during remote interactions.

### 4. Critical Vulnerabilities in Finance Leadership
A significant insight of the report is the **gap between the threat level and the organizational response**:
*   **Underestimating the Threat:** While 98% of CFOs believe cybercrime is increasing, only **8%** rank deepfakes (AI-generated media) as a major threat.
*   **Lack of Basic Controls:** Despite the high risk, many organizations are failing to use proven anti-fraud controls:
    *   Only **59%** of organizations use **segregation of duties**.
    *   Only **46%** use **verbal verifications** (call-back controls) to confirm bank details.
*   **The "Post-Trust" Reality:** The document warns that because AI can now mimic almost any digital "evidence" (voice, text, or video), traditional content-based verification is breaking down.

### Summary for CFOs
The document serves as a warning that **technological advances are outstripping regulations and corporate defenses.** To survive, finance leaders must move beyond traditional cybersecurity and develop specialized **synthetic media incident response playbooks** and more rigorous, manual verification processes that do not rely solely on digital communication.
