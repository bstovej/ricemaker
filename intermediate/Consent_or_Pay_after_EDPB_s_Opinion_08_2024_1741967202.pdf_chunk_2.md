The author questions if the EDPB is using data protection law to pursue a **"social justice policy"** (demanding free essential services) that the GDPR was never intended to address.

The document provides a sophisticated analysis of the legal weight of the EDPB’s opinions:
*   **Non-Binding but Influential:** While the EDPB’s opinions and recommendations are technically **"soft law"** (not legally binding like Regulations or Directives), they are not without legal effect.
*   **Legal Consequences:** Under EU law, national courts are bound to consider these recommendations when deciding disputes. Furthermore, these instruments can create **"legitimate expectations"** and "self-bindingness" for controllers (companies), leading to significant legal uncertainty when opinions change the "rules of the game" mid-stream.
