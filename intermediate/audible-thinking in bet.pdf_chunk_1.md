Based on the provided document segments from Annie Duke’s *Thinking in Bets*, here are the key insights and facts organized by theme.

### 1. The Nature of Decision-Making: "Life is Poker, Not Chess"
*   **Uncertainty is Inherent:** Unlike chess, where all information is visible on the board, life is more like poker. Decisions are made with incomplete information, meaning you cannot always predict the outcome with certainty.
*   **Decisions as Bets:** Every decision—from minor daily choices (staying up late) to major life changes (moving to a new city)—is essentially a "bet" on a specific future. We are betting that a certain action will lead to a more favorable future.
*   **The Role of Cognitive Bias:** The text uses the **Müller-Lyer Illusion** (where lines of equal length appear different) to demonstrate that our brains often process reality incorrectly. While we cannot "unsee" these biological illusions, we can use "practical work-arounds" (like using a ruler) to check our perceptions against reality.

### 2. The Learning Loop: Distinguishing Skill from Luck
The core of becoming an expert lies in the ability to analyze outcomes to improve future decisions.
*   **The Skill vs. Luck Sorting:** When an outcome occurs, we face a critical second decision: *Why did this happen?* We must decide if the result was driven by **skill** (things within our control) or **luck** (things outside our control).
*   **The Importance of Correct Attribution:** 
    *   If you attribute a good result to **skill**, you can learn from it and repeat it.
    *   If you attribute a bad result to **luck**, you can avoid the error in the future.
    *   Mistakenly attributing luck to skill (or vice versa) prevents the formation of an effective "learning loop."
*   **Expertise vs. Experience:** Simply having experience is not enough to become an expert. Expertise requires the ability to identify what a specific outcome can teach you and how to update your beliefs based on that data.

### 3. Perspective and the "Ticker Watcher" Trap
*   **The Ticker Watcher Metaphor:** The text compares people to investors who obsessively watch "tickers" (real-time stock price movements). We tend to "zoom in" on the daily, minute-by-minute fluctuations of our lives, which can look frightening and volatile.
*   **Long-term vs. Short-term:** Using Berkshire Hathaway’s stock performance as an example, the text shows that while short-term views (like the 2008 banking crisis) look disastrous, the long-term trajectory is upward. 
*   **The Goal for Happiness:** The author suggests we should view happiness not as a moment-by-moment metric, but as a "long-term stock holding." We should strive for a sustained upward trend rather than obsessing over daily fluctuations.

### 4. Practical Strategy: Scenario Planning
*   **Reconnaissance on the Future:** To make better decisions, one must perform "reconnaissance" by imagining the range of possible futures that could result from a decision.
*   **Scenario Planning:** This involves two steps:
    1.  **Identify Possibilities:** List the various potential outcomes (Scenario Planning).
    2.  **Assign Probabilities:** After identifying the possibilities, estimate the likelihood of each occurring.
*   **Mental Time Travel:** We can use "mental time travel"—leveraging the perspectives of our past and future selves—to gain the necessary distance to evaluate these scenarios objectively.
