### **Key Insights**

**1. The Shift to a "Borderless" Threat Landscape**
The digital domain is fundamentally different from physical domains (air, sea, land) because it is borderless. This allows threats to originate from anywhere, making the security of the digital ecosystem highly vulnerable to global geopolitical shifts and sophisticated, persistent attackers.

**2. The Necessity of an "Ecosystem Approach"**
Cybersecurity cannot be achieved by the government alone. A resilient digital future requires a three-pillar collaborative strategy involving:
*   **Government:** Implementing regulations, establishing "baseline requirements" for infrastructure, and fostering international cooperation.
*   **Industry:** Adopting "secure-by-design" principles and balancing profit with long-term consumer safety.
*   **The Public:** Staying vigilant, using security ratings (like MHA’s TSR), and actively participating in vulnerability reporting.

**3. Emerging Technology as a Double-Edged Sword**
While technology drives economic growth, it simultaneously expands the "attack surface":
*   **Generative AI (e.g., ChatGPT):** Can be exploited by low-skill actors to create sophisticated phishing, malware, and deepfakes.
*   **IoT Expansion:** The proliferation of connected devices (estimated at over 55 billion by 2025) creates more entry points for attackers.
*   **Quantum Computing:** Represents a future existential threat to current encryption standards, potentially compromising the confidentiality of communications.

**4. Proactive Vulnerability Management**
The document highlights a shift toward "crowdsourced" security. By using programs like GovTech’s **Vulnerability Disclosure Programme (VDP)**, the government leverages the global research community to identify and patch flaws before they can be exploited by malicious actors.

**Global Cyber Incident Data:**

*   **MongoDB (2022):** The company unilaterally terminated services in Russia and Belarus following US/Western sanctions.
