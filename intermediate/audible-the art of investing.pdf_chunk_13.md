### 3. Core Investment Philosophies & Frameworks
The document outlines several distinct schools of thought in the investment world:
*   **Value/Special Situations:** Looking for undervalued assets, distressed companies, or companies undergoing structural changes (e.s., spinoffs).
*   **Quantitative/Systematic:** Using algorithms and historical data to find patterns and execute trades without emotional bias (Bragg/Bragg-style).
*   **Fundamental/Deep Value:** Analyzing the intrinsic health of a business (cash flow, debt, market position) to find discrepancies between price and value.

The text provides a roadmap for disciplined investing through several key "rules":
*   **Risk Management:** The importance of maintaining liquidity (cash reserves) and avoiding excessive leverage.
*   **Psychological Discipline:** The need to manage emotions (fear and greed) and the value of having a systematic "rule-based" approach to minimize human error.
*   **The Importance of Research:** 
    *   **Fundamental Analysis:** Looking at balance sheets and cash flows.
    *   **Macro Analysis:** Understanding interest rates and economic cycles.
    *   **Technical Analysis:** Using price action and trends.
*   **Diversification vs. Concentration:** The tension between spreading risk (to protect capital) and concentrating bets (to maximize returns).
