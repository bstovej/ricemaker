Based on the document segments provided from the **Singapore Cyber Landscape 2022**, here are the key insights and facts organized by theme:

### 1. Emerging Cyber Threats and Attack Trends
*   **Double Extortion Ransomware:** A growing trend where threat actors do not just encrypt data but also exfiltrate (steal) it. This puts extra pressure on victims to pay, as data exposure remains a threat even if backups are available.
*   **Business Email Compromise (BEC):** Attackers impersonate or take control of legitimate email accounts to defraud companies. The document notes approximately 50 reported cases of this in Singapore in 2022.
*   **Exploitation of Unpatched Vulnerabilities:** A significant trend involves attackers targeting unpatched software, specifically Microsoft Exchange Servers, to deploy backdoors and malware.
*   **Diversified Threats:** Beyond ransomware, the landscape includes crypto-miners, data breaches, and cyber espionage (often politically or financially motivated).

### 2. Economic Impact and Financial Insights
*   **Cost of Remediation vs. Prevention:** The cost of remediating a cyber-attack can be nearly **triple** the cost of taking preventive measures. 
*   **The Value of Prevention:** 
    *   An estimated **20%** of total costs are spent on preventing attacks.
    *   The average cost savings from preventing an attack is approximately **US$682,650.80**.
*   **Financial Motivation:** Most incidents in the private sector are characterized as financially motivated and opportunistic.

### 3. Best Practices for Organizations and SMEs
*   **Top Three Defensive Actions:**
    1.  **Set up Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA):** To prevent access via stolen or leaked credentials.
    2.  **Establish an Effective Patch Management Regime:** To close known vulnerabilities.
    3.  **Back up Data Regularly:** To ensure quick recovery from ransomware.
*   **Strategic Approach (Prevent, Detect, Respond):**
    *   **Prevent:** Minimize Internet exposure, limit user privileges, and use anti-virus software.
    *   **Detect:** Conduct regular security posture assessments and set up alerts for suspicious activity.
    *   **Respond:** Develop incident response plans and "playbooks," and conduct regular cyber exercises.
*   **Resources for SMEs:** SMEs can leverage Singapore’s **Cyber Security Agency (CSA)** for SME owner toolkits and grant-supported programs to develop cybersecurity health plans.

### 4. Personal Cybersecurity (Home Network Security)
*   **Router Security:** For personal hardware, the document suggests looking for **CLS (Cyber Landscape Security) Level 4** ratings, which imply the manufacturer is obligated to update software and fix flaws.
*   **Essential Home Habits:**
    *   Use strong, unique passwords.
    *   Enable automatic software updates.
    *   Disable remote management features on routers unless absolutely necessary.

### 5. Strategic Outlook: "Collective Efficacy"
*   **The Problem of Risk Shifting:** There is a growing concern that businesses are using "Terms and Conditions" to shift the burden of cyber risk onto the consumer (e.g., stating the customer is responsible for spotting scams).
*   **The Concept of Collective Efficacy:** Moving beyond "personal efficacy" (where the individual is solely responsible), the document advocates for a **collective model**. 
    *   **Example:** Just as neighborhood groups work together to fight crime, cybersecurity should be a shared responsibility where families (e.g., younger members helping seniors) and businesses (e.g., redesigning processes to obstruct scammers) actively support one another.
*   **Future Challenges:** The landscape is being shaped by geopolitical tensions (e.g., Russia-Ukraine conflict), the rise of AI, and the transition to a more digitalized post-pandemic world.
