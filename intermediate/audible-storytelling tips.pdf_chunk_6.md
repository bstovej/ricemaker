Based on the text provided, here is a summary of the different techniques for introducing characters in literature:

### **1. Character in Action (Showing through Behavior)**
This method introduces a character by depicting them engaged in a specific activity or movement. 
*   **Example from text:** The opening of *The Sea* (implied), where the character is observed through their movements and the environment.
*   **Effect:** It allows the reader to see the character's personality through their actions rather than through description.

### **2. Character through Direct Description (Appearance/Setting)**
This involves providing the reader with details about the character’s physical appearance or the immediate environment they inhabit.
*   **Example from text:** The description of the character in *The Sea*, focusing on their physical sensations and the atmosphere.
*   **Effect:** It creates a visual image and establishes the mood or tone of the character's world.

### **3. Character through Dialogue or Monologue (Direct Speech)**
The character is introduced through the words they speak, which can reveal their personality, social status, or temperament.
*   **Example from text:** The use of "Direct Speech" to convey information.
*   **Effect:** It gives the character an immediate "voice" and allows for characterization through tone and vocabulary.

### **4. Character through Interaction (Dialogue with Others)**
The character is revealed through their conversations and relationships with other characters.
*   **Example from text:** The use of "Dialogue" between characters to show dynamics.
*   **Effect:** This reveals how the character relates to others and their place within a social hierarchy.

### **5. Character through External Observation (The "Observer" Method)**
A character is introduced via the eyes of another character or a narrator observing them.
*   **Example from text:** The "Character as Observer" technique.
*   **Effect:** This creates a sense of mystery or provides a specific perspective on the character's traits.

### **6. Character through Indirect Perspective (The "Reported" Method)**
The character is introduced through the thoughts, opinions, or descriptions provided by other characters or a narrator.
*   **Example from text:** The "Character as Reported" technique.
*   **Effect:** This can create a sense of reputation or mystery, as the reader only knows what is being said about them.
