### **Lecture 11: Products Liability Today**

#### **Key Facts**
* **The Standard for Casual Sellers:** Unlike professional manufacturers, casual sellers are judged by a **negligence standard** rather than strict liability.
* **Product Seller vs. Service Provider:** Strict liability applies to product sellers (e.g., a manufacturer of a defective pipe). However, it does not apply to service providers (e.g., a plumber installing the pipe), who are subject only to negligence claims.
* **The Economic Loss Rule:** Strict liability applies to harm caused by a product to *other* things (people or surrounding property), but it **does not** apply to harm to the product itself (e.g., a defective faucet that simply fails to work is a contract issue, not a tort issue).
* **The Three Types of Defects:**
    1. **Manufacturing Defects:** When a product departs from its intended design, regardless of the care taken during production. These trigger strict liability.
    2. **Design Defects:** When foreseeable risks could have been avoided by adopting a "reasonable alternative design."
    3. **Failure to Warn:** When a manufacturer fails to provide adequate instructions or warnings about potential dangers.

#### **Key Insights**
* **Rationale for the "Casual Seller" Exception:** The law does not hold casual sellers to a standard of strict liability because they lack the "loss-spreading" ability of corporations and do not have the same control over the manufacturing process.
* **The Tension in Design Defect Law:** There is an ongoing debate regarding the "risk-utility" balance—deciding when the cost/burden of a safer design outweighs the benefit of the risk reduction.

### **Lecture 12: Punitive Damages and Legal Limitations**

#### **Key Facts**
* **Purposes of Punitive Damages:** Beyond compensation, punitive damages serve to **deter** future misconduct and **punish** the defendant for particularly egregious behavior.
* **Legislative/Judicial Limits on Damages:**
    * **Multipliers:** Courts often limit punitive damages to a specific ratio relative to compensatory damages (e.g., a 1:1 or 1:4 ratio).
    * **Caps:** Some jurisdictions implement statutory caps on the total amount of damages a plaintiff can recover.
* **The Role of "Remedial" vs. "Punitive":** Compensatory damages aim to make the plaintiff "whole" (returning them to their pre-injury state), whereas punitive damages focus on the defendant's culpability.

#### **Key Insights**
* **The Deterrence Function:** Punitive damages are a tool of social policy used to discourage behavior that is "willful, wanton, or reckless," even if the behavior doesn't result in significant economic loss.
* **The Constitutional/Legal Tension:** There is a constant struggle between the "right to be made whole" for victims and the "due process" rights of defendants to avoid "excessive" or arbitrary punishments.
