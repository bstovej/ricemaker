### **2. Technical Rules for Dialogue & Formatting**
The document provides specific, technical instructions for handling dialogue and prose:

**Dialogue Punctuation & Structure:**
*   **Use of Quotation Marks:** Dialogue should be enclosed in quotation marks.
*   **Dialogue Tags:** When using tags (e.g., *he said*), the tag should be integrated into the flow.
*   **Paragraph Breaks:** A new paragraph should be used whenever a new character speaks to avoid confusion.

**Dialogue Tags & Mechanics:**
*   **Attribution:** Use dialogue tags to identify the speaker, especially when multiple characters are present.
*   **Tag Placement:** Tags can be placed at the beginning, middle, or end of a piece of dialogue.
*   **Punctuation with Tags:**
    *   If the tag follows the dialogue, use a comma inside the quotation marks (e.g., *"Hello," he said.*).
    *   If the tag is a separate sentence, use a period (e.g., *"Hello." He waved.*).
*   **Avoiding Overuse:** While tags are necessary for clarity, excessive or repetitive use of "he said/she said" can clutter the prose.

### **3. Narrative Flow and Clarity**
*   **Clarity of Speaker:** The primary goal of dialogue formatting is to ensure the reader always knows who is speaking without having to guess.
*   **Managing Multiple Characters:** In scenes with more than two people, frequent paragraph breaks and clear attribution are essential to prevent reader confusion.
*   **Maintaining Rhythm:** Good writing balances the "action" (what characters do) with "dialogue" (what characters say) to create a natural narrative rhythm.
