This document segment reviews advanced methodologies in research, specifically focusing on experimental designs, the distinction between true and quasi-experiments, and survey design principles.

Here are the key insights and facts, organized by topic:

---

## 🔬 I. Experimental Designs (Controls and Groups)

### 1. Core Design Types:
*   **Posttest-Only Design:** The measurement of the dependent variable occurs *after* the treatment.
*   **2-Group Pretest-Posttest Design:** Measures the dependent variable **before** (pretest) and **after** (posttest) the treatment for both the control and treatment groups.
    *   **Advantage:** Allows comparison of scores *within* a subject (before vs. after) and checks if the initial groups were equal.
    *   **Caution:** The pretest itself can cause "testing effects," which can lower the study's internal validity.
*   **Solomon 4-Group Design:** The most robust design, featuring two control groups and two treatment groups. Only one group in each pair receives a pretest.
    *   **Advantage:** Maximizes control and allows researchers to specifically test for testing or reactivity effects.

### 2. Key Experimental Challenges:
*   **Internal Validity Threat:** Even with random assignment, systematic differences between groups due to random chance can mean the treatment had no true effect.
*   **Hawthorne Effects:** Changes in behavior due to the knowledge that one is being observed or studied (a type of reactivity effect).

## ⚖️ II. True vs. Quasi-Experiments (Validity and Scope)

### 1. Defining True Experiments:
*   **Criteria:** A true experiment requires four elements: **Control, Manipulation, Random Selection, and Random Assignment.**
*   **Validity:** Because the researcher controls and manipulates variables and uses random assignment, a true experiment is the standard for establishing **causality** (cause and effect).

### 2. Defining Quasi-Experiments:
*   **Nature:** Quasi-experiments meet *some* but not all of the criteria for a true experiment.
*   **Common Scenario:** Researchers often use quasi-experiments when random selection or random assignment is not possible (e.g., studying economic policies between countries, or studying fixed groups like students in different sections).
*   **Validity Limitation:** Because true randomness or full control is missing, the findings are interpreted with **caution**. Differences observed may be due to factors other than the independent variable.

### 3. Specialized Quasi-Designs:
*   **Single-Group Pretest-Posttest:** Used when a control group is impractical. Often used as **pilot studies** to test if a treatment is even worth implementing before a full, resource-intensive study.
*   **Nonequivalent Groups Design:** Uses two pre-existing, nonrandom groups (e.g., two organizations of the same size) as the control and treatment groups.
*   **Interrupted Time Series Designs:** Measures the dependent variable multiple times both **before and after** an intervention to help reduce the impact of maturation effects (natural changes over time).

## 📊 III. Survey Design and Execution

### 1. Goals and Function:
*   **Primary Goal:** To identify the characteristics (attitudes, beliefs, behaviors, preferences) of a large population by asking structured questions.
*   **Data Type:** Tends to produce highly **quantitative data** (measurable numbers).

### 2. Types of Data Collection:
*   **Surveys (Classic):** Structured questionnaires administered to a large sample, ideal for quantitative comparison.
*   **Interviews:** Best used for smaller groups; allows the interviewer to significantly adapt questions based on responses.
*   **Focus Groups:** Small groups are brought together to answer questions, responding both to the interviewer and to each other.

### 3. Administration Logistics and Efficiency:
| Method | Pros | Cons |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **In-Person** | Highest response rates (due to rapport). | Highest cost (time, payment). |
| **Online/Email** | Low cost; allows for great anonymity. | Frequently ignored or forgotten. |
| **General** | (Structured Questioning) | (Use logic to guide respondents and maintain standardization.) |

### 4. Key Technical Points:
*   **Sampling:** Effort is required to minimize bias and ensure representative data.
*   **Logic:** The structure of the survey must be logical to prevent confusion or misunderstanding.
*   **Best Practice:** The questionnaire should be designed to gather precise, actionable data, avoiding ambiguous or loaded questions.
