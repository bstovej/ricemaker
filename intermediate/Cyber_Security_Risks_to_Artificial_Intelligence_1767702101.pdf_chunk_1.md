This document is a formal risk assessment report titled **"Cyber Security Risks to Artificial Intelligence,"** produced by Grant Thornton UK LLP and Manchester Metropolitan University, commissioned by the UK Department for Science, Innovation, and Technology.

Below are the key insights and facts extracted from the provided text.

### **1. Core Objective & Scope**
*   **Primary Goal:** To identify, map, and assess vulnerabilities across the entire **AI lifecycle**.
*   **Key Distinction:** The report explicitly differentiates between **traditional software vulnerabilities** and **vulnerables specific to AI**.
*   **Analytical Focus:** The assessment evaluates the potential for **exploitation** (how an attack happens) and the **impact** (the resulting damage) of these vulnerabilities.

### **2. The AI Lifecycle Framework**
The report breaks down AI security into four critical, interconnected phases. Security must be addressed at every stage to be effective:
*   **Design:** Focuses on problem definition, data preparation, algorithm selection, and prototyping.
*   **Development:** Involves data pre-processing, model training, evaluation, and optimization.
*   **Deployment:** Focuses on integration into operational environments, scalability, and monitoring for "performance drift."
*   **Maintenance:** Involves ongoing retraining, bug fixing, updates, and ensuring long-term security and compliance.

### **3. Key Research Findings**
*   **The "Knowledge Gap" in Literature:** The authors found that while many studies exist, most are fragmented. They tend to focus on specific phases (e.g., only data collection or only model development) rather than providing a **comprehensive evaluation of the entire lifecycle**.
*   **Case Study Evidence:** The researchers identified **23 case studies**—comprising both real-world cyber-attacks and theoretical "proof of concept" attacks—to demonstrate how AI vulnerabilities are exploited.
*   **The Complexity of Rapid Adoption:** The rapid integration of AI is outpacing traditional cybersecurity practices, creating a need for new, holistic security frameworks.

### **4. Market Readiness & Industry Insights**
Through interviews with clients in the **Insurance, Banking, and Media** sectors, the report identified two distinct types of organizational risk profiles:
1.  **The Unaware:** Organizations that are currently unaware of how AI is being used within their operations and, consequently, the risks involved.
2.  **The Under-equipped:** Organizations that recognize the risks but **lack the internal expertise** to assess and manage them effectively.

### **5. Methodology & Rigor**
*   **Dual-Stream Review:** The study used two complementary literature streams:
    *   **LS1:** Government and industry reports (to see real-world applications).
    *   **LS2:** Peer-reviewed academic publications (to provide conceptual models).
*   **PRISMA Method:** The researchers used the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) method to ensure a transparent, unbiased, and systematic selection of data.
*   **Temporal Cut-off:** A cut-off date of **February 10, 2024**, was used to ensure the findings reflected the most current technological landscape.

### **6. Global Landscape Context**
The report notes that AI security is a global priority, highlighting significant regulatory and research efforts from:
*   **Governments/Bodies:** EU (ENISA), USA (NIST, US AI Safety Institute), China, Japan, and South Korea.
*   **Industry Leaders:** Major players such as Anthropic, AWS, Google DeepMind, Meta, Microsoft, NVIDIA, and OpenAI are all actively contributing to the AI safety and risk landscape.
