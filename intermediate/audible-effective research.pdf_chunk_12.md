## 📊 I. Standardizing Variables: Z-Scores

The z-score is a crucial method for standardizing data, making it comparable even when variables are measured in different units.

**Key Facts:**
*   **Definition:** A z-score measures the distance of a single data point from the mean, expressed in units of standard deviation.
*   **Calculation:** $z = \frac{\text{Score} - \text{Mean}}{\text{Standard Deviation}}$
*   **Interpretation:**
    *   $z=0$: The case value is exactly the average (mean).
    *   $z=1.5$: The case is 1.5 standard deviations above the mean.
*   **Purpose:** To standardize data, allowing comparison across variables with different scales.

## II. Core Concepts in Inferential Statistics

### A. Hypothesis Testing
*   **Purpose:** To determine if observed patterns in a sample data are statistically significant enough to generalize to a larger population.
*   **Process:** Involves testing the null hypothesis against an alternative hypothesis.

### B. Relationship Between Variables
*   **Correlation:** Measures the strength and direction of a linear relationship between two variables.
*   **Causation:** A strong, well-established relationship where one variable *causes* a change in another. *Correlation does not imply causation.*

### C. Correlation vs. Causation
*   This is a critical distinction. Even if two variables are highly correlated, a third, unseen variable might be influencing both (a confounding variable).

***

## III. Advanced Analysis and Interpretation

### A. Types of Relationships (Advanced)
*   **Linear Relationship:** Data points fall roughly along a straight line.
*   **Curvilinear Relationship:** The relationship follows a curve (e.g., diminishing returns).

### B. Causality Testing
*   Requires rigorous experimental design (like Randomized Controlled Trials) to minimize bias and isolate true causal factors.

***

## IV. Focus on Sampling and Generalization

### A. Sampling Methods
*   **Random Sampling:** Every member of the population has an equal chance of being selected; this is the ideal method for generalization.
*   **Bias:** A systematic error in the data collection process that skews the results away from the truth.

### B. Statistical Significance
*   Indicates the probability that the observed result happened by chance. Low probability = high significance.
