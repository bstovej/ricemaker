This document is a Goldman Sachs Global Investment Research report (dated March 9, 2026) focused on the transformative impact of "agentic AI" on the software industry and the broader global macroeconomic landscape.

Below are the key insights and facts categorized by theme.

### 1. The Central Debate: "Will AI Eat Software?"
The report examines whether the rise of agentic AI (AI that can act as an agent to perform tasks) will destroy the software sector or expand it.

*   **The Consensus:** The industry experts interviewed (Gabriela Borges, Rick Sherlund, and Sanjay Poonen) generally agree that **AI will not "eat" software** in terms of replacing the category, but it will fundamentally re-architect it.
*   **The "Expansion" Argument (Borges):** AI is essentially "software" (code designed to perform tasks). Therefore, AI expands the Total Addressable Market (TAM). The risk isn't the disappearance of software, but the **disintermediation of legacy firms**—where new AI-native entrants capture new opportunities while incumbents are left managing shrinking "systems of record."
*   **The "Re-architecture" Argument (Sherlund/Poonen):** The industry is undergoing a "rebirth." Success requires moving away from simply "bolting on" AI to a fundamental re-architecture around Large Language Models (LLMs) and agents. As Poonen notes, companies must "surf this tsunami, or it will demolish you."
*   **The Role of "Systems of Record":** A key defensive moat for legacy software is being the "system of record" (e.g., ERP, CRM). Because AI requires high-quality, proprietary, and real-time data to be effective, it is more economically efficient for AI agents to leverage existing systems of record rather than try to replace them.

### 2. Strategic Imperatives for Software Incumbents
To survive the AI transition, legacy software firms must execute a three-fold playbook:
1.  **Minimize Technical Debt:** Ensure all acquisitions and data flows are centralized and clean.
2.  **Be a "Fast Follower":** Rather than trying to invent every new technology, firms should use their capital to acquire or integrate proven technologies from the VC ecosystem.
3.  **Revolutionize Pricing Models:** Shift from "per-seat" (per user) models to "outcome-based" models. An example provided is **Salesforce’s "Agentic ELAs" (AELAs)**, which allow customers to pay for AI-driven outcomes rather than just user licenses.

### 3. Macroeconomic Outlook (Global Snapshot)
The report provides a "Top of Mind" view of global economic drivers:

*   **United States:** Focus is on risks from the **US-Iran conflict** (oil disruptions/inflation), the impact of new tariffs, and a potentially weakening labor market.
*   **Japan:** GS has raised its FY26 core CPI forecast to 2.1% due to energy prices. The BoJ is expected to hike rates in July 2026.
*   **Europe:** Growth forecasts for the Eurozone and UK have been lowered due to higher energy prices resulting from the US-Iran conflict.
*   **Emerging Markets (EM):**
    *   The US-Iran conflict is a major risk: A 10% rise in oil prices would hurt importers (Turkey, Asia) but benefit exporters (Brazil, Russia).
    *   **China:** Real GDP growth is expected to fall to 4.8% in 2026.

### 4. Investment Implications & Risks
*   **Strategy:** The overarching recommendation for investors is **selectivity**. The report warns against "binary bets" (betting on either total collapse or total survival).
*   **Target Sectors:** Investors should look for firms with "complex workflows" that are hard to disrupt, specifically in **Vertical Software, Data Infrastructure, and Physical-to-Digital firms.**
*   **Portfolio Management:** To manage AI-driven volatility, GS suggests a potential rotation from "capital-light" sectors (Technology) to "capital-heavy" sectors (Telecoms, Industrials, Utilities) that are less susceptible to AI disruption.
*   **Credit Risk:** While software-exposed loans may see some impairment, a total collapse in the credit default cycle is considered "unlikely" given the current macro environment.
