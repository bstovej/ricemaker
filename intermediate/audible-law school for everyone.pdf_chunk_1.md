### **1. The American Legal System: Core Principles**
*   **Foundation of Law:** The U.S. legal system is built on the idea that power should be derived from the people rather than a monarch. It is characterized by the principle that "the law" is a tool to ensure justice and check the power of the government.
*   **The Common Law Tradition:** The system relies heavily on **precedent** (the idea that previous judicial decisions should guide current ones). This is known as the "power of precedent," where lawyers and judges look at past rulings to determine the outcome of current cases.
*   **Adversarial System:** The U.S. uses an **adversarial model**, where two opposing parties (the prosecution/plaintiff and the defense) present their versions of the facts and legal arguments before an impartial judge or jury.
*   **Rule of Law and Precedent:** A central theme is that the law is applied consistently. Courts use "precedent" to ensure stability and predictability, though they can distinguish new cases from old ones to allow for legal evolution.

### **2. The Hierarchy of the Legal System**
*   **Legislative Power:** Laws are created by legislatures (statutes).
*   **Judicial Power:** Courts interpret these laws and apply them to specific facts.
*   **The Federal vs. State Divide:** The system operates on two levels. The **Federal system** handles matters of national importance (treaties, interstate commerce, constitutional issues), while **State systems** handle much of the day-to-day law (property, family, much of criminal law).

### **3. Key Legal Terminology & Concepts (from the text)**
*   **Precedent:** A prior judicial decision that serves as an authority for deciding similar issues in future cases.
*   **Stare Decisis:** The legal principle of determining points in litigation according to precedent.
*   **Civil vs. Criminal Law:** 
    *   *Civil:* Disputes between individuals or organizations (e.g., breach of contract).
    *   *Criminal:* Actions against the state/society (e.g., theft, assault).
*   **Plaintiff/Prosecution:** The party bringing the legal action or charges.
*   **Defendant:** The party being sued or accused of a crime.
