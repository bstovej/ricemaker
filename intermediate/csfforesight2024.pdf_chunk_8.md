### 2. Sociological Essay: "Against Ageism"
This section argues that our current linguistic and social frameworks for aging are discriminatory and need to be redefined.
*   **Key Insights on Ageism:**
    *   **The Burden of "Success":** Terms like "active," "productive," or "successful" aging are criticized as "normative." They imply that an individual's value is tied to their economic contribution and that those who experience natural physical decline are a "burden" to society.
    *   **The Medicalization of Aging:** The author argues that treating natural processes (wrinkles, hair loss) as "problems to be fixed" perpetuates fear of mortality.
    *   **The Shift in Narrative:** Historically, aging was associated with heroism and kinship; modern narratives have shifted toward themes of illness, death, and "demographic timebombs."
*   **Biological vs. Chronological Age (BA vs. CA):**
    *   **Chronological Age (CA):** The unchangeable number of years lived.
    *   **Biological Age (BA):** A more accurate measure of physical/cognitive decline based on cellular and molecular biomarkers.
    *   **Primary Biomarkers:** The text identifies **Telomere Length** (protective caps on chromosomes) and **Epigenetic Clocks** (DNA methylation patterns) as the most promising methods for measuring BA.
    *   **Proposed Use Case:** The author suggests using BA instead of CA to determine eligibility for sensitive roles (e.g., pilots, air traffic controllers, or elected officials), allowing those who are biologically fit to continue working despite their chronological age.
*   **Key Demographic Facts:**
    *   In 1800, global life expectancy was no higher than 40 years.
    *   In heavily indebted poor countries, life expectancy rose from 41 years (1960) to 62 years (2021).
    *   2019 was the first year in history where the population of people over 65 exceeded the population of children under five.
