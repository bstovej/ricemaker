This document is a segment of the **AAAI 2025 Presidential Panel on the Future of AI Research**. It outlines the current state, historical context, and future challenges of AI research, specifically focusing on the domains of **AI Reasoning** and **AI Factuality & Trustworthiness**.

Below are the key insights and facts extracted from the text.

### 1. High-Level Research Trends
*   **Shift in Research Focus:** AI research is moving from purely algorithmic/software development toward "socio-technical" study. This requires interdisciplinary collaboration with psychologists, sociologists, philosophers, and economists.
*   **The Rise of "Plausible" vs. "Sound" Reasoning:** There is a significant shift from traditional **sound reasoning** (which provides logical guarantees and correctness) to **plausible reasoning** (pattern-based, emergent behaviors seen in Large Language Models/LLMs that lack formal guarantees).
*   **The Hardware-Software Link:** AI research is increasingly inseparable from hardware (specifically GPUs), leading to a new era of "AI architecture co-creation."
*   **Corporate vs. Academia:** A growing trend shows researchers moving from academia to corporate environments because corporations possess the necessary large-scale hardware and resources.

### 2. AI Reasoning: Key Insights
*   **The Core Challenge:** The primary goal in modern reasoning research is to bring **rigorous guarantees** (a priori or post hoc) back to the "plausible" patterns produced by LLMs. This is often referred to as **"Neuro-symbolic AI"** (integrating neural networks with symbolic logic).
*   **Applications of Formal Reasoning:** While less "in vogue" than generative AI, formal reasoning remains critical for high-stakes tasks like verifying computer hardware/software, protein design, and ensuring the robustness of neural networks against attacks.
*   **Community Consensus:**
    *   The AI community highly values the integration of **learning and reasoning** (76.9% importance rating).
    *   There is a strong emphasis on **explainability and verifiability** (71.7% importance rating).
    *   A majority of researchers believe that at least 50% of symbolic AI techniques are required to achieve human-level reasoning.

### 3. AI Factuality & Trustworthiness: Key Insights
*   **Defining Terms:**
    *   **Factuality:** The ability of a system to refrain from outputting false statements (avoiding "hallucinations").
    *   **Trustworthiness:** A broader metric that includes understandability, robustness, and the incorporation of human values.
*   **Current Strategies for Factuality:**
    *   **Fine-tuning:** Training models on specific domain data (though the cost of human feedback/RLHF is a major bottleneck).
    *   **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG):** Using external, relevant documents to ground the AI's answers.
    *   **Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** Breaking complex questions into smaller, manageable logical steps.
*   **The "Pessimism" Factor:** Despite significant progress, the report notes that most scientists are **pessimistic** that the problem of AI factuality will be solved in the near future.
*   **Risks of RAG:** While RAG improves accuracy, it is only as good as its source material; if the retrieved data (e.g., from the open web) contains satire or errors, the AI will incorporate them.

### 4. Report Methodology & Context
*   **Scope of Study:** Conducted between Summer 2024 and Spring 2025 by a panel of 24 experienced researchers.
*   **Community Survey Data:**
    *   **Respondents:** 475 participants (approx. 20% students).
    *   **Affiliation:** 67% Academia, 19% Corporate.
    *   **Geography:** North America (53%), Asia (20%), Europe (19%).
    *   **Interdisciplinary Interest:** 95% of respondents expressed interest in multi-disciplinary research.
