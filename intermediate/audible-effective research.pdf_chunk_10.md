## 🔬 I. Data Collection Methods: Interviews and Observation

The text defines three distinct approaches to conducting interviews and two primary modes of observation, each with specific strengths and weaknesses.

### A. Interview Styles
| Style | Key Characteristic | Process/Control | Best Used When... |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Structured** | Functional equivalent of a survey. | Fixed list of questions; everyone is asked the same things. | The interviewer’s role is minimal; efficient data collection is needed. |
| **Unstructured** | Entirely open-ended; narrative-driven. | No predetermined list; the subject controls the conversation. | You need to hear a person’s personal narrative or story. |
| **Semi-structured** | Combination of structured and unstructured. | Core list of topics/themes exists, but the interviewer has the freedom to tailor questions, follow up, and deviate. | It is the most typical/common method, balancing focus with flexibility. |

### B. Observation
Observation involves studying behavior in the **field** (the natural environment), not in an artificial setting (like a laboratory).

*   **Direct Observation:** Observing the subject's actual behavior in real-time.
*   **Indirect Observation:** Analyzing artifacts, objects, or evidence left behind by the subjects.
*   **Participant Observation (Ethnography):** The researcher participates *to some degree* in the community being studied. The goal is to understand the culture from the insiders' perspective.

#### 💡 Key Insights: Strengths and Limitations of Observation
*   **Strength (Low Bias):** Allows the researcher to assess actual behavior without the subject's perspective interfering (less risk of people lying or forgetting).
*   **Weakness (Lack of Context):** The researcher may lack insight into the subject's motivations or justifications for their actions, risking assumption-making.
*   **Best Practice:** Observation is highly effective when **combined with interviews** to mitigate the risk of assuming motivations.

## 🗺️ II. Types of Research (Based on Purpose)

The methods are often categorized by their overarching goal:

*   **Basic Research:** Focuses on advancing general knowledge and explaining phenomena out of intrinsic academic interest. The goal is abstract.
*   **Applied Research:** Focuses on solving specific, practical, real-world problems for a company, community, or personal life.
*   **Market/Product Research:** Specifically designed to determine the preferences and interests of potential consumers to tailor products and services.
*   **Evaluation Research:** Used for assessing the **need for, design, progress, and outcomes** of a specific program or action.
    *   **Normative Rationale:** Results often include recommendations on whether an action should be continued, terminated, or initiated.
    *   **Types:**
        1.  **Needs Assessment:** Done *before* a program starts; diagnoses deficiencies or desired changes.
        2.  **Formative Assessment:** Done *during* an ongoing program; provides feedback to improve implementation/structure.
        3.  **Summative Assessment:** Done *after* a program; assesses overall impact and effectiveness (giving the program a "grade").
*   **Action Research:** A highly democratic, collaborative process aimed at finding solutions to specific community problems.
    *   **Process:** Follows a continuous cycle: **Look $\rightarrow$ Think $\rightarrow$ Act**.
    *   **Key Difference:** Unlike other research types, the **community members/practitioners are integral to the research team** and decision-making, and the goal is immediate change, not just publication.

#### 💡 Key Insights: Comparing Applied Approaches
| Comparison Point | Evaluation Research | Action Research |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Focus** | Assessing the effectiveness of an existing/proposed program. | Solving a specific, community-defined problem collaboratively. |
| **Involvement** | Researcher guides the assessment; client/agency dictates the topic. | Community members are active participants and define the problem. |
| **Outcome Goal** | Making recommendations on continuance/termination. | Direct, actionable change in the community or group. |

## 📝 III. Data Collection Formats
The final data collected is typically assessed through different formats:

*   **Experimental Data:** Measures taken on subjects relative to independent and dependent variables (e.g., pre-test vs. post-test scores).
*   **Survey Data:** Responses gathered when subjects fill out a questionnaire.
*   **Interview Data:** Transcripts or notes from individual or group interviews (unstructured, structured, or semi-structured).

#### 💡 Key Insights: Practical Method Trade-Offs
*   **Focus Groups:** An efficient alternative to one-on-one interviews. They are useful for observing **social dynamics** and group interactions, especially in market research.
    *   *Drawback:* Less control than one-on-one interviews; results are hard to generalize because sample size is small (usually 10-12 people).
*   **Combining Methods:** Due to the limitations and biases of any single method (e.g., the lack of motivation context in observation), the text stresses that combining different methods (e.g., observation + interview) creates the most effective data sources.
