### **1. Fundamental Narrative Perspectives (Point of View)**
The text defines the three primary modes of narration and their impact on the reader:
*   **First Person ("I"):** Provides intimacy and direct access to a character's internal thoughts and emotions. However, it can be limited by the narrator's subjective perspective.
*   **Second Person ("You"):** A less common mode that addresses the reader directly.
*   **Third Person ("He/She/They"):** Can range from **objective** (reporting only actions and speech) to **omniscient** (knowing the internal thoughts of all characters). It provides a sense of detachment or a "god-like" overview.

### **2. Character Construction and Complexity**
The quality of a character is measured by their complexity and the avoidance of "stock" tropes.
*   **The Stock Character:** A character defined by a single trait or stereotype (e./g., the "hero," the "villain," or a "cliché"). These characters lack depth and can become predictable and unengaging.
*   **The Complex Character:** A character possessing internal conflict, contradictory traits, and deep motivations. 
    *   **The Protagonist/Antagonist Dynamic:** The tension between the main character and the opposing force (the antagonist) drives the narrative.
    *   **The Power of Flaws:** Using "negative" traits or historical baggage (as seen in the Coco Chanel example) to create depth.
*   **Character Archetypes vs. Reality:** The text suggests that while characters like the "villain" exist, the most compelling characters are those where the reader can see the human struggle behind the role (e.g., a villain driven by trauma or a hero driven by doubt).

### **3. Narrative Techniques and Literary Devices**
The text outlines several technical tools used to deepen the texture of a story:
*   **Direct vs. Indirect Discourse:**
    *   **Direct Discourse:** Using the character's actual words (quotations). This provides immediacy and allows the reader to hear the character's unique voice.
    *   **Indirect Discourse:** Summarizing or paraphrasing what was said. This allows for faster narrative pacing and a more detached, observant tone.
*   **Metonymy and Synecdoche:**
    *   **Metonymy:** Using an attribute or related concept to represent a whole (e.g., "The Crown" to represent the Monarchy).
    *   **Synecdoche:** Using a part to represent the whole (e.	g., "all hands on deck" where "hands" represents people).
*   **Metaphorical Characterization:** Using objects, clothing, or specific actions to symbolize a character's internal state or social standing without explicitly stating it.

### **4. Structural Elements of Storytelling**
*   **Narrative Voice/Tone:** The "personality" of the narrator, which can be detached and clinical or intimate and emotional.
*   **The Role of Conflict:** Conflict is the engine of the narrative. It can be **internal** (character vs. self) or **external** (character vs. society, nature, or another character).
*   **Historical/Biographical Application:** The text uses the life of **Coco Chanel** as a case study to demonstrate how real-world complexities—social status, scandal, and personal ambition—can be translated into compelling character studies.
