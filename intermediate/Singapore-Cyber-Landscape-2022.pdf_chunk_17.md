### 1. The "Boon": Opportunities and Advancements
Quantum computing is presented as a transformative technology with the potential to revolutionize scientific and computational capabilities.
*   **Exponential Computing Power:** Unlike classical computers that use binary bits (0 or 1), quantum computers use **qubits**, which can exist in a "superposition" of both states. This allows them to perform multiple calculations simultaneously, solving complex problems (like global climate modeling) in seconds that would take traditional computers years.
*   **AI Amplification:** Quantum computing can significantly enhance AI capabilities by allowing the analysis of trillions of data points. This could lead to:
    *   Real-time insights and predictive capabilities for cyberattacks.
    *   The creation of customized, industry-specific cyber threat scenarios.
*   **Market Explosion:** The quantum computing market is experiencing massive growth. It was valued at **US$412 million in 2020** and is projected to reach **US$8.6 billion by 2027** (per IDC), with McKinsey forecasting it could exceed **US$90 billion by 2040**.

### 2. The "Bane": Cybersecurity Threats
The primary risk identified is the potential for quantum computers to render current encryption standards obsolete.
*   **Threat to RSA Encryption:** Quantum computing poses a direct threat to the **RSA algorithm**, a ubiquitous standard used to protect websites, mobile apps, and communications.
*   **The "Qubit Gap":** While the threat is real, it is currently a medium-to-long-term risk. Breaking RSA requires roughly **one million or more qubits**. Current technology is significantly below this threshold (e.g., IBM’s **433-qubit Osprey** processor).
*   **The Necessity of Proactive Defense:** Because the eventual possibility of broken encryption exists, organizations and nations must proactively deploy **"quantum-safe" (post-quantum) encryption methods** now.
