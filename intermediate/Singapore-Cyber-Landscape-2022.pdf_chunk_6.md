This document segment from the **Singapore Cyber Landscape 2022** report outlines the strategic initiatives undertaken by the Singapore government (specifically the Cyber Security Agency of Singapore - CSA) to strengthen national cybersecurity.

The report is structured around the **Singapore Cybersecurity Strategy 2021**, which focuses on three strategic pillars and two foundational enablers.

### 1. Strategic Framework
The core strategy is built on:
*   **Pillar 1: Build Resilient Infrastructure** (Strengthening digital foundations).
*   **Pillar 2: Enable a Safer Cyberspace** (Creating a healthy digital environment).
*   **Pillar 3: Enhance International Cyber Cooperation** (Promoting global norms).
*   **Foundational Enablers:** Developing a vibrant cybersecurity ecosystem and growing a robust cyber talent pipeline.

---

### 2. Key Insights: Pillar 1 (Build Resilient Infrastructure)
This pillar focuses on legislative updates, supply chain security, and proactive threat management.

**A. Legislative & Regulatory Updates (2022)**
*   **Cybersecurity (Composition of Offences) Regulations 2022:** Gazetted in April 2022, these regulations allow authorities to take measures to manage threats and introduce "compoundable offences," where fines can be paid in lieu of prosecution to optimize court resources.
*   **Cybersecurity Code of Practice (CCoP) Second Edition:** Issued in July 2022, this update focuses on a threat-based approach, agility in responding to emerging risks (such as 5G technology), and better coordination between the public and private sectors.

**B. Critical Information Infrastructure (CII) Supply Chain Programme**
*   **Purpose:** A national effort to manage risks introduced by third-party vendors and the increasing complexity of digital supply chains.
*   **Components:** The programme includes a risk assessment toolkit, a vendor certification programme, a learning hub, and a platform for international cooperation.
*   **Approach:** It moves beyond regulation, aiming to help CII owners identify critical vendors and manage risks holistically.

**C. Proactive Threat Management & Exercises**
*   **National Cyber Threat Monitoring Centre (NCTMC):** Issued **102 advisories** in 2022. A notable highlight was the warning regarding the **"Pipedream" malware toolkit**, which targets industrial control systems.
*   **Sectoral Exercises:** Five CII sectors conducted cyber exercises in 2022 to validate readiness.
*   **Exercise Cyber Star 2021 (XCS21):** A large-scale exercise (Nov 2021–Jan 2022) that simulated ransomware and OT (Operational Technology) system compromises to improve national incident management.

---

### 3. Key Insights: Pillar 2 (Enable a Safer Cyberspace)
This pillar focuses on public awareness, education, and community resilience.

**A. Youth and Student Engagement**
*   **SG Cyber Safe Students Programme:** Designed to educate students on online dangers.
*   **High-Engagement Content:** The "Better Cyber Safe Than Sorry" music video achieved significant social media reach (e.g., 44,000+ views on Instagram).
*   **Community Building:** A school dance challenge involving over 300 students helped promote cyber hygiene through pop culture.

**B. Protecting Vulnerable Demographics**
*   **Intergenerational Support:** Programs paired students with seniors to provide one-on-one tutorials on using digital apps safely.
*   **The Go Safe Online Pop-up:** In partnership with the Singapore Police Force, this initiative provides updated information on current scam trends and digital safety.
