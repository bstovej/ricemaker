### 1. The Art of the Objection (Evidentiary Rules)
This section focuses on the procedural mechanics of trial law under the Federal Rules of Evidence (FRE).

*   **Preserving the Right to Appeal (Rule 103):** A timely objection is crucial because it preserves the right to raise the issue on appeal. Once an objection is made and overruled, the lawyer does not need to object every single time that specific evidence is mentioned.
*   /  **Relevance (Rule 401 & 403):** 
    *   Evidence must be **relevant** (tending to make a fact more or less probable) to be admissible.
    *   Even relevant evidence can be excluded under **Rule 403** if its "probative value" is substantially outweighed by the danger of prejudice, confusion, or wasting time.
*   **Character Evidence (Rule 404b & 608):** 
    *   Generally, a defendant’s "prior bad acts" cannot be used solely to show they have a bad character (propensity). 
    *   However, they **can** be admitted to prove other things, such as motive, plan, or special knowledge.
    *   **Rule 608** allows attacking a witness's credibility via their reputation for truthfulness, but external evidence is only permitted for criminal convictions.
*   **Hearsay (Rules 801 & 803):** 
    *   **Definition:** An out-of-court statement offered to prove the truth of the matter asserted.
    *   **Exemptions:** Statements by an opposing party or statements offered for a purpose *other* than proving truth (e.g., to show the speaker's state of mind).
    *   **Exceptions:** Statements made for medical treatment, "present sense impressions" (spontaneous reactions), and "excited utterances" are often admissible because they are seen as more reliable.
*   **Exhibits and Authentication:** Documents must be **authenticated** (proven to be what they claim to be) before being admitted. The "Best Evidence Rule" is implied regarding the need for proper documentation.

### 2. Problematic Evidence and Risks
The text identifies significant risks regarding the reliability of certain types of testimony:

*   **False Confessions:** The text notes that vulnerable populations (the mentally ill or juveniles) are at higher risk of false confessions. It also highlights that police tactics (like withholding information) can lead to innocent people confessing.
*   **Eyewitness Reliability:** Eyewitness testimony is subject to error due to environmental factors, stress, and "suggestive" police procedures (like lineup compositions).
*   **Forensic Science Risks:** While DNA is highly reliable, other forensic methods (like bite mark analysis or certain hair comparisons) can be scientifically unreliable and lead to wrongful convictions.

### 3. Controlling Cross-Examination
The final section provides a tactical guide for lawyers during cross-examination:

*   **Scope of Examination:** A lawyer’s ability to cross-examine is generally limited to the scope of the direct examination (the topics already covered by the opposing counsel).
*   **Control the Witness:** The goal of cross-examination is to use "leading questions" to control the witness and elicit specific facts, rather than allowing the witness to explain or provide narrative.
*   **The "Impeachment" Goal:** A primary goal of cross-examination is to "impeach" the witness—undermining their credibility by highlighting biases, prior inconsistent statements, or lack of personal knowledge.
