This document segment provides a detailed legal analysis of the **EU AI Act**, specifically focusing on the regulation, oversight, and implementation of **Remote Biometric Identification (RBI)** systems and the responsibilities of Member States and law enforcement.

Below are the key insights and facts categorized by theme.

### 1. Human Oversight and AI Literacy
*   **Mandatory Verification:** Under Article 14(5), any identification resulting from an AI system cannot be acted upon by a deployer unless it has been **separately verified and confirmed by at least two natural persons** possessing the necessary competence, training, and authority (unless national/Union law deems this disproportionate).
*   **Mitigating "Automation Bias":** For oversight to be effective, the law emphasizes the need for an organizational structure that prevents "automation bias"—the uncritical acceptance of AI results due to time pressure, burdensome procedures, or fear of career repercussions.

*   **AI Literacy:** Article 4 requires both providers and users to ensure a "sufficient level of AI literacy" among staff and individuals involved in the operation of AI systems.

### 2. Regulation of "Real-Time" RBI in Law Enforcement
*   **Notification Requirement:** Each use of a "real-time" RBI system in publicly accessible spaces for law enforcement must be **notified to the relevant market surveillance authority and the national data protection authority**.
*   **Sensitive Data Protection:** While notifications are required after each use, they **must not include "sensitive operational data"**—information that could jeopardize the integrity of criminal proceedings (e.g., specific locations or active investigative details).
*   **National Discretion:** Member States have the choice to adopt national laws that allow for the full or partial authorization of real-time RBI, provided they stay within the strict limits and conditions set by the AI Act.

### 3. Requirements for National Legislation
*   **Legal Basis:** National laws are necessary to provide the legal basis for authorizing the use of real-time RBI. 
*   **Strict Boundaries:** National laws **cannot expand the objectives** for which real-time RBI can be used beyond those specifically listed in Article 5(1)(h)(i)-(iii) of the AI Act. However, Member States **can** introduce more restrictive laws.
*   **Mandatory Content for National Law:** If a Member State chooses to authorize these systems, its national law must specify detailed rules regarding:
    *   Competent authorities for requesting and issuing authorizations.
    *   The specific scope and objectives of use.
    *   Requirements for written requests and evidence of "strict necessity and proportionality."
    *   Technical standards (accuracy, reliability, data retention, and security).
    *   Procedures for appeals, sanctions, and the rights of the individuals being searched.
*   **Notification to the Commission:** Member States must notify the European Commission of these national laws within **30 days of adoption**. Failure to notify may render the national law unenforceable in legal proceedings.

### 4. Scope and Classification of AI Systems
*   **Prohibited vs. High-Risk:**
    *   **Prohibited:** Real-time RBI in public spaces for unauthorized law enforcement purposes (e.g., indiscriminate identification or using emotion AI to trigger identification).
    *   **High-Risk:** All other uses of RBI that do not fall under the prohibition (e.g., biometric authentication, retrospective use of RBI, or use by private actors like supermarkets) are classified as **high-risk AI systems**.
*   **Retrospective RBI:** The use of RBI to analyze past footage (e.g., comparing images against a criminal database) is **not prohibited** by the AI Act, but it is subject to additional conditions and safeguards as high-risk AI.
*   **Private Sector Constraints:** While the AI Act may not prohibit private use of RBI (e.g., supermarkets using it to prevent shoplifting), such uses must still comply with **GDPR**. The document cites precedents where such uses were found illegal because they were deemed neither necessary nor proportionate.

### 5. Reporting and Transparency
*   **Annual Reporting (Authorities):** National market surveillance and data protection authorities must submit annual reports on the use of real-time RBI to the Commission using a standard template.
*   **Annual Reporting (Commission):** The Commission is required to publish annual reports based on aggregated, non-sensitive data regarding the use of these systems across the EU.
