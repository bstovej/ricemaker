### **II  Strategies for Supporting an Argument**
A strong argument requires more than just facts; it requires the logical connection of those facts to your thesis.

*   **The Role of Evidence:** Evidence alone is insufficient; the writer must actively interpret the evidence to show *how* it supports the claim.
*   **The Danger of "Unexplained" Evidence:** Simply presenting a fact or a quote without explaining its significance leaves the reader to draw their own conclusions, which may not align with the author's intent.
*   **The Power of Concession (Counter-arguments):**
    *   Addressing opposing views is a way to build credibility.
    *   **Concession vs. Refutation:** A writer can acknowledge the validity of an opposing point (concession) before explaining why their own position remains stronger (refutation).
