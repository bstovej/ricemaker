This document is an information paper from December 2024, detailing observations from a thematic review conducted by the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) regarding **Artificial Intelligence (AI) Model Risk Management (MRM)** in the banking sector.

The following are the key insights and facts categorized by theme:

### 1. The AI Landscape in Banking
*   **Current Use Cases:** Banks are already heavily utilizing traditional AI (e.g., decision tree-based models like XGBoost) for financial risk management, anti-money laundering (AML), fraud detection, and customer engagement.
*   **Generative AI (GenAI) Maturity:** The use of GenAI in banks is currently in an **early stage**. The primary focus is on **"human augmentation"** (using AI to assist staff with productivity, coding, and summarization) rather than direct, customer-facing applications.
*   **Categorization of Risks:**
    *   **Financial:** Inaccurate models leading to poor risk assessments and losses.
    *   **Operational:** Unexpected AI behavior causing disruptions in automated processes.
    *   **Regulatory:** Failure of AI (like AML systems) leading to non-compliance.
    *   **Reputational:** Inappropriate chatbot responses causing customer complaints.
    *   **GenAI-Specific Risks:** Hallucinations, unpredictable behaviors, lack of explainability, and data bias.

### 2. Best Practices in Governance and Oversight
The review identified several "good practices" implemented by leading banks to manage AI risks:
*   **Cross-Functional Oversight:** Establishing forums that include representatives from Data/Analytics, Risk Management, Legal, Compliance, Technology, and Audit to prevent gaps in oversight.
*   **Policy Centralization:** Creating central guides and updated policies that specifically address AI-specific challenges (e.g., dynamically updated models).
*   **Ethical Frameworks:** Implementing clear principles governing the **Fair, Ethical, Accountable, and Transparent (FEAT)** use of AI.
*   **Capability Building:** Investing in AI "Centres of Excellence" and upskilling both general staff and senior executives to manage both innovation and risk.

### 3. Key Risk Management Systems and Processes
Effective AI MRM relies on three pillars:
*   **Identification:** Using existing Model Risk Management (MRM) frameworks as a foundation but adapting them to account for the unique uncertainties of AI. Banks are using tools and portals to classify AI consistently.
*   **Inventory Management:** Maintaining formal **AI inventories** is critical. This ensures AI is only used within its "approved scope" (specific purpose, jurisdiction, and use case) and prevents unauthorized, high-risk usage.
*   **Risk Materiality Assessment:** Assessing AI based on its impact, complexity, and the bank's level of reliance on the model to ensure that controls are applied proportionately.

### 4. Development and Deployment Standards
To ensure responsible deployment, the paper highlights the need for:
*   **Rigorous Standards:** Establishing processes for data management, robustness, stability, explainability, fairness, and auditability.
*   **Independent Validation:** Conducting independent or peer reviews of AI models before they are deployed, particularly those identified as high-risk.
*   **Continuous Monitoring:** Implementing pre-deployment checks and ongoing monitoring to ensure the AI continues to behave as intended after it is live.

### 5. Regulatory Context (MAS)
The paper situates these observations within the broader framework of MAS’s efforts to promote **Responsible AI**, including:
*   **FEAT Principles:** A framework for Fairness, Ethics, Accountability, and Transparency.
*   **Veritas Initiative:** A project to help financial institutions implement FEAT principles.
*   **Project MindForge:** A specific initiative focused on examining the risks and opportunities of Generative AI.
