This document is a course guidebook for a lecture series titled **"Writing Creative Nonfiction,"** produced by **The Great Courses** and taught by **Professor Tilar J. Mazzeo**.

The following are the key facts and educational insights extracted from the text:

### **Key Facts**
*   **Instructor Profile:** Tilar J. Mazzeo, Ph.D., is the Clara C. Piper Professor of English at Colby College. She is a *New York Times* bestselling author (notably of *The Widow Clicquot* and *The Secret of Chanel No. 5*) and an award-winning writer specializing in food, wine, and travel.
*   **Course Scope:** The course consists of 24 lectures designed to teach the art of using storytelling techniques to convey true stories. It covers topics ranging from finding inspiration and character development to the technicalities of publishing.
*   **Publication Details:** The material is part of "The Great Courses" series, published by The Teaching Company.
*   **Core Subject Matter:** The text focuses on the intersection of factual reporting and narrative artistry—specifically, how to use "fiction" tools (character, plot, setting, tension) to tell "non-fiction" truths.

### **Key Educational Insights**

#### **1. The Definition of Creative Nonfiction**
The course defines the genre as the application of literary techniques—such as dialogue, setting, and narrative arc—to real-life events. The goal is to use the "how" of fiction to enhance the "what" of non-fiction.

#### **_2. The "Non-Fiction Contract" (Ethics and Accuracy)_**
A central theme is the ethical boundary of the genre. 
*   **The Contract:** The writer has an obligation to the truth. While they can use descriptive language and dramatic pacing, they cannot invent facts.
*   **The Non-Negotiable:** Unlike fiction, where the author creates the reality, in creative nonfiction, the author must adhere to the "truth" of the events, even while interpreting them through a narrative lens.

#### **3. The Power of Perspective (Point of View)**
The text emphasizes that **perspective** is the primary tool for a non-fiction writer. 
*   The same set of facts can be transformed into different stories simply by changing the lens (e.g., focusing on the internal emotions of a subject vs. the external environmental details).
*   The author uses the concept of "the lens" to show how a writer can create atmosphere, tension, or intimacy without altering the underlying facts.

#### **4. Narrative Elements in Non-Fiction**
The lectures outline several "fiction" elements that are essential to good non-fiction:
*   **Characterization:** Treating real people as "characters" with motivations, flaws, and arcs.
*   **Setting:** Using sensory details (sight, sound, smell) to immerse the reader in a real location.
*   **Conflict/Tension:** Identifying the natural struggles or "stakes" within a true story to drive the narrative forward.
*   **Narrative Arc:** Structuring a true event with a beginning, middle, and end to create a sense of progression.

#### **5. The Role of "The Lens" and Emotion**
The text suggests that a skilled writer uses "sensory' writing" to bridge the gap between a dry report and a compelling story. By focusing on small, evocative details (the way a room smells, the tension in a person's hands), the writer allows the reader to experience the truth emotionally rather than just intellectually.
