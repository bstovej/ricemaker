Based on the provided document segment from the **Singapore Cyber Landscape 2022** report, here are the key insights and facts categorized by the specific case studies and industry trends presented.

### **1. Case Study: Ransomware Attack (Engineering Company)**
*   **The Incident:** An engineering company experienced a ransomware attack where files were encrypted and renamed to "gibberish," accompanied by a ransom note.
*   **The Demand:** The attackers demanded **50 Bitcoins** (approximately **S$3.5 million** at the time).
*   **The Response:** The company **refused to pay** the ransom. Despite the refusal, they managed to resume business operations within two days.
*   **The Impact:** 
    *   **Financial:** Significant losses amounting to hundreds of thousands of dollars.
    *   **Operational:** Threat to intellectual property (blueprints and engineering projects).
*   **Key Lessons:** 
    *   Cybersecurity must be treated as a **board-level risk** rather than just an IT issue.
       *   The company increased investment in regular penetration testing and drills.
        *   The importance of cross-functional coordination (IT, Legal, and Public Communications).

### **2. Case Study: Business Email Compromise (Law Firm)**
*   **The Incident:** A Singapore-based law firm was compromised when attackers hacked into their mail server to send **2,000 phishing emails** to clients.
*   **The Method:** Attackers used the firm's legitimate email address to trick clients into providing banking credentials under the guise of "providing compensation."
*   **The Impact:** 
    *   **Financial:** Clients suffered unauthorized bank deductions totaling more than **S$100,000**.
    *   **Reputational:** The firm lost several major clients who lost faith in their ability to protect data.
*   **Key Lessons:** 
    *   Cybersecurity is a "duty of care" to clients.
    *   **Remediation:** The firm implemented robust email security, multi-factor authentication (MFA), and is working toward **ISO 27001** and **CSA’s Cyber Trust** certification.

### **3. Case Study: Exploited Unpatched Vulnerabilities (Tuition Centre)**
*   **The Incident:** A small tuition centre had its computer systems exploited, leading to the exposure of student academic records and personal information.
*   **The Root Cause:** The centre lacked dedicated IT staff and operated on a "we don't fix what isn't broken" mentality, only updating software when prompted by the system.
*   **The Discovery:** The breach was discovered when a parent reported being able to access other students' private data via the centre's web portal.
*   **Key Lesson:** Small businesses are highly vulnerable when they lack proactive maintenance and view cybersecurity as a secondary concern to budget constraints.

### **4. Key Industry Statistics & Trends**
*   **Rising Costs:** The average cost of cybercrime for a company rose to **US$13 million** (an 11% year-on-year increase from US$11.7 million in 2019).
*   **BEC Trends in Singapore:** Between January and March 2022, **93 victims** in Singapore lost **S$56.2 million** to Business Email Compromise (BEC) scams.
*   **Vulnerability Gaps:** 
    *   **62%** of organizations were vulnerable to a breach *before* the actual data breach occurred.
    *   **60%** of breach victims identified that they were breached due to an **unpatched and known vulnerability**.

### **5. Role of CSA and SingCERT**
*   **SingCERT’s Function:** Focuses on the detection, resolution, and prevention of cybersecurity incidents in Singapore. 
*   **Capabilities:** They provide alerts, advisories, and incident handling guidelines. They also work with international CERTs to foster information sharing.
*   **Incident Response:** Professionals like Wayne Lim (CSA) perform digital forensics, reconstructing sequences of events and identifying malware to provide remediation recommendations to stakeholders.
