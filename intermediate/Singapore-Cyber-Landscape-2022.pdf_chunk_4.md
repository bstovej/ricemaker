### **2. Singapore Cyber Landscape: Key Statistics (2022)**
The report identifies a "dual-natured" landscape for Singapore: while certain threats like phishing are surging, other areas like infected infrastructure and website defacements are improving due to better cyber hygiene.

#### **A. Phishing (The Primary Threat)**
*   **Massive Increase:** There were **8,500 reported phishing attempts** in 2022, a **174% increase** from 3,100 cases in 2021.
*   **Top Target Sector:** **Banking and Financial Services** accounted for **more than 80%** of all phishing attempts on organizations.
*   **The "China Connection":** A spike in phishing was linked to the June 2020/21 China rural bank scandal, with attackers using the "trust" in Chinese banks to lure victims.
*   **Second & Third Targets:** Government organizations (e.g., LTA, Singpass, IRAS) and Logistics (e.g., SingPost) were the next most targeted.
*   **Attack Vector:** Over **50% of phishing attempts** utilized **".xyz"** domains (which are cheap and easy to register) via links in emails or SMS.

#### **B. Ransomware**
*   **Stable but High:** There were **132 cases** in 2022, a slight **4% decrease** from 137 cases in 2021.
*   **Contrast to Global Trends:** While Singapore saw a slight drop, the global trend showed a **13% increase** in ransomware incidents.
*   **Primary Targets:** Small and Medium Enterprises (**SMEs**) in the **Manufacturing and Retail** sectors.

#### **C. Infected Infrastructure & Website Defacements**
*   **Infected Infrastructure:** Decreased by **13%** (from 94,000 systems in 2021 to **81,500** in 2022). Singapore's global share of infected infrastructure also dropped significantly from 0.84% to 0.34%.
*   **Website Defacements:** Decreased by **19%** (from 419 in 2021 to **340** in 2022). This is attributed to hacktivists moving their activities to social media platforms with wider reach.

### **3. Summary of Key Insights**
*   **Improving Cyber Hygiene:** The decrease in infected infrastructure and website defacements suggests that local cybersecurity posture and hygiene in Singapore are improving.
*   **Expanding Attack Surface:** Despite improvements, the "attack surface" remains high because the number of connected devices per household in Singapore continues to grow (from 6.5 in 2020 to 7.0 in 2021).
*   **The "Spray and Pray" Tactic:** Phishing attackers are increasingly using low-cost tools (like .xyz domains) and broad-spectrum tactics to exploit anxieties surrounding banking and government services.
