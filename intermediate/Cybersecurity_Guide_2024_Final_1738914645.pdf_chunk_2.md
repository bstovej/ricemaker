This document, part of the **2023/2024 Cybersecurity Guide for CFOs**, outlines the escalating threat landscape created by Generative AI and provides a strategic framework for finance leaders to mitigate these new risks.

Below are the key insights and facts extracted from the text.

### **Key Insights**

#### **1. The Shift in the Threat Landscape**
*   **The "Double Exponential Curve":** AI advancements are occurring at an accelerating rate because AI tools can self-strengthen, making the speed of new threat development difficult to predict.
*   **The "Unknown" Risk:** Beyond known threats (like deepfakes), there is a fundamental risk in "the unknown"—the possibility that AI models may develop unanticipated skills or capabilities that creators cannot currently foresee.
*   **Augmentation, Not Just Innovation:** While AI introduces new tools (synthetic video), it is primarily being used to augment and scale existing, well-known attacks like Business Email Compromise (BEC) and social engineering.

#### **2. Psychological Vulnerabilities (The Human Element)**
The document highlights that even "unnatural" deepfakes can succeed because the human brain is evolutionarily wired to trust rich sensory information. Attackers exploit four specific cognitive biases:
*   **Limited Awareness:** A lack of understanding regarding how realistic AI-generated content can be.
*   **Familiarity Heuristics:** The natural instinct to trust a voice or face that looks or sounds like a known colleague.
*   **Confirmation Bias:** An inclination to believe information that aligns with what the employee already believes to be true.
*   **Authority Bias:** The tendency to follow instructions from a perceived superior (e.g., an impersonated CEO) without questioning their legitimacy.

#### **3. Strategic Recommendations for CFOs**
The document moves from identifying risks to proposing a multi-layered defense strategy:
*   **Unified Strategy:** Finance leaders must break silos and develop a cybercrime strategy in direct alignment with IT and Security teams.
*   **Pressure-Testing Controls:** Organizations should not just implement controls but actively "penetration test" them using simulated AI-driven attacks (e.g., fake invoices with modified bank details or spoofed executive identities).
*   **Incident Response Playbook:** A specialized six-phase playbook for "synthetic media incidents" is recommended (Preparation $\rightarrow$ Assessment $\rightarrow$ Detection $\rightarrow$ Containment $\rightarrow$ Recovery $\rightarrow$ Post-Incident).
*   **Protecting "Crown Jewels":** Security efforts should focus heavily on the authentication and approval processes surrounding an organization's most valuable assets (primarily cash).

### **Key Facts and Statistics**

#### **Control Procedure Failures (Eftsure Survey Data)**
*   **Low Adoption of Verification:** Less than **50%** of organizations use verbal verifications to prevent voice conversion scams.
*   **Incomplete Best Practices:** Among the 46% of organizations that *do* use verbal verification:
    *   Only **41%** use independently sourced phone numbers.
    *   Only **38%** initiate calls via outbound lines.
    *   Only **37%** use open-ended questions to identify synthetic media.
*   **Siloed Training:** Less than **50%** of organizations employ training modules developed in collaboration with their IT or security teams.

#### **The "Three-Fold Risk" for CFOs**
The document defines the current crisis as a combination of three factors:
1.  Organizations are unprepared for even **basic** cyber attacks.
2.  Generative AI is increasing the **efficiency, reach, and scale** of existing attacks.
3.  Current anti-fraud measures were **not designed** to counter synthetic media.

#### **Technical/Operational Facts**
*   **Data Vulnerability:** The widespread use of recorded video calls provides a massive, accessible pool of biometric data for threat actors to use in creating deepfakes.
*   **Attack Vectors:** Threat actors specifically target "vulnerable" human moments, such as periods of high cognitive load, skeleton staffing, or when employees are about to go on leave.
