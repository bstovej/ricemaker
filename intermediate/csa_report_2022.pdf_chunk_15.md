Based on the provided document segment from the **Singapore Cyber Landscape 2022**, here are the key insights and facts organized by theme:

### 1. Evolving Cyber Threat Trends
* **Double Extortion Ransomware:** A growing trend where attackers do not just encrypt data but also exfiltrate (steal) it. This puts extreme pressure on victims to pay, as even having backups will not prevent the threat of a data leak.
* **Exploitation of Unpatched Vulnerabilities:** Threat actors are actively targeting unpatched vulnerabilities, specifically noting a trend in targeting **Microsoft Exchange Servers** to deploy backdoors and malware.
* **Sector-Specific Motivations:** 
    * **Private Sector:** Primarily targeted by financially motivated and opportunistic attacks, such as ransomware, data breaches, and crypto-mining.
    * **Public Sector:** Primarily faces threats from cyber espionage and politically or financially motivated attacks.

### 2. The Economics of Cybersecurity
* **The Cost of Neglect:** The document highlights a significant disparity between prevention and remediation. One case study noted that **remediation costs were nearly triple the amount** it would have cost to implement preventive measures.
* **Prevention vs. Recovery:** Effective prevention (like timely patching) can prevent "costly remediation efforts." While total prevention is impossible, addressing "opportunistic attacks" through good cyber hygiene is highly effective.

### 3. Strategic Best Practices
**For Organizations (especially SMEs):**
* **Core Defense Pillars:** 
    1. **Multi-factor Authentication (MFA):** Essential to prevent access via stolen or leaked credentials.
    2. **Patch Management:** Establishing a regime to address known vulnerabilities.
    3. **Regular Backups:** Critical for recovering from ransomware.
* **Resource Management:** SMEs that lack in-house expertise should leverage managed security service providers, freelance CISOs, or government resources like the **CSA (Cyber Security Agency of Singapore)** SME toolkits and grant-supported health plans.
* **Proactive Posture:** Organizations should adopt a multi-layered approach: **Prevent** (anti-virus, limiting privileges), **Detect** (monitoring, alerts), and **Respond** (incident response plans and regular exercises).

**For Personal/Home Security:**
* **Hardware Standards:** The use of products with the **Cybersecurity Labeling Scheme (CLS)**—specifically Level 4—provides assurance of penetration testing and mandatory security updates.
* **Basic Hygiene:** Use strong/unique passwords, enable automatic updates, and disable remote management on routers unless strictly necessary.

### 4. Societal and Policy Challenges
* **The "Risk Shift" Problem:** There is a growing concern that companies are using "Terms and Conditions" to shift the legal and financial burden of cyberattacks onto consumers (e.g., claiming a customer is responsible for being scammed via phishing).
* **From "Personal" to "Collective" Efficacy:** 
    * **Personal Efficacy** (individual responsibility) is insufficient against sophisticated scams.
    * **Collective Efficacy** is proposed as a solution, where society (families, community groups, or digital hubs) works together to protect vulnerable members (such as the elderly) from navigating complex digital threats.
* **Future Frontiers:** The landscape is facing new complexities due to the Russia-Ukraine conflict, the rise of **Artificial Intelligence (AI)**, the threat of **Quantum Computing**, and the potential for "Ransom for Reputation" (R4R) schemes.
