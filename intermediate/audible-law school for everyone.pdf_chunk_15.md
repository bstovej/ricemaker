### 1. Complex Litigation (Lecture 6)
This section outlines how parties can expand the scope of a lawsuit through various types of claims.

**Key Facts:**
*   **Counterclaims:** Claims brought by a defendant against an opposing party (the plaintiff). In many jurisdictions, these *must* be asserted during the original suit or the right to them is forfeited.
*   **Cross-claims:** Claims brought between co-parties (e.g., Defendant A vs. Defendant B). These do not necessarily have to be related to the original claim.
*   **Impleaders (Third-Party Claims):** A claim brought against someone who was not originally part of the suit. This is only permitted if the impleaded party is liable to indemnify or reimburse the defendant for the judgment.
*   **Class Actions:** A lawsuit brought by a representative plaintiff on behalf of a group of similarly situated people. 
*   **Legal Standard (*Hansberry v. Lee*):** A class action can only bind absent class members if they are **adequately represented** (i.e., the interests of the representative and the class members are aligned).

**Key Insights:**
*   **Efficiency vs. Due Process:** Class actions are an exception to the due process rule that a person shouldn't be bound by a suit they didn't participate in. The exception is justified by the need for judicial efficiency and to prevent inconsistent judgments.
*   **The "Weapon" Critique:** While class actions provide access to justice, they are criticized by some as a tool for "coercive" settlements (implied by the context of litigation).

### 2. The Discovery Process and Information Exchange (Lecture 7)
This section focuses on the tension between the need for transparency and the protection of privacy/privilege.

**Key Facts:**
*   **The Goal:** To allow parties to access information to prepare for trial.
*   **The Limits of Discovery:**
    *   **Privilege:** Certain communications (like attorney-client privilege) are protected from disclosure.
    *   **Relevance/Proportionality:** Information must be relevant and not unduly burdensome.
*   **The Standard for Discovery:** Information is generally discoverable if it is relevant to the claims or defenses in the case.

### 3. Pre-Trial Motions and Summary Judgment (Lecture 8)
This section discusses the mechanisms used to end a case before it reaches a jury.

**Key Facts:**
*   **Summary Judgment:** A motion used to dispose of a case without a trial. 
*   **The Legal Standard:** The moving party must show there is **"no genuine dispute as to any material fact"** and that they are entitled to judgment as a matter of law.
*   **Burden of Proof:** The moving party must demonstrate that even if all the non-moving party's evidence is taken as true, they still cannot win the case.
