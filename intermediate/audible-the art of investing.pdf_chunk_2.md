### 1. Benjamin Graham: The Father of Value Investing
Graham’s approach is rooted in quantitative analysis, margin of safety, and the distinction between investment and speculation.

**Key Philosophies:**
*   **Margin of Safety:** The central pillar of his strategy. Investors should buy assets at a significant discount to their intrinsic value to protect against errors in calculation or unforeseen market events.
*   **Investment vs. Speculation:** An "investment" requires thorough analysis, the promise of safety of principal, and an adequate return. Anything else is "speculative."
*   **The "Company" Rule:** Never buy a share of stock unless you would be willing to buy the entire company itself.
*   **Mean Reversion:** The belief that market prices eventually return to equilibrium, meaning past winners often become losers and vice versa.
*   **Quantitative Focus:** He prioritized examining the balance sheet (assets, liabilities, and net worth), focusing on existing assets and current earnings over speculative future profits.

**Key Strategies:**
*   **Net–Net Strategy:** A "deep-value" approach where he sought companies trading for less than their cash-liquidation value (Cash + conservative receivables/inventory minus all liabilities). This strategy reportedly yielded ~20% annual returns.
*   **7 Criteria for Defensive Investors:**
    1.  **Adequate Size:** Large enough to weather storms (>$600M today).
    2.  **Strong Financial Condition:** Current assets at least 2x current liabilities.
    3.  **Earnings Stability:** Positive earnings for at least 10 consecutive years.
    4.  **Dividend Record:** A long history of consistent payments.
    5.  **Low Debt:** Manageable debt-to-equity ratios.
    6   **Earnings Growth:** Sustained long-term growth.
    7.  **Dividend per Share:** Not an unsustainable payout.

### 2. Warren Buffett: The Evolution of Moats
The text highlights how Buffett evolved from Graham’s "cigar butt" style (buying cheap, low-quality companies) to seeking high-quality companies with "moats."

*   **The "Economic Moat":** Buffett shifted focus toward companies with a competitive advantage that protects them from competitors.
    *   **Intangible Assets:** Brand power, patents, and reputation.
    *   **Cost Advantages:** The ability to produce goods/services at a lower cost than rivals.
    *   **Network Effects/Switching Costs:** Making it difficult or expensive for customers to leave (e.g., brand loyalty or high integration).
*   **Key Concepts:**
    *   **Float/Float Management:** Using the "float" (capital held for a period before being paid out) to fund investments.
    *   **Margin of Safety:** Continuing the Graham tradition of buying at a discount to intrinsic value.

### 3. Notable Case Studies & Concepts
*   **Northern Pipeline (Graham/Buffett era):** A classic example of finding undervalued assets.
*   **Berkshire Hathaway:** The vehicle used to implement these strategies.
*   **The "Moat" Frameworks:**
    *   **Porter’s Five Forces:** Assessing industry competition, supplier/buyer power, and threats of substitutes.
    *   **Buffett’s Moat:** Focus on high barriers to entry, brand strength, and cost advantages.

### Summary Table of Investment Philosophies

| Feature | Benjamin Graham (Value) | Warren Buffett (Quality/Moat) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Goal** | Find undervalued "cigar butts" | Find high-quality companies with durable advantages |
| **Core Metric** | Net-Net value / Margin of safety | Return on Equity (ROE) / Economic Moat |
| **Risk Mitigation** | Buying significantly below intrinsic value | Investing in businesses with high barriers to entry |
