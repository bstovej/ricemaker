Based on the document provided, here are the key insights and facts regarding the course **"Analysis and Critique: How to Empathize and Write about Anything."**

### **Course Overview**
*   **Core Objective:** The course aims to transform students from "passive-receptive" readers into "active-analytical" readers. The fundamental premise is that developing the ability to critically evaluate literature and language is the essential foundation for producing effective, persuasive, and engaging writing.
*   **Instructional Scope:** The curriculum covers five major literary genres: **Fiction, Essay, Poetry, Drama, and Autobiography.**
*   **The Writing Process:** Beyond literary analysis, the course provides a step-by-step guide to the mechanics of writing, including researching, drafting, editing, and rewriting.

### **Key Educational Insights**
*   **Moving Beyond "Precritical" Responses:** A central teaching point is how to move past initial, subjective reactions (e.g., "I like this" or "I don't understand this") toward deeper, critical engagement. This involves analyzing *why* a text affects a reader in a specific way.
*   **The Link Between Reading and Writing:** The course asserts that "great writers are always great readers." By identifying how masters of prose (like Jane Austen or Herman Melville) use voice, persona, and rhythm, students can learn to implement those same strategies in their own work.
*   **The Power of Rhetoric:** The course emphasizes classical rhetorical concepts—such as **ethos, pathos, deductive reasoning, and invention**—as tools to help writers adapt their message to different audiences and contexts.
*   **The Importance of Rereading:** The document notes that a piece of writing has multiple "lives." The first life is the initial experience of the text; the second life is the period of reflection and re-analysis that follows.

### **Instructor Profile**
*   **Professor Dorsey Armstrong:** An Associate Professor of English and Medieval Literature at Purdue University.
*   **Expertise:** She specializes in Middle English, Arthurian literature, Anglo-Saxon literature, and gender/feminist studies. She is also the editor-in-chief of the academic journal *Arthuriana*.

### **Curriculum Highlights**
*   **Structure:** The course consists of **24 lectures**.
*   **Key Literary Examples Used:**
    *   **Jonathan Swift’s "A Modest Proposal":** Used to demonstrate how tone and satire can provoke extreme reactions.
    *   **Herman Melville’s *Moby Dick*:** Used to teach students how to navigate "disorienting" or difficult prose.
    *   **Bulwer-Lytton Bad Writing Contest:** Used as a negative example to illustrate how poor structure and unnecessary detail can ruin a text.
    *   **Other Authors Mentioned:** Jane Austen, Edgar Allan Poe, James Joyce, Stephen Crane, and Katherine Mansfield.
*   **Technical Skills:** The course includes specific instruction on avoiding common errors in grammar and usage, as well as the ethical use of online research tools.
