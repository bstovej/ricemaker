This document is a procedural framework titled **"AI Impact Assessment (AIIA): The tool for a responsible AI project" (Version 2.0, December 2024)**. It is a standardized methodology developed by a collaboration of Dutch government entities, including the Ministry of Infrastructure and Water Management (I&W), the Human Environment and Transport Inspectorate (ILT), and the RWS Datalab.

Below are the key insights and facts extracted from the document.

### 1. Core Purpose and Objective
*   **Responsible AI by Design:** The primary goal is to ensure AI is deployed responsibly by identifying risks, obstacles in data collection, and algorithmic biases *before* deployment.
*   **Accountability and Transparency:** The tool is designed to increase the quality, reproducibility, and transparency of AI decisions, providing a documented trail of the assessments made when deciding whether to use a system.
*   **Decision Support:** It serves as a "go/no-go" tool to determine if an AI application is proportionate, desirable, and legally compliant.

### 2. The Two-Part Framework
The assessment is divided into two distinct but interconnected sections:
*   **Part A (Assessment - The "Why"):** Focuses on the high-level impact. It evaluates the system's purpose, necessity, fundamental rights, sustainability, and whether the intended solution is the most appropriate (considering alternatives).
*   **Part B (Implementation & Use - The "How"):** Focuses on technical and operational execution. It covers technical robustness (bias, accuracy, explainability), data governance (privacy, quality), risk management (security, prevention), and accountability (transparency, archiving).

### 3. Risk-Based Approach (EU AI Act Alignment)
The tool is strictly aligned with the **EU AI Act** risk categories:
*   **Unacceptable Risk:** If a system is categorized here, the assessment stops, and the system is **not permitted** to be used.
*   **High-Risk:** Requires much stricter requirements and the completion of additional, specific questions (found in the document's Appendix 1).
*   **Low/Minimal Risk:** Standard AIIA questions are sufficient.

### 4. Lifecycle Integration
The AIIA is not a one-time event but should be used across all phases of an AI project:
*   **Quick-Scan:** Used during the initial idea phase to check feasibility.
*   **Project Planning:** It is **mandatory** to complete the AIIA when compiling a project plan.
*   **Development:** Used to monitor how implementation choices (like model type) affect the initial impact assessment.
*   **Production:** It is **mandatory** that a completed AIIA is in place before any system (including pilots) goes into production.

### 5. Key Operational Requirements
*   **Multidisciplinary Approach:** The document emphasizes that the AIIA **cannot** be completed by developers alone. It requires a team including Data Scientists, Legal Experts, Privacy Professionals, CISO, Domain Experts, and Stakeholders.
*   **Proportionality and Subsidiarity:** A central theme is determining if the AI's impact is proportionate to its goal and whether a "less radical" (less complex or non-AI) method could achieve the same result.
*   **Sustainability:** A notable feature of Version 2.0 is the explicit focus on the **ecological footprint**, requiring developers to consider the energy and water consumption of their models.
*   **Fundamental Rights:** The assessment requires checking for potential breaches of the Dutch Constitution and the European Convention on Human Rights.
