### 1. Rhetorical and Sound Devices
The text outlines various tools writers use to create impact, rhythm, and emphasis. These are divided into two main categories:

**Structural Devices (Pattern and Repetition):**
*   **Anaphora/Repetition:** Using words or phrases at the beginning of successive clauses to create rhythm.
*   **Antimetabole:** Reversing the order of words in successive clauses (e.g., "Ask not what your country can do for you; ask what you can do for your country").
*   **Antithesis:** Using contrasting ideas within a parallel grammatical structure to create tension.
*   **Chiasmus/Antimetabole:** The reversal of grammatical structures or concepts to create a "mirror" effect.

**Sound Devices (Auditory Impact):**
*   **Alliteration/Consonance/Assonance:** Using repeated consonant or vowel sounds to create texture and mood.
*   **Purpose:** These tools are used to influence the reader's emotional response and to drive the "music" of the prose.

### 2. The Art of Revision and "Showing vs. Telling"
A significant portion of the text focuses on the distinction between effective and ineffective writing, specifically regarding the "show, don't tell" principle.

**Common Pitfalls in Weak Writing:**
*   **Over-reliance on "Telling":** Using adjectives to describe emotions or states of being rather than allowing the action to reveal them.
*   **Passive Voice/Weak Verbs:** Using "to be" verbs or passive constructions that drain the momentum from a sentence.
*   **Redundancy and Clutter:** Including unnecessary details that do not contribute to the theme or tension.
*   **Lack of Tension:** Writing that fails to establish stakes or conflict, resulting in a "flat" narrative.

**Effective Revision Strategies:**
*   **Identifying the "Core":** Stripping away "filler" to find the essential action or emotion.
*   **Strengthening Verbs:** Replacing weak, descriptive phrases with strong, active verbs.
*   **Enhancing Sensory Detail:** Using specific imagery to ground the reader in the scene.

### 3. Character Development and Narrative Tension
The text suggests that character and plot are not merely "subjects" but are revealed through specific technical choices.

**Revealing Character through Action:**
*   **Character via Conflict:** True character is revealed through how a person handles tension, specifically during moments of crisis or moral choice.
*   **The Power of Detail:** Small, specific details (the way a character moves, a specific item they carry) are more effective at building a persona than broad descriptions.
*   **Showing through Subtext:** Instead of stating a character is "angry," a writer should describe the physical manifestations of that anger (e.g., a tightened jaw, a white-knuckled grip).

**Building Narrative Tension:**
*   **The Stakes:** For a story to be engaging, there must be something at risk (the "stakes").
*   **Internal vs. External Conflict:** Great writing balances external struggles (physical obstacles) with internal struggles (moral or emotional dilemmas).
*   **Pacing:** Using sentence structure to control speed—short, punchy sentences for action/tension; longer, flowing sentences for reflection/description.
