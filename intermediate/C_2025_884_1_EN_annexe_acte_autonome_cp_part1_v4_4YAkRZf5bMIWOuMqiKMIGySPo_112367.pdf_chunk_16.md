This document segment outlines the regulatory framework, limitations, and required safeguards for the use of **real-time Remote Biometric Identification (RBI)** systems by law enforcement under the **EU AI Act**.

The following are the key insights and facts categorized by theme:

### 1. Permitted Criminal Offences and Scope
The use of real-time RBI is strictly limited to a specific list of serious crimes (Annex II).
*   **Included Crimes:** Terrorism, human trafficking, sexual exploitation of children, drug/weapon/nuclear material trafficking, murder, organ trade, kidnapping, hijacking, rape, environmental crime, robbery, and sabotage.
*   **The "4-Year" Rule:** For the use of RBI to be authorized, the specific offence must be punishable by a maximum period of at least **four years** of imprisonment.
*   **Categorization:** The first five offences listed are categorized as "Euro crimes" (Article 83 TFEU), while the remaining are considered law enforcement priorities.
*   **Target Subjects:** The technology can be used to locate/identify **suspects** (persons with serious grounds for belief of involvement) and **perpetrators** (those accused or convicted).

### 2. Operational Limitations (Preventing Mass Surveillance)
The document emphasizes that RBI must **not** be used for indiscriminate or untargeted mass surveillance.
*   **"Confirming Identity" vs. "Identification":** The law requires use to "confirm the identity of a specifically targeted individual." This is a safeguard intended to prevent the monitoring of entire populations.
*   **Prohibited Uses:** Authorities are prohibited from using RBI for general security, crime prevention, or managing overcrowding in public spaces.
*   **Strict Parameters:** Any deployment must be limited in three dimensions:
    *   **Geographic:** Must have a clearly delineated boundary (not an entire city/country).
    *   **Temporal:** Must have a specific duration (not indefinite or vague).
    *   **Personal:** Must target specific individuals or categories of persons.
*   **Proportionality Test:** Authorities must weigh the **seriousness of the harm** that would occur if the system is *not* used against the **consequences for fundamental rights** if the system *is* used. They must also consider if less intrusive alternatives exist.

### 3. Legal and Procedural Safeguards
The deployment of RBI is subject to rigorous oversight:
*   **Authorization:** Use must be authorized by a **judicial authority** or another independent authority.
*   **Registration:** The system must be registered in an **EU database** (though exceptions exist for cases of extreme urgency).
*   **FRIA vs. DPIA:**
    *   **DPIA (Data Protection Impact Assessment):** Focuses specifically on the risks to personal data processing.
    *   **FRIA (Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment):** A broader, mandatory assessment that must be conducted by the **deployer**. It evaluates the impact of the AI system on various human rights beyond just data privacy.
*   **FRIA Requirements:** A FRIA must include a description of the technology, the legal basis, the database sources, the frequency of use, and an assessment of the impact on all affected persons (including bystanders).

### 4. Fundamental Rights at Risk
The document identifies several critical rights that may be impacted by the use of real-time RBI:
*   **Privacy and Data Protection:** Specifically the expectation of anonymity in public spaces.
*   **Civil Liberties:** Freedom of thought, conscience, religion, expression, assembly, and association (due to the "chilling effect" of being monitored).
*   **Legal Protections:** The right to a fair trial, the presumption of innocence, and the right to an effective remedy.
*   **Equality and Dignity:** The risk of **algorithmic bias** (gender, ethnic, or racial) leading to misidentification, and the risk of humans being "reduced to an object" by the system.
*   **Vulnerable Groups:** Special consideration must be given to the rights of children and the elderly.
